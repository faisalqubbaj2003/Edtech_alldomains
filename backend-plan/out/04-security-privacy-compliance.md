### 0.4 The security model on one page

1. **The school is the controller; CAROS is its processor.** Everything CAROS does with a student's data is on the school's documented instruction, inside the UAE, under a data processing agreement whose annexes are this document's §1.4, §7.1 and §9 (§1.3).
2. **Identity is federated for people the school already knows, and bound to verified relationships for people it does not.** Staff and students sign in with the school's Google Workspace and get *no access* until an imported roster or an administrative act gives them a membership. Parents sign in by magic link but *see* only the children a verified SIS link connects them to, after a one-time activation the school controls. Mentors are invited, vetted under the school's volunteer policy, and hold a passkey (§2).
3. **Permissions are rows, enforced by the database.** The tenant policy is restrictive, the role policies are permissive, both are generated from the matrix in §3.6, and a query with no context sees nothing. Application checks sit on top for the verbs the database cannot see (approve, escalate, configure, export) (§3).
4. **Sensitive reads are acts.** Opening a file, a note, an escalation, a document, the audit log itself, or building a prompt writes an audit entry in the same transaction. The log is append-only, hash-chained per school, and anchored weekly to immutable storage (§4).
5. **Escalation is a workflow with a clock, and the email is a doorbell.** The email says that a referral exists and who raised it. Everything else is behind sign-in and step-up. Unacknowledged referrals climb a configured route, and the school's own duty to ADEK within 24 hours is displayed, never assumed to be done (§5).
6. **What leaves the country is pseudonymised, minimised, not retained, and never from the excluded classes.** Only zero-data-retention models with US-pinned inference; no Covered Models; no safeguarding, health, nationality, fairness or audit data ever; a per-school switch that turns the AI features off with nothing else lost (§6).
7. **Retention is a schedule, erasure is a procedure, and both survive backups** because the erasure log is replayed on every restore and raw files sit under a per-school key (§7).
8. **The baseline is boring on purpose:** TLS everywhere, customer-managed keys, managed identities, no secrets in code, logs without names, dependency cooldowns, signed provenance, quarterly restore drills, a pen test before the first real record, and an incident process the school's IT can read (§9).

## 1. The regulatory map

### 1.1 What applies by law

**UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the Protection of Personal Data (the PDPL).** In force since 2 January 2022 ([u.ae, data protection laws](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/data/data-protection-laws), page updated 4 December 2025). The facts that matter on 2026-09-23:

- **The Executive Regulations have still not been issued.** The Chambers *Data Protection & Privacy 2026* UAE chapter (BSA Law and Bizilance, 10 March 2026) states that "The Implementing Regulations, intended to clarify key aspects of the law, have yet to be issued" and that "the Data Office has not yet become fully operational", with the TDRA giving administrative support during a transitional period ([Chambers](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/uae/trends-and-developments)). DLA Piper's handbook (updated 27 January 2025) adds that organisations will have six months after issuance to comply, and that no adequacy decisions and no penalty schedule exist yet ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)). **Consequence:** the obligations below exist in the law, but the breach-notification timeline, the penalties, the adequacy list and the detail of consent for minors are undefined. The plan designs to the strictest plausible reading (the GDPR shape the law follows) and re-checks the Data Office quarterly; that check is a runbook item, not a hope.
- **Scope (Article 2).** Applies to controllers and processors in the UAE regardless of where the data subject is, and to those outside the UAE who process data of subjects inside it. Excluded: government data, personal data for personal use, health and credit data "governed by their own respective legislation", and free zones with their own data protection laws ([Securiti](https://securiti.ai/uae-personal-data-protection-law/); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)). ACS sits on Saadiyat Island in mainland Abu Dhabi, not in ADGM or DIFC, so the federal law applies to it and to CAROS.
- **Definitions (Article 1).** Personal data is any data relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. Sensitive personal data is data revealing "family, racial origin, political or philosophical opinions, religious beliefs, criminal records, biometric data, or ... health" (DLA Piper's rendering). Safeguarding records reveal family circumstances; SEN records are health data; nationality is treated as sensitive by policy in this plan because it sits next to racial origin and because pass 3 needs it only for fairness testing.
- **Lawful basis and consent (Articles 4 and 6).** Processing requires consent except in listed cases: performance of a contract with the data subject, a legal obligation on the controller, protection of the data subject's interests, public interest, legal claims, medical and public-health purposes, archival and statistical purposes, and other cases the Executive Regulations may add. Consent must be "clear, simple, unambiguous, and accessible", in writing or electronic, and withdrawable ([Securiti](https://securiti.ai/uae-personal-data-protection-law/)). §1.5 applies this to minors.
- **Controller and processor (Articles 7 and 8).** The controller must ensure the processor provides "sufficient guarantees to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures"; the processor processes on the controller's instructions ([Securiti](https://securiti.ai/uae-personal-data-protection-law/)).
- **Breach notification (Article 9).** The controller notifies the Data Office and the data subjects "immediately, after having become aware" of a breach that may risk their privacy, confidentiality and security; the processor notifies the controller immediately on becoming aware; the procedures and time limits are left to the Executive Regulations, which do not exist ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE); [Securiti](https://securiti.ai/uae-personal-data-protection-law/)). §9.6 sets a contractual clock that is tighter than any plausible regulation.
- **Data protection officer (Articles 10 and 11).** Mandatory where processing creates high risk through new technologies, involves "systematic and comprehensive assessment of sensitive personal data, including profiling and automated processing", or involves large volumes of sensitive data ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)). The signal engine is a systematic assessment of minors' data that includes profiling and automated processing. **CAROS appoints a DPO** before the first real record (it may be an external appointee; UAE residency is not required per DLA Piper), and the DPA asks the school to name its own.
- **Rights (Articles 13 to 18).** Access (including recipients and automated decisions), portability in machine-readable form, rectification and erasure, restriction, cessation, and objection to automated decisions with legal or serious effects; response within one calendar month, extendable by two ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)). §7.4 designs the subject access procedure.
- **Security and records (Articles 20 and 21).** Measures "according to the best international standards and practices, including encryption, pseudonymization"; a record of processing activities covering categories, purposes, authorised personnel, retention, transfers and security measures ([Securiti](https://securiti.ai/uae-personal-data-protection-law/)). `privacy.table_registry` (pass 1) is the machine-readable half of that record; §1.4 lists the human-readable half.
- **Cross-border transfer (Articles 22 and 23).** Transfers to countries with an adequate level of protection (a list the Data Office has not published) or, otherwise, under standard contractual clauses, express consent, contractual necessity, judicial cooperation or public interest ([Securiti](https://securiti.ai/uae-personal-data-protection-law/); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)). **Every AI call that sends a student's data to a model outside the UAE is a transfer under Article 23**, even when nothing is stored abroad. §6 is built on that reading.
- **Penalties.** None in the law itself; a Cabinet decision is pending. Criminal exposure exists independently under the Cybercrimes law and the Penal Code (below).

**Federal Law No. 3 of 2016 on Child Rights (Wadeema's Law).** Read from the KHDA-hosted English text ([PDF](https://www.khda.gov.ae/CMS/WebParts/TextEditor/Documents/Children_Law_English.pdf)). Article 1: "Child: Every human being born alive and who is under eighteen years old." Article 42(1): "Every person shall notify the child protection specialist or child protection units in case of anything that threatens the child's physical, psychological, moral or mental integrity or health"; 42(2): "The notification shall be mandatory for the educators, physicians, social specialists or others entrusted with the protection, care or education of the child." Article 44: "The identity of the reporter shall only be disclosed with his consent and it shall be forbidden to disclose the identity of all the parties of the incident and the witnesses in the cases of child assault or abuse". Article 60: failure to report under Article 42 is punished "by imprisonment or a fine not less than AED 5,000". **Consequences:** the counselor's and the teacher's reporting duty is personal and criminal; CAROS supports it and never claims to discharge it (§5.1). Article 44 shapes who may see a reporter's identity on an escalation and forbids any export that would reveal it (§5.6).

**ADEK school policies.** These are conditions of a private school's licence in Abu Dhabi; the ones that bind what a vendor must do were read from the published PDFs (all "Effective From AY 2024/25 (Fall term), Compliance From AY 2025/26 (Fall term)").

- *Student Protection Policy* ([ADEK, hosted copy](https://www.gemsaa-abudhabi.com/en/-/media/project/gems/gaa_gems_american_academy_abu_dhabi/_files-and-documents/adek_s_student-protection-policy.pdf)). Definitions: "Child: Any resident of the UAE under 18 years of age"; the **Child Protection Coordinator (CPC)** is "an appointed staff member at the educational institution who is professionally trained ... the key contact for staff and authorities where a concern is identified ... the point of contact for the institution to respond to emergencies, report to the CPU and other authorities involved"; a **Child Protection Team (CPT)** of "3-5 members consisting of the counselor and/or social worker as well as relevant members of the senior leadership team" (2.4.2); a delegate "should always be nominated in case the CPC is unavailable" (2.4.2). Staff "are mandated by Federal Law No. (3) of 2016 ... to report all cases of alleged and/or suspected maltreatment ... directly to the ADEK Child Protection Unit (CPU) within 24 hours upon suspicion" (2.1); concerns disclosed in the institution "should be notified to the CPC" (2.2); staff report to the CPC "immediately" (2.5); if the CPC cannot be reached or it is not in the student's best interest, staff report directly to the ADEK CPU or the Family Care Authority through the Safety Concern Portal (2.6). Data confidentiality: "Case reports and student data are strictly confidential" (3.1); shared "only with authorized individuals from the ADEK CPU and the FCA, within investigative teams, and the Abu Dhabi Judicial Department" (3.2); no discussion "with the media, any third parties or other staff" (3.3). Principals must "Maintain students' records in compliance with Policy 35 (Records) and ensure confidentiality of open and closed cases" (Roles, 12) and "Ensure the vetting, hiring, and monitoring of all staff, volunteers, and invited visitors" (15). A **volunteer** is "an individual engaged by a educational institution on a non-remunerated basis to interact with students". **Consequences:** ACS's "Lead Child Protection Officer" is ADEK's CPC; CAROS's escalation is the 2.2/2.5 step ("notify the CPC"), and the 24-hour clock to ADEK belongs to the CPC, so CAROS shows it and records the outcome but never reports to ADEK itself (§5). Alumni mentors are volunteers under this policy, so the school must vet them (§2.3). Confidentiality clause 3.2 is the legal reason the escalation record has the narrowest visibility of any class in the matrix.
- *Records Policy* v1.1, September 2024 ([ADEK](https://www.adek.gov.ae/-/media/Project/TAMM/ADEK/Policies/School-Policies/Governance-and-Operations/ADEK_S_Records-Policy_EN.pdf)). Schools are "obliged to comply with laws in the UAE regarding data protection"; a school data protection plan must include "mechanisms for classifying documents for authorized access, use, and disposal based on defined data sensitivity standards" and "mechanisms for identifying, reporting, and handling security breaches" (1.3.1); storage "accessible only to authorized staff" (1.4.1); disposal with "proof of evidence of destruction" (1.4.2.b); **"Schools shall obtain the parent's permission before transferring student information to any third party" (1.5.1)**; student records (enrolment, academic achievements, daily attendance, disciplinary) kept "for at least 5 years after the student leaves the school, preferably in electronic archives" (2.1.3); graduation records "indefinitely" (2.1.3.a); child protection cases are a required record category; parents may access their child's records on request (2.1.2.b). **Consequences:** CAROS is a third party, so the school needs documented parental permission, or another lawful basis it is prepared to defend, before a student's data is imported (§1.5, question 109). The five-year rule binds the *school's* record, which lives in Veracross; CAROS should not become the school's only copy of anything the policy requires (§7.1).
- *Digital Policy* v1.1, September 2024 ([ADEK, hosted copy](https://gardencityschool.ae/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/ADEK-School-Digital-Policy-Final.pdf)). Schools must have "a third-party risk assessment framework for selecting external IT service providers and products ... including learning application providers" covering, at minimum: compatibility, "secure management of data", "compliance with cybersecurity standards and frameworks", "security against cyber threats", "service delivery and backup/recovery provisions", "reputation and financial stability of the provider", "adherence of the vendor to the Federal Decree-Law No. (45) of 2021 ... and the ADEK terms and conditions, copyright policy, and data privacy policy", and age-appropriateness (5.5.1 a to h); schools must tell vendors they are subject to the PDPL (5.5.2); responsible-use standards must cover "the uploading of student data on external applications and learning tools" (4.1.5); cybersecurity incidents need response plans "including the protocols for reporting the incident to the school leadership team and to ADEK", and **"Schools shall not communicate any cybersecurity incident to external parties except for the service provider involved and ADEK" (6.6.1)**; a school Data Protection Policy is required (7.1). **Consequences:** §9.13's review pack is organised by 5.5.1 a to h, because that is the checklist ACS's IT is obliged to run. §9.6's incident process respects 6.6.1: CAROS speaks to the school and, on the school's instruction, to ADEK; it does not notify families directly.
- *Reporting Policy* v1.1 and *Compliance Policy* v1.1 ([ADEK](https://www.adek.gov.ae/en/Education-System/Education-Policies/School-Policies-Governance-and-Operations)). Communication with ADEK through official portals under the PDPL (Reporting 1.2); student data flows to ADEK's eSIS (Reporting 1.3.3). Nothing in either policy requires in-country hosting of student data.
- *Career and University Guidance Policy* v1.2 ([ADEK](https://www.adek.gov.ae/-/media/Project/TAMM/ADEK/Policies/School-Policies/Teaching-and-Learning/ADEK_S_CU-Guidance-Policy_EN.pdf)). Schools must appoint at least one full-time-equivalent CU Guidance Counselor, "with the aim of ensuring that they have at least one dedicated full-time CU Guidance Counselor by 1 September, 2026" (2.1); the counselor's responsibilities include "understanding the career aspirations of each student in Cycle 3, starting in Grade 9/Year 10", guiding to best-fit destinations, "ensuring that each student has submitted at least one application", and "engaging with parents throughout" (2.4); schools report "student coverage" and "counselor-student ratio" to ADEK annually (3.2). **Consequence:** counseling records are processing the school is *required* to do, which is the strongest lawful-basis argument for the caseload data (§1.5), and pass 6 can give the reporting module the two ADEK indicators for free.
- **No ADEK policy retrieved requires student data to be hosted inside the UAE.** The residency decision in CONTEXT §3 stands on the PDPL's transfer rules, on the ICT Health Law where health data is involved, and on the school's expectation; it is not an ADEK rule as far as this pass can find. Question 29 in `ACS-IT-QUESTIONS.md` stays open.

**Federal Decree-Law No. 26 of 2025 on Child Digital Safety.** In force 1 January 2026, with full compliance expected by January 2027 "unless the deadline is extended by a Cabinet decision" ([Clyde & Co, January 2026](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/01/uae-issues-landmark-child-digital-safety-law); [Baker McKenzie, January 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/uae-issues-new-child-digital-safety-law)). It applies to "digital platforms and internet service providers operating in the UAE or directed to users in the UAE", with digital platforms described broadly (websites, applications, search, gaming, streaming, e-commerce). A child is "an individual under 18 years of age". Obligations include privacy-by-default settings, "effective and reasonable age-verification mechanisms, with the strength of the mechanism proportionate to the platform's risk classification", for children under 13 no processing of personal data without verifiable custodian consent and clear disclosures, no targeted advertising or commercial profiling of children, filtering and parental controls, and reporting channels; the TDRA supervises ISPs; penalties are "blocking, suspension, or fines". Clyde & Co notes that "Certain education and health platforms may be exempted by Cabinet decision, subject to safeguards". **Consequence:** CAROS is plausibly a digital platform with under-18 users. Whether an education platform used under a school contract is in scope, or exempted, is unknown and is the first question for counsel. The plan complies regardless: privacy by default (§3), age from the SIS date of birth (a stronger check than any self-declaration), no advertising and no commercial profiling (the engine profiles for welfare, which must be disclosed as such), a student-facing reporting channel (§5.7), and a hard gate that a student under 13 gets no free-text-to-model feature until custodian consent is recorded (§1.5).

**Federal Decree-Law No. 34 of 2021 on Combatting Rumours and Cybercrimes.** Unauthorised access to systems and data is criminal (Articles 2 to 4), interception and its disclosure carry imprisonment and fines up to AED 1,000,000 (Article 12), and DLA Piper reads Article 13 as imposing detention and/or AED 50,000 to 500,000 for collecting or processing personal data in violation of UAE law ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE); [Kayrouz & Associates guide](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/guide-to-cybercrime-law-in-the-uae)). **Consequence:** an insider at CAROS who reads a school's data without a grant is not only breaching a contract (§3.7, §8.7). ADEK Digital 6.6.2 also requires schools to adhere to this law in incident handling.

**Federal Decree-Law No. 31 of 2021 (Penal Code), Articles 431 and 432.** Disclosure of confidential information by a person with professional access: a fine of at least AED 20,000 and/or at least one year's imprisonment (DLA Piper's summary). Applies to counselors and to CAROS staff alike.

**Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 on the Use of ICT in Health Fields, Article 13.** Health data "may not be stored, processed, generated or transferred outside the UAE" without authorisation; fines AED 500,000 to 700,000; exemptions under Ministerial Decision No. 51 of 2021 ([Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/news/health-data-transfers-outside-the-uae/); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2019/06/new-uae-law-regulates-healthcare-data); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)). Whether a school's SEN or medical fields are "health fields" processing is untested. **Rule:** CAROS imports no medical records (ADEK lists them as a school record category; CAROS has no use for them), and SEN status exists only as a `fairness.label` that never leaves the UAE and that the engine cannot read (pass 3 §12.4). Even that is flagged for counsel (question C5).

**Federal Decree-Law No. 25 of 2025 (Civil Transactions Law).** The age of majority is 18 Gregorian years from 1 June 2026, replacing 21 lunar years ([The National, 1 January 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/01/01/uae-lowers-age-of-adulthood-to-18-in-modernisation-drive/); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/legal/uae-civil-transaction-law)). **Consequence:** a Grade 12 student who turns 18 is an adult data subject. §3.5 sets what that does to parental access; counsel confirms.

### 1.2 What is good practice here, and not law

These shape the design and are what a school's lawyers and a US-trained counseling team will recognise. None of them binds ACS or CAROS in Abu Dhabi, and the plan says so wherever it borrows from them.

- **GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679).** Article 3 applies the Regulation to processing "in the context of the activities of an establishment of a controller or a processor in the Union" or to the offering of goods or services to, or monitoring of, data subjects "who are in the Union" ([Article 3](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-3-gdpr/)). ACS and CAROS have no EU establishment and offer nothing to people in the Union; families holding EU passports do not bring the GDPR with them. It is the design floor anyway, because the PDPL follows its shape, because the Executive Regulations are expected to as well, and because a second school may be in a jurisdiction where it does apply. Articles used as templates: 5 (principles), 6 and 9 (bases and special categories), 8 (a child's consent for information society services is lawful from 16, Member States may lower to 13, and the controller "shall make reasonable efforts to verify" parental consent; [Article 8](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-8-gdpr/)), 12 to 22 (rights), 25 (data protection by design), 28(3)(a) to (h) (the processor contract; quoted in §1.4), 30 (records), 32 (security), 33 (notification to the authority "not later than 72 hours" and by the processor to the controller "without undue delay"; [Article 33](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-33-gdpr/)), 35 (impact assessment). The Commission's Digital Omnibus would change the breach threshold and single-entry-point mechanics, but as of mid-2026 "the Data Omnibus, covering GDPR ... is still under negotiation" ([European Parliament legislative train](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-a-new-plan-for-europe-s-sustainable-prosperity-and-competitiveness/file-digital-package)); nothing to plan on.
- **FERPA (20 U.S.C. §1232g; 34 CFR Part 99).** Part 99 "applies to an educational agency or institution to which funds have been made available under any program administered by the Secretary" of Education ([34 CFR 99.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/99.1)). ACS is assisted by the State Department's Office of Overseas Schools, which "provides guidance and best practices but is not involved in day-to-day policies" and does not operate the schools ([State Department](https://www.state.gov/bureaus-offices/under-secretary-for-management/bureau-of-administration/office-of-overseas-schools)); those are not Department of Education funds. **Assumption:** FERPA does not bind ACS. The expectations it sets are real at an American-curriculum school, and the plan adopts three of its shapes. (1) A contractor is a "school official" only if it "performs an institutional service or function for which the agency or institution would otherwise use employees", "is under the direct control of the agency or institution with respect to the use and maintenance of education records" and is bound by the redisclosure rules ([34 CFR 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/99.31)); the DPA says exactly this. (2) The institution must use "reasonable methods to ensure that school officials obtain access to only those education records in which they have legitimate educational interests" (99.31(a)(1)(ii)); §3 is that method. (3) Records "kept in the sole possession of the maker, are used only as a personal memory aid, and are not accessible or revealed to any other person" are not education records ([34 CFR 99.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/99.3)); a counselor note in CAROS is *not* sole possession, because cover and the caseload lead can read it, so in a FERPA frame it becomes part of the record a parent may request. §7.4's subject-access procedure is built for that. An "eligible student" is one who "has reached 18 years of age" (99.3), the same threshold the UAE now uses.
- **The SDPC National Data Privacy Agreement, v2.2 (19 November 2025).** The Student Data Privacy Consortium's template, used in over 130,000 signed agreements between US districts and vendors ([SDPC](https://privacy.a4l.org/national-dpa/); [A4L announcement](https://a4l.org/student-data-privacy-consortium-sdpc-announce-the-release-of-the-national-data-privacy-agreement-version-2/)). It is the DPA shape an American-curriculum school's leadership may already know; §1.4 borrows its exhibit structure. The Student Privacy Pledge was retired by the Future of Privacy Forum on 25 April 2025 and should not be cited ([FPF](https://fpf.org/student-privacy-pledge/)).
- **CISA's K-12 Education Technology Secure by Design pledge** (September 2023): publish a vulnerability disclosure policy and a secure-by-design roadmap, provide single sign-on at no extra cost, and provide security audit logs at no extra cost ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/cisa-announces-secure-design-pledge-k-12-education-technology-providers)). All four are commitments in §9.13.
- **The UK ICO's Age Appropriate Design Code ("Children's code").** A statutory code of fifteen standards for online services "likely to be accessed by children" ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/)). Not UAE law; used as the template for the child-facing surface: best interests as the first test, high-privacy defaults, minimisation, profiling off unless justified with protections, no nudging, and notices a Grade 9 student can read (§1.5).
- **Standards.** OWASP ASVS 5.0 (released May 2025; about 350 requirements in 17 chapters; [OWASP ASVS on GitHub](https://github.com/OWASP/ASVS)) at Level 2 is the verification target; OWASP Top 10:2025 (A01 Broken Access Control through A10 Mishandling of Exceptional Conditions; [OWASP](https://top10.owasp.org/2025)) and the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 (LLM01 Prompt Injection through LLM10 Unbounded Consumption; [OWASP GenAI](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/)) organise §8; NIST SP 800-63B-4 (final, 26 August 2025; [NIST](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html)) sets the session and authenticator rules in §2.4; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and SOC 2 Type II are the certifications a school's IT will eventually ask for (§9.13). Microsoft's DESC CSP Security Standard certificate "applies to two Azure UAE regions: UAE Central (Abu Dhabi) and UAE North (Dubai)" ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/compliance/offerings/offering-uae-desc)); DESC is a Dubai standard and does not bind an Abu Dhabi school, but it is in-country evidence the school can read.

### 1.5 Lawful basis, consent and notices for minors

The school chooses the basis; this plan recommends one and builds the mechanics for the alternatives.

**Recommended basis for the core processing** (directory, academic, attendance, behaviour, signals, cases, notes, university journey): not consent. Three of the PDPL Article 4 cases apply together: performance of the enrolment contract between the school and the family; the school's legal obligations under the ADEK Career and University Guidance Policy (guidance for every Cycle 3 student from Grade 9) and the Student Protection Policy (identifying and supporting students at risk); and protection of the data subject's interests (safeguarding). Consent is the wrong instrument for a function the school must perform and a child cannot opt out of; a withdrawn consent would leave a counselor unable to see a student who needs them. This reading is an **assumption for counsel** (question C1) and the school's DPO decides.

**Where consent is right:** optional processing that the family or student can decline without losing the school's service. Mentor programme participation (student and custodian, recorded on `mentor.pairing`); Google Classroom activity as an engagement source (§6.6); AI features where the school elects consent as its Article 23 transfer basis rather than contractual safeguards (open decision 4); a student under 13 using any feature that sends their free text to a model (Child Digital Safety Law; hard gate on `sis.student.date_of_birth`).

**ADEK Records 1.5.1 ("parent's permission before transferring student information to any third party").** This is the one rule that is not satisfied by a lawful basis. The school must be able to show that parents permitted the transfer to CAROS. Two mechanisms, both supported: the school's enrolment terms name educational service providers and CAROS is added to the school's published list (the usual practice; question 109 asks whether ACS's terms do this), or a per-family notice with acknowledgement collected through the parent portal's activation (§2.2). CAROS records the school's attestation of its mechanism in `core.school.legal_basis_attestation` (jsonb: mechanism, date, attesting person) and **refuses to import real data until it is present** (the tenant stays `onboarding`). Per-guardian `consent_status` (pass 1) then means: `given` when the family acknowledged the notice or a consent column was mapped; `withdrawn` when a guardian objected through the portal or the school recorded it; `unknown` otherwise. **What `unknown` permits:** everything the school's attested basis covers. **What `withdrawn` does:** it is a signal to the school, shown on the file, not an automatic switch, because the school as controller decides whether the objection can be honoured (an objection to safeguarding processing cannot be). Optional features check `consent_status = 'given'` on the relevant link.

**Transparency.** Three notices, versioned in `privacy.notice_version` (new table, §10) and acknowledged in `privacy.notice_ack` on first sign-in and on each new version: one for staff, one for parents, one for students written at a reading level a Grade 9 student can follow (the Children's code's transparency standard). Each states: what CAROS holds, why, that an engine evaluates each student against their own history and what that means (pass 3's disclosure question 107), that AI features send pseudonymised text outside the UAE to a named provider under the retention terms in §6, that shadow mode produces inferences nobody acts on during the pilot, who can see what (the matrix rendered from `auth.role_permission`), how long data is kept, and how to ask for a copy, a correction or erasure. The school reviews the text; CAROS does not publish a notice a school has not approved.

**Students who turn 18.** From the eighteenth birthday, rights belong to the student (Civil Transactions Law; FERPA's "eligible student" as the American frame). Default: existing guardian links continue, because the school's relationship with the family continues and tuition is a family matter; the student is told, on their next sign-in, that their parents can see the parent portal's view and may ask the school to end it. If the student objects, the school decides and records it on the link (`ended_reason = 'student_objection_18'`). Counsel confirms whether UAE law requires the default to be the other way round (question C6).

**Age verification.** The SIS date of birth is the age source; CAROS asks nobody their age. Under-13 students (rare in Grade 9; possible in an international intake) are identified at import and the under-13 gate applies automatically.

## 2. Identity

Three populations, three paths, one rule: **signing in proves who you are; it never decides what you may see.** Access comes from memberships, roster rows, guardian links and pairings, all of which are written by imports or by an administrator's audited act, never by the act of signing in.

### 2.1 Staff and students: Google Workspace

**Protocol.** OpenID Connect authorization-code flow with PKCE, handled server-side in `apps/web`, scopes `openid email profile` only. No Drive, Gmail or Classroom scope is ever requested by the sign-in client; the Classroom reader (pass 2 §7.6) is a separate service account the school delegates to, so the sign-in app stays eligible for the Workspace admin's "Limited" setting ("App can only access unrestricted services"; [Google Workspace Admin Help](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/apps/control-which-apps-access-google-workspace-data)). The request carries `hd=<school domain>` for the account-picker, and the server **validates the `hd` claim in the ID token**, not the parameter: Google's own guidance is "Don't rely on this UI optimization to control who can access your app, as client-side requests can be modified. Be sure to validate that the returned ID token has an `hd` claim value that matches what you expect", because "the ID token `hd` claim is contained within a security token from Google, so the value can be trusted" ([Google OpenID Connect](https://developers.google.com/identity/openid-connect/openid-connect)). The server also validates `iss` (`https://accounts.google.com` or `accounts.google.com`), `aud` (the client id), `exp`, `nonce`, `state`, and requires `email_verified = true`. The identity key is `sub`, which is "unique among all Google Accounts and never reused"; the email is display text (same page). A token whose `hd` matches no row in `auth.school_domain` (new, §10: `school_id`, `domain`, `population staff|student|both`, `verified_at`) is rejected before any tenant query runs; the domain-to-school lookup is the one deliberate cross-tenant read in the system, and it reads a table that holds domains and nothing about people.

**Mapping a Google identity to a person and a role.** In the tenant the `hd` claim selects: (1) look up `core.person.google_subject = sub`; (2) if absent, look up `core.person.email = token.email` among persons of kind `staff` or `student` (pass 2's rung 3, with the same corroboration rules: for students the SIS is the source of the address, so a unique match binds; for staff a unique match binds and the binding is audited `IDENTITY_BOUND` with both values); (3) if nothing matches, the person sees a "no access" page that names the school's contact, the attempt is audited `SIGN_IN_DENIED` with reason `no_person`, and a queue item appears for the `school_admin` ("a Google account in your domain signed in and has no CAROS record"). **Nothing is created at sign-in.** Pass 1's `auth.membership.source = 'sso_jit'` is therefore never written; §10 removes the value so that no later session can reintroduce just-in-time provisioning by accident. A person with no active membership sees the same "no access" page. A person with one membership enters as that role; with several, they choose an acting role (§2.4).

**Roles come from two sources only.** `teacher` memberships are written by the staff import (pass 2 §4.8) and end when the staff snapshot no longer lists the person. `counselor` memberships and every capability (`ib_coordinator`, `caseload_lead`, `safeguarding_lead`, `school_admin`, and this pass's `mentor_coordinator`) are granted by a `school_admin` in the administration screens (§2.5), under step-up, and audited. A job title in an export is never an authorisation (pass 2's rule, kept).

**What the school must configure, and why it is a gate.** Google Workspace for Education requires the school to configure third-party apps for users designated under 18: such users "are blocked from using unconfigured third-party apps" and may only request access; the admin marks the app Trusted, Limited, Specific Google data or Blocked per organisational unit ([Manage access to unconfigured third-party apps for users designated as under 18](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/getting-started/editions/manage-access-to-unconfigured-third-party-apps-for-users-designated-as-under-18)). The same page states: "For users under the age of 18, your organization is responsible for obtaining parental consent, if required by applicable law, before allowing these users to access third-party apps." That sentence is Google saying what §1.5 says: the school's basis and its ADEK Records 1.5.1 permission come before a student can sign in. Onboarding step (pass 6): the school's Workspace admin sets CAROS to Limited for the staff and student organisational units, confirms which units are designated under 18 in the age-based access setting, and CAROS records the confirmation in the tenant's onboarding checklist.

**Deprovisioning.** Four signals, each of which revokes every session of the person and is audited `SESSION_REVOKED` with its reason:

1. **Membership end.** When the last active membership ends (import absence after pass 2's 14-day grace, or an administrator's "offboard now", which needs no grace), sessions are revoked in the same transaction. Pass 2 said "sessions are revoked at `left`"; the precise rule is *at the end of the last active membership*, which is normally the same moment, and immediately when an administrator ends it by hand.
2. **Google's Cross-Account Protection (RISC).** CAROS registers a receiver for the security event types `sessions-revoked`, `tokens-revoked`, `account-disabled`, `account-credential-change-required` (and `account-enabled`, `verification`), each delivered as a signed security event token that the receiver validates against Google's keys ([Google, Cross-Account Protection](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/risc)). An `account-disabled` or `sessions-revoked` event for a `sub` revokes CAROS sessions within seconds of the school suspending the account, which makes the school's ordinary leaver process ("suspend the Google account", question 20) the fastest deprovisioning path. The RISC stream is configured through a Google Cloud project that holds only the receiver URL and the event-type list; the tokens carry the `sub` Google already holds, so nothing new leaves the country.
3. **Directory reconciliation (optional, recommended).** A nightly `users.list` on the Admin SDK Directory API, through the same domain-wide-delegated reader account pass 2 designs for Classroom, with the read-only scope `admin.directory.user.readonly`, ends memberships whose Google account is `suspended` or deleted ([Directory API users resource](https://developers.google.com/workspace/admin/directory/reference/rest/v1/users)). It is the belt to RISC's braces, and it needs one more scope the school must approve (question 110).
4. **CAROS administrator action.** "Sign out everywhere" on a person, or ending the tenant.

**Students whose accounts persist after leaving.** A student who has graduated may keep a Google account for a while; their `student` membership ends with the enrolment (pass 2 §4.4), so the account signs in to "no access" until the alumni or mentor path gives it something (§2.3).

### 2.2 Parents: magic links bound to verified relationships

Three ideas, kept apart: the **link** signs in a person; the **relationship** decides what that person sees; the **activation** proves that the inbox reaches the right human before either matters.

1. **Guardians exist only from the SIS.** A `core.person` of kind `guardian` and its `family.guardian_link` rows come from the contacts import (`verified_source = 'sis_import'`) or from a `school_admin` who creates a link by hand with a reason (`verified_source = 'school_admin'`). No self-registration exists. `parentOf()`'s fabricated default is not ported.
2. **Activation, once per guardian.** Before the first magic link is ever sent, the guardian must present an **activation code** that the school issued through a channel that is not the email: printed in the enrolment pack, handed over at a parent evening, sent from the school's own SMS or messaging system, or read out by the front office after the school's identity check. The code is eight characters of Crockford base32 (40 bits), single-use, expires after 30 days, stored hashed, issued and reissued only by a `school_admin` (audited `ACTIVATION_ISSUED`). The guardian enters the code and their email; the email **must equal the SIS address** on the guardian record; a mismatch is refused with a message to contact the school, which corrects the SIS and re-imports. CAROS never lets a guardian change the address they are about to prove. Then, and only then, the first magic link goes to the SIS address. The activation binds `verified_at` on every link of that guardian. Schools that cannot distribute codes may choose knowledge-based activation (student number plus the student's date of birth plus the last four digits of the guardian's SIS phone number); it is weaker, because the student's number and birthday are widely known, so it is allowed only when the school records that choice in `core.school.parent_activation_mode = 'knowledge'` and accepts the residual risk in the DPA. Recommendation: codes.
3. **The link.** 256 bits from a CSPRNG; only its SHA-256 is stored (`auth.magic_link.token_hash`); 15-minute expiry (a compromise between NIST's 10-minute out-of-band rule and email delivery delays; [NIST SP 800-63B-4 §3.1.3.2](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html)); single use; **bound to the requesting browser**: the request sets a `__Host-ml_nonce` cookie, the link's GET renders a confirmation page, and consumption is a POST that succeeds only when the cookie matches the row. A link opened on another device shows "open this on the device where you asked for it", with a six-digit code displayed on the requesting device that can be typed on the second one. This defeats the two ordinary failures of magic links: a forwarded or harvested link, and mail-security proxies that pre-fetch every URL (the GET does nothing). Rate limits: three links per address per hour, ten per day, uniform responses so addresses cannot be enumerated. Audited `MAGIC_LINK_SENT` and `MAGIC_LINK_CONSUMED`.
4. **After activation.** Plain magic links to the SIS address, or a passkey the guardian may enrol to skip the inbox (WebAuthn Level 3 became a W3C Recommendation on 25 August 2026; [W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/)).
5. **What a signed-in guardian sees.** Exactly the students with an active, verified `guardian_link` (`linked_children` scope, pass 1). Several children: a child switcher; each child under that child's grade-conditional navigation; no merged view (pass 2 §4.7). The switcher lists linked children only, and every route that takes a student id returns the same not-found response for a non-linked id as for a non-existent one, so a guardian cannot learn that another child exists.
6. **Two guardians sharing one email** (pass 2 open decision 4). Accepted as pass 2's option (b) with conditions: one `core.person`, both SIS contact ids as external identities, one activation code **per SIS contact**; the first to activate binds the login; the second contact's code adds them as a named co-holder of the same login (`family.guardian_link.co_holder_activated_at`) so that consent is recorded per person. Every message and meeting request from a shared login shows "shared family login" to the counselor, who then never assumes which parent wrote it. A family that wants separate logins gives the school separate addresses.
7. **The SIS address changes.** When an import changes a guardian's email, the activation is invalidated (`activation_status = 'reactivation_required'`), sessions are revoked, and the new address must activate again with a new code. The school confirmed the change by importing it; CAROS still confirms that the new inbox reaches the same relationship, because this is where a typo becomes a stranger reading a child's file (§8.3).
8. **Ended links.** A child leaves, or the school ends a link (custody change, court order): access ends at the moment the link ends; the guardian sees "no children linked" and the school's contact.
9. **A guardian who is also staff.** Two persons unless a `school_admin` links them through pass 2's identity candidate. Even when linked, sessions are per acting role (§2.4): a staff session never gains `linked_children` and a parent session never gains `caseload` or `roster`.

### 2.3 Mentors: invited, vetted, in-platform only

Mentors are alumni volunteers, adults, outside the school's identity system, and in contact with minors. ADEK's Student Protection Policy defines a volunteer as "an individual engaged by a educational institution on a non-remunerated basis to interact with students" and obliges principals to "ensure the vetting, hiring, and monitoring of all staff, volunteers, and invited visitors" ([ADEK Student Protection Policy](https://www.gemsaa-abudhabi.com/en/-/media/project/gems/gaa_gems_american_academy_abu_dhabi/_files-and-documents/adek_s_student-protection-policy.pdf), Roles, 15). The path follows that obligation.

1. **Sponsorship and vetting.** A staff member holding the new `mentor_coordinator` capability creates the mentor as a `core.person` of kind `mentor` (name, personal email) and records the school's vetting on `mentor.profile.vetting` (jsonb, §10): identity document seen (type, expiry, by whom, when), criminal-record check (jurisdiction, date, reference), safeguarding training date, signed volunteer agreement date, and a `vetting_expires_at` for annual renewal. The documents themselves stay in the school's volunteer file; CAROS holds the fact that they were seen, not the images (minimisation). `verification_status = 'verified'` can be set only when every field is present, and `active` requires `verified` (pass 1's CHECK). Only the school can do any of this; CAROS staff cannot verify a mentor.
2. **Invitation and credential.** A single-use, seven-day invitation token by email. On acceptance the mentor creates a **passkey** (WebAuthn: platform authenticator or security key). For a device without passkey support, the fallback is a password of at least 12 characters checked against the Have I Been Pwned Pwned Passwords range API (k-anonymity on the first five characters of the SHA-1; no key or licence required; [HIBP API v3](https://haveibeenpwned.com/API/v3)), which is how NIST's requirement that "verifiers SHALL compare the prospective secret against a blocklist that contains known commonly used, expected, or compromised passwords" is met ([NIST SP 800-63B-4 §3.1.1.2](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html)), stored with Argon2id, **plus mandatory TOTP**. Recovery is by the coordinator re-inviting, never by email alone. `auth.session.auth_method` gains `passkey`, `password_totp` and drops the placeholder `mentor_password` (§10).
3. **Assurance and lifetime.** AAL2-equivalent; sessions of 12 hours absolute and 30 minutes idle (§2.4). Mentors are the population with the least school oversight of their devices, so they get the shortest staff-like sessions.
4. **What a mentor can see.** Students with an `active` pairing only (`paired` scope). Columns: the student's name, year group, declared goal, and whatever the student put in `share_scope` (goal by default; network optional). Pass 1's column revocations hide email, phone, date of birth, nationality and fee status from the mentor tier; this pass adds `sis.student.student_number` and all of `academic`, `attendance`, `behaviour`, `signal`, `family` to the never-visible list for mentors (the matrix in §3.6 has no mentor entry in those rows). A mentor cannot search for students, cannot see other mentors' pairings, and cannot initiate a pairing: the school arranges introductions (`mentor.pairing.arranged_by_person_id` is required before `active`), which is invariant 11 as a constraint.
5. **In-platform contact, visible to the school.** The `mentor` schema has no email or phone column (pass 1). Messages are `mentor.message` rows, append-only. Every thread is readable by the student's counselor (caseload scope), by the `mentor_coordinator` and by the `safeguarding_lead` (school scope), and both participants are told so in the thread header. That visibility is the safeguarding control; the deterrent is knowing it exists. Two mechanical controls sit under it. **Holding:** a message containing a phone number, an email address or a social-media handle (pattern match, plus pass 5's classifier for "let's talk somewhere else" phrasing) is held for the coordinator's review before delivery, and the sender is told it is held. **Rate:** a mentor may send at most twenty messages a day per student. Pass 5's safety screen runs on every message: a disclosure routes to the counselor exactly as student free text does (§5.7); grooming indicators route to the coordinator and the safeguarding lead.
6. **Sessions.** Scheduled through CAROS; held on the school's own video platform in the school's tenant, or in person on school premises, under the school's supervision policy. CAROS stores the schedule, whether it was held, and the mentor's note (`school_visible` is always true in v1; the column stays for a later decision but no screen can set it false). **CAROS records nothing.** The prototype's "sessions ... are recorded" is not ported (§0.2); `recording_file_id` stays NULL. Whether the school records its own calls is the school's decision under its own policy (open decision 9).
7. **Ending.** The student, the counselor or the coordinator ends a pairing with one action; the mentor's read access to that student ends at that instant (RLS), and mentors have no export, so nothing leaves with them. A mentor's `active` flag is cleared at the end of the programme year and on any safeguarding concern; the person record follows the mentor retention class (§7.1).
8. **Pass 1's alumni fixtures** (`PATH_ALUMNI`, `ALUMNI_OUTCOMES`) are reporting content, not identities, and never a route into the mentor path.

### 2.4 Sessions, the acting role, and step-up

- **Sessions are server-side rows** (`auth.session`) referenced by an opaque identifier of 128 random bits, well above the 64 bits OWASP requires ([OWASP Session Management Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html)), in a `__Host-caros_s` cookie with `Secure`, `HttpOnly` and `SameSite=Lax` (`Lax` because the return from Google is a top-level navigation that must carry the cookie; CSRF on mutations is covered by tRPC's origin check and a per-session token). No JWT-only trust: every request loads the row, checks `revoked_at`, `expires_at` and idle time, and updates `last_seen_at`. Every check runs before `withTenant()` opens the transaction that will carry the person id (pass 1 DR-4).
- **One acting role per session** (pass 1 open decision 7, resolved as option (a)). A person with several memberships picks one at sign-in; the row records `acting_role`; the database tier role and the column privileges follow it. Switching role creates a **new** session after re-authentication (the OWASP rule that the identifier "must be renewed or regenerated ... after any privilege level change"), and the old session is revoked. Capabilities are read live from the membership on every request, so ending a capability takes effect on the next request.
- **Lifetimes.** NIST SP 800-63B-4 sets, at AAL2, a reauthentication timeout that "SHOULD be no more than 24 hours" and an inactivity timeout that "SHOULD be no more than 1 hour"; at AAL1 an overall timeout of no more than 30 days ([NIST](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html), §§2.1.3, 2.2.3). Proposal, per population:

| Population | Absolute | Idle | Concurrent sessions | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Counselor, teacher, any capability holder | 12 hours | 60 minutes | 5 | A school day; welfare data; shared staffroom machines |
| Student | 7 days | 24 hours | unlimited | Own record only; phones; NIST AAL1 allows 30 days |
| Parent | 14 days | 48 hours | unlimited | Own children's record; phones; the 14 days *is* the "remember me" |
| Mentor | 12 hours | 30 minutes | 2 | Outsider with access to a minor's messages |
| CAROS support (under grant) | 1 hour | 15 minutes | 1 | AAL3-shaped; every read audited |

- **Revocation** on: membership end, RISC event, credential change, "sign out everywhere", tenant suspension, and the daily job that expires stale rows. Sessions belong to the `session` retention class (30 days after expiry, pass 1).
- **Step-up** is re-authentication within the last ten minutes, recorded in `auth.session.step_up_at` and audited `STEP_UP` with the action it unlocked. For Google users it is a fresh Google sign-in with `prompt=login`; for mentors a passkey assertion with user verification; for parents a fresh magic link. Required for: raising an escalation; acknowledging or closing one; any `export` action (§3.6); opening the audit-log screen; granting, accepting or revoking a support grant; changing memberships, capabilities, role permissions or the safeguarding route; activating a mapping profile or rolling back an import; self-granting break-glass cover (§3.3); scheduling or executing an erasure; downloading a document of class `safeguarding` or `case_note`.
- **Anomalies.** A staff sign-in from a new country or autonomous system emits an in-app notice to the person and the `school_admin` (identifiers and coarse location only); more than five failed magic-link requests for one address in an hour throttles the address; more than twenty `SIGN_IN_DENIED` events from one domain in a day pages the on-call, because it may be a misconfigured import rather than an attack.

### 2.5 Identity administration

The `school_admin` capability owns five screens, each action audited (`MEMBERSHIP_CHANGED`, `CASELOAD_ASSIGNED`, `COVER_GRANTED`, `ACTIVATION_ISSUED`, `MENTOR_VETTED`, `ROUTE_CHANGED`) and each grant behind step-up: **People** (identities that signed in with no person, and pass 2's identity candidates); **Memberships** (grant or end `counselor`, grant or end capabilities); **Caseload and cover** (primary assignments and time-boxed cover); **Parents** (activation codes, activation status, link ends); **Mentors** (vetting, invitations, pairings, held messages, with the `mentor_coordinator`). Three rules keep the administrator from becoming a single point of failure: the first `school_admin` membership in a real tenant is created by CAROS at onboarding for the person the DPA names, and CAROS creates no other membership in a real tenant except through a support grant approved by the school (§3.7); granting `school_admin` to a second person requires confirmation by an existing `school_admin` (four eyes; audited); `school_admin` cannot read welfare data by virtue of the capability, because the matrix gives it `config`, `ingest`, `directory` and `documents` of kind `sis_export` only.

## 3. Authorization

### 3.1 The permission model as data

Pass 1's `auth.role_permission` (`school_id`, `role_key`, `data_class`, `action`, `scope`, `capability`) stands. This pass changes four things and fills the rows.

1. **`auth.data_class` gains `sensitivity`** (`0` to `5`, §10), which drives three mechanical consequences: whether a read writes an access entry (sensitivity 3 and above always, 2 at file-open granularity, 0 and 1 never), whether a download is watermarked and step-up gated (4 and 5 always), and whether a class may ever be sent to a model (5 never, 4 never, 3 only pseudonymised; §6.2).

| Sensitivity | Classes | Access logging |
|---|---|---|
| 0 reference | `reference`, `config` (non-personal rows) | none |
| 1 directory | `directory` | none for lists; `DIRECTORY_SEARCH` for whole-school search |
| 2 educational record | `academic`, `attendance`, `behaviour`, `university`, `ib`, `discovery` (proposals, roadmaps), `documents`, `family`, `ingest`, `reporting` | `FILE_ACCESS` when a student's file is opened; `PREVIEW_ACCESS` for import previews; `EXPORT` |
| 3 welfare-sensitive | `signal`, `signal_shadow`, `counselor_log`, `case_note`, `teacher_flag`, `student_voice`, `engagement`, `mentor` (messages, notes), `ai` (generations about a student), `discovery` (chat text) | every read of a record, by the reading transaction |
| 4 safeguarding | `safeguarding` | every read, with purpose required |
| 5 special category | `fairness_label`; columns `sis.student.nationality_iso`, `fee_status_home_country`, `date_of_birth`; any SEN or health field if one is ever imported | no application role reads `fairness_label`; column reads are inside a file open and are logged with it |

2. **Three data classes from pass 3 are added** (`signal_shadow`, `counselor_log`, `fairness_label`; pass 3 D30) and placed in the matrix.
3. **A sixth action, `export`,** is added to `auth.perm_action`: producing a file, a PDF, a printable list or a clipboard copy of more than one student's record, or any document of sensitivity 3 or above. Export rows are sparse on purpose.
4. **A fifth capability, `mentor_coordinator`,** on the counselor role: vets mentors, arranges pairings, reviews held messages (§2.3).

A new role, or a school that wants a stricter matrix, is rows. The school-specific override rule (a school row with the same key wins over the global default) is pass 1's; a school may only **narrow** the default: a trigger refuses a school row whose scope is wider than the default row's scope for the same key, because widening a default is a product decision, not a configuration.

### 3.2 Enforcement, in four layers that each hold alone

1. **Database, tenant.** The `RESTRICTIVE` `tenant_isolation` policy on every tenant table, `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`, composite foreign keys, a `NOBYPASSRLS` login role, and a transaction context that fails closed (pass 1 DR-4). Nothing in this pass weakens it; §8.1 lists the tests.
2. **Database, role.** The `PERMISSIVE` role policies generated by `auth.protect()` from `auth.role_permission`, plus column privileges per tier. These enforce `read` and `write`.
3. **Domain layer.** Every domain function begins with `assertAllowed(class, action, subjects)` for its verb, which is the only place `approve`, `escalate`, `configure` and `export` are enforced (row-level security cannot see a verb). The assertion calls the same `auth.allowed()` function the policies call, so there is one definition of "allowed". A function that touches a sensitivity-3-or-above record then writes its access entry in the same transaction (§4.1). A lint rule (`dependency-cruiser` plus a custom rule) fails the build if a domain function issues a query before its assertion.
4. **API and UI.** tRPC middleware refuses any procedure without an authenticated session and an acting role, sets the tenant context from the session (never from the request body), and rate-limits per session. The UI hides what a role cannot do, and is never the reason it cannot.

The worker runs as `actor_kind = 'system'` under `caros_t_system` and is bound by the tenant policy like everyone else; it holds no cross-tenant query path. Its per-school jobs open one `withTenant()` per school.

### 3.3 Counselor cover and handover: caseload plus explicit, logged cover

Pass 1's open decision 8 offered caseload-scoped (a), school-scoped (b) or caseload plus explicit cover (c). **Decision: (c).** The default is caseload scope. A counselor reaches another counselor's student through exactly four paths, each of which is a row and an audit entry:

| Path | Who | How | Duration | Audit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Planned cover** | `caseload_lead` grants; or the owner grants for their own absence | `auth.caseload_assignment` with `kind = 'cover'`, `reason`, `valid_from`, `valid_to` | ≤ 30 days; renewable | `COVER_GRANTED`; every read under cover carries `purpose = 'cover:<assignment_id>'` |
| **Break-glass cover** | any counselor, for one student, when the owner is unreachable | self-granted `cover` row with `reason ≥ 20 chars`, `valid_to = now() + 24 hours`, `self_granted = true`; step-up required | 24 hours; not renewable by the same person; the lead can convert it to planned cover | `COVER_GRANTED` with `self_granted`; in-app notice to the owner and the lead at once; a weekly digest of break-glass use to the lead; the counselor sees a persistent banner "you are reading under break-glass cover" |
| **Reassignment** | owner, or `caseload_lead` | pass 1 C14; the primary assignment moves | permanent | `CASE_REASSIGNED`, `CASELOAD_ASSIGNED` |
| **Coordinator** | `ib_coordinator` | `ib_cohort` scope for `ib` data only | standing | reads of IB records are sensitivity 2; no case, note or signal is reachable through this path |

The `caseload_lead` sees case existence, tier and owner for the whole school (a team lead has to run a team) and may open any file, with every open written as `FILE_ACCESS` with `purpose` required and visible to the owning counselor on the file's access panel. What the lead does *not* get by default is school-wide read of `case_note`: notes are readable by the owner, active cover, and the lead **only when a cover or reassignment is in progress for that student** (`auth.allowed()` checks for an active cover row held by anyone, or a reassignment in the last 14 days). Question 65 asked ACS whether counselors should see each other's cases by default; if the team says yes, the school override rows widen `signal` read to school scope for the counselor role, which the narrowing trigger permits because it is a *school* choosing to be more open, not a default; the trigger rule in §3.1 applies to CAROS's defaults, and a school may widen only `signal` and `case_note`, never `safeguarding`.

The **safeguarding lead** reaches a case only through an escalation (`ESC` scope: the case, its evidence, its notes and the student's file, read-only, while the escalation is open and for 90 days after outcome). They do not see the caseload.

### 3.4 Teachers, scoped by roster

`roster` and `own_section` scopes come from `sis.section_teacher × sis.section_membership` on current rows only (pass 1, pass 2 §5.4). A teacher sees: the directory of their sections (name, year group, section); their own sections' assessments and grades; attendance events for their own sections (code only, no reason text: column privilege on `reason_code`, `reason_label`, `minutes_late` for the staff tier when the acting role is `teacher`, implemented as a security-barrier view `sis.v_attendance_teacher`); the flags they wrote and the routed note; the IB level question for their subjects; Extended Essays they are party to; documents attached to those essays. A teacher never sees a tier, a case, a note, another teacher's flag, a signal, family data, university data or discovery data. **The routed note never states a tier or that a case exists**; its vocabulary is `attached_to_open_case` rendered as "the counselor has taken this up", `reviewed_no_action` as "reviewed by the counselor", and `merged_with_other_concerns` as "linked with other observations". Pass 3's per-teacher concern-rate report is readable by the `caseload_lead` only.

### 3.5 Students and parents

Students read their own record (`self`) across `directory`, `academic`, `attendance` (summary counts and their own events without reason text), `university`, `ib`, `discovery`, `documents`, `mentor`, and write where the product lets them. They never read `signal`, `case_note`, `safeguarding`, `teacher_flag`, `engagement`, `family` messages, `ai` generations other than their own discovery and Extended Essay feedback, or `audit`. Parents read `linked_children` across `directory`, `academic` (the school-issued transcript summaries and current grades the parent portal already shows for Grade 11 and above), `attendance` (summary; no reason text), `university` (targets, requirements, costs, offers; not statement drafts, which are the student's own writing), `ib` (selection status and CAS totals; not sign-off notes), `discovery` (approved pathways only), `family` (their own messages and requests), `mentor` (that a pairing exists and the mentor's headline; not the messages), and `documents` of kind `transcript`. A parent never reads `student_voice` (a child's words to their counselor are not the parent's by default; question C7 asks counsel whether the school must disclose them on request), `signal`, `case_note`, `safeguarding`, `teacher_flag`, `behaviour` (the school communicates behaviour through its own channels in v1; question 111), or `engagement`.

The grade-conditional navigation is a product rule, not a boundary: RLS bounds what the person can read regardless of which grade the UI thinks they are.

**At 18** (Civil Transactions Law), the student's own access is unchanged and parental access continues under the default in §1.5 unless the student objects and the school agrees.

### 3.7 The CAROS support path: not a role, unavoidable, and designed to be embarrassing to use

**Principle.** No CAROS person has standing read access to any real tenant. Production database credentials for humans do not exist; the application connects with a managed identity; developers work against the synthetic tenants (pass 1 DR-9). The onboarding engineer builds a school's mapping profiles against a pseudonymised sample the school produces (pass 2 §9), and the first real preview is the school's own.

**When support must look.** A defect the synthetic tenants cannot reproduce, a school's request to diagnose a wrong import, a restore verification, an incident. The path is pass 1's `auth.support_grant`, completed:

1. **Request.** A CAROS operator opens a grant request naming the school, a ticket reference, a reason of at least 20 characters, the requested duration (≤ 8 hours; pass 1's CHECK), the tables or screens needed, and whether **unmasking** is required (default no: support reads see person names, emails and free text replaced by stable per-grant pseudonyms, through views that the support tier is granted instead of the base tables).
2. **Approval.** By the school: a `school_admin` or `caseload_lead` approves in-app under step-up, and the approval is what activates the grant. If the school is unreachable and the ticket is a live incident affecting the school's service, a **second CAROS operator** may approve (four eyes), the school is notified at once, and the grant is limited to 2 hours and to masked reads; the school's retrospective acknowledgement is requested and its absence is reported in the monthly review. A `safeguarding`-class read needs a separate flag on the grant that only the school's `safeguarding_lead` can approve; the expectation is that it is never used, and any use is reported to the school's leadership.
3. **Session.** The operator authenticates to the support console with a hardware-key passkey (Entra ID with phishing-resistant MFA and Privileged Identity Management for the Azure side; the CAROS support console for the application side), and the session is `auth_method = 'support'`, 1 hour absolute, 15 minutes idle, `actor_kind = 'support'`, `actor_label = <operator id>`, `SET LOCAL ROLE caros_t_support` (read-only grants; pass 1). `auth.allowed()` honours the support claim only while an unrevoked, unexpired grant exists for that school (pass 1's function does this).
4. **Every read is a row.** `SUPPORT_ACCESS` with the grant id, ticket, tables and, for a student-subject row, the student id; the school sees these entries in its own audit screen as "CAROS support read this record", and the counselor sees them on the file's access panel like any other read.
5. **No writes.** A support grant never writes tenant data. A fix is a migration or a job reviewed in a pull request (pass 1 DR-2), or an action the school's own `school_admin` performs with the operator on a call. Pass 2's rollback authority: the `school_admin` rolls back; support may roll back only as a school-approved action recorded on the grant.
6. **Review.** Monthly: every grant, its approver, its duration, its row count, and any four-eyes approvals, reviewed by CAROS's DPO and sent to each school as part of its monthly report. A grant with no ticket, or more than two grants for one school in a month, is an item on that report.
7. **The onboarding engineer's path** (pass 2) is the same grant, requested by the engineer, approved by the school's `school_admin` as part of onboarding, limited to `ingest` screens and previews, and masked by default. Threshold activation during shadow mode (pass 3) is the caseload lead's act with the engineer's acknowledgement recorded on the version note; it needs no grant, because the engineer does not perform it.

**Azure-side access** is separate and also grant-shaped: no standing `Owner` or `Contributor` on the production subscription; Entra Privileged Identity Management activates a role for a bounded time with justification; the activation appears in Azure Activity Log, which is exported to the security Log Analytics workspace and reviewed monthly. Database-level emergencies (a stuck migration) use a break-glass `caros_owner` credential held in Key Vault behind a second person's approval, rotated after each use, and its use is an incident record.

### 3.8 Tests that make the matrix true

In `packages/db/test/authz/`, run on every pull request against both synthetic tenants (pass 1 DR-8):

- For every (role or capability, data class, action) cell in §3.6: a positive fixture (a student in scope) and a negative fixture (a student out of scope: other caseload, other roster, other child, other pairing, other school). The suite is generated from `auth.role_permission` and a fixture map, so a new row without fixtures fails the build.
- The eight "should see nothing" cases: no context; expired session; ended membership; ended cover; ended guardian link; ended pairing; teacher after a roster move; counselor after reassignment.
- Column cases: mentor cannot select the revoked columns; teacher's attendance view has no reason text; guardian tier cannot select nationality.
- Verb cases: `approve`, `escalate`, `configure`, `export` refused in the domain layer for every role that lacks the row, including when the row exists for `read`.
- Narrowing trigger: a school override wider than the default is refused; a `safeguarding` override is refused.
- Support: reads without a grant return zero rows; reads with a masked grant return pseudonyms; an expired grant returns zero rows in the same session.

## 4. Audit

### 4.1 What is logged

Pass 1's `audit.action` taxonomy stands and grows. Two rules govern every entry: **`detail` holds identifiers and enumerations only**, validated on insert against a per-action JSON schema in `packages/contracts` (an entry with an unknown key, or a string longer than 64 characters in any value, is refused), and **the actor, the school, the session and the request come from the transaction context**, never from the caller (pass 1's `audit.record()`). Names, note bodies, reasons, message text, email addresses and phone numbers never enter the log; the entry points at the row that holds them, and that row can be erased without breaking the log.

**Changes.** Every mutation in CONTEXT §9 and pass 1 §2.19, plus this pass's additions: `IDENTITY_BOUND`, `SIGN_IN_DENIED`, `ACTIVATION_ISSUED`, `ACTIVATION_COMPLETED`, `MENTOR_INVITED`, `MENTOR_VETTED`, `PAIRING_ENDED`, `MESSAGE_HELD`, `MESSAGE_RELEASED`, `ROUTE_CHANGED`, `ESCALATION_REMINDED`, `ESCALATION_ROUTED_TO_DEPUTY`, `ESCALATION_UNACKNOWLEDGED_24H`, `DIRECT_REPORT_RECORDED`, `ESCALATION_CLOSED`, `NOTICE_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `SAR_REQUESTED`, `SAR_DELIVERED`, `KEY_DESTROYED`, `TENANT_OFFBOARDED`, `GRANT_REQUESTED`, `GRANT_APPROVED`, `GRANT_REVOKED`, `AI_RULE_REFUSED` (a generation refused because the model or data class failed §6's checks).

**Access.** The reads that write an entry, by sensitivity (§3.1), at the granularity that keeps the log useful rather than enormous:

| Read | Action | Granularity | Subject and detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caseload sheet, command centre, any list that shows tiers | `CASELOAD_VIEW` | one entry per render | `detail.student_ids` (identifiers), `detail.filter` (enumeration) |
| Opening a student's file | `FILE_ACCESS` | one entry per open, plus one per sensitivity-3 tab rendered (`CASE_ACCESS`, `NOTE_ACCESS`, `FLAG_ACCESS`, `VOICE_ACCESS`, `MENTOR_THREAD_ACCESS`) | the student; `purpose` from the vocabulary below, required when the reader is not the owner |
| Opening an escalation | `SAFEGUARDING_ACCESS` | every read | the student; `purpose` required always |
| Downloading or previewing a document | `DOCUMENT_ACCESS` | every read | the student; `detail.file_id`, `detail.kind` |
| Whole-school directory search by a counselor | `DIRECTORY_SEARCH` | per query | `detail.result_count` (never the query text) |
| The co-pilot and every AI feature | `COPILOT_QUERY` plus `AI_PROMPT_BUILT` | per generation | `detail.generation_id`, `detail.input_refs` (the record ids that went into the prompt: this is the answer to "what left the country about this student") |
| The audit screen | `AUDIT_ACCESS` | per query | `detail.filter` (enumerations), `detail.result_count` |
| Any export | `EXPORT` | per file | `detail.row_count`, `detail.format`, `detail.file_sha256`, `detail.watermark_id` |

| Reading a mentor thread as staff | `MENTOR_THREAD_ACCESS` | per thread open | the student; `detail.pairing_id` |

**Purpose vocabulary** (`config.vocabulary 'access_purpose'`, global defaults, no free text): `case_review`, `meeting_prep`, `cover`, `handover`, `escalation`, `parent_request`, `subject_access`, `ib_review`, `support_ticket`, `other`. The prototype's seeded entry, "Scope: assigned cohort · purpose: case review", becomes `purpose = 'case_review'` and the scope is implied by the actor's role.

**Not logged, by rule.** Directory lists inside one's own scope; a student or parent reading their own record (their sessions and `portal.session` events cover it); reference data. Logging a parent's every page view would be surveillance of the family; logging a student's would be surveillance of the child.

### 4.2 Append-only, and whether tamper evidence is warranted

**What already holds** (pass 1): no tier holds `UPDATE` or `DELETE` on `audit.entry`; a trigger raises on both; the only insert path is `audit.record()`, `SECURITY DEFINER`, owned by `caros_policy`; partitions carry no direct privileges; the application role cannot bypass row security or own tables.

**What remains.** Three actors could still alter the log: the migration role `caros_owner`, which owns the table and could drop the trigger; an Azure administrator, who could restore an altered backup over the live server; and a holder of the break-glass database credential. Each is a CAROS insider or a compromised CAROS credential, and the log is precisely the evidence a school would need against that insider. So **the hash chain is on** (pass 1 open decision 13), and it is anchored outside the database:

1. **Chain.** Pass 1's `audit.chain()` trigger: per school, `entry_hash = sha256(prev_hash ‖ id ‖ occurred_at ‖ action ‖ actor ‖ object ‖ subject ‖ detail)`.
2. **Anchor.** A weekly job writes, per school, `{school_id, as_of, entry_count, head_hash}` to a blob in a container with a **locked time-based retention policy** of ten years in UAE North. Under such a policy "objects can be created and read, but not modified or deleted", a locked policy "can't be deleted" and its retention can be extended but not decreased, and immutability holds "not even [for] users with account administrative privileges" ([Azure immutable storage overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/immutable-storage-overview)). The same head hash is printed in the school's monthly report, so the school holds a copy CAROS cannot reach.
3. **Verification.** Weekly, the job recomputes each school's chain from the last anchor and compares. A mismatch is a P1 incident (§9.6) and the school is told the same day.
4. **DDL and role changes are logged outside the database.** `pgaudit` is supported on Azure Database for PostgreSQL flexible server and can log by statement class; `pgaudit.log` is set to `DDL, ROLE` so that dropping a trigger, altering a policy or granting a privilege lands in Azure Monitor Logs, in a workspace the application identity cannot write to ([Azure, audit logging with pgaudit](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/postgresql/security/security-audit)). `READ` and `WRITE` classes are deliberately **not** enabled: they would copy row contents, including personal data, into a log store with its own retention.
5. **Migrations** run under a dedicated deploy identity, from reviewed SQL, with pass 1's nightly drift check; a migration that touches `audit.*` requires two approvals in `CODEOWNERS`.

Is it warranted? The log is the record a safeguarding review, an ADEK inspection, a family dispute or a court will ask for. The cost is one digest per row and one small blob per week. A school's IT review will ask whether the vendor's own administrator can edit the log; the answer becomes "not without breaking a chain whose head you already hold". Yes.

### 4.3 Who may read the audit log

| Reader | What | How |
|---|---|---|
| The owning counselor | Every entry about a student in their caseload: their own actions and everyone else's reads, including cover, the lead, the safeguarding lead and CAROS support | The file's "who has looked at this file" panel (the prototype's access audit, made real); step-up not required, because transparency to the owner is the point |
| `caseload_lead`, `school_admin` | The school's whole log, filtered by actor, student, action, period | The audit screen; step-up required; every query writes `AUDIT_ACCESS`, and the screen shows that entry too, so reading the log is visibly an act |
| `safeguarding_lead` | Entries on escalations and on the cases they were given through an escalation | Same screen, `ESC` scope |
| Student, parent | Not the raw log. The subject-access extract (§7.4) includes an access history: **date and role of each reader**, not staff names, following the PDPL's access right to the categories of recipients and Wadeema Article 44's bar on revealing a reporter | Generated by the SAR job; reviewed by the school before release |
| CAROS support | Under a grant, `AUDIT_ACCESS` logged | The support console |
| CAROS security monitoring | Aggregates over metadata only (action counts by actor, off-hours reads, bulk reads, reads outside caseload under break-glass) run by the worker as `system`, emitting alerts with identifiers only | No human reads an entry without a grant |

### 4.4 The audit log's retention, and erasure

**Proposal for counsel:** audit entries are kept for **seven years after the subject student leaves the school** (entries with no student subject: seven years from `occurred_at`); entries about an escalation follow the `safeguarding` class if that is longer. Rationale: ADEK requires the school to keep student records at least five years after leaving (Records Policy 2.1.3); disputes about access to a child's welfare record can arise years later; seven years is the common commercial limitation horizon. A shorter number leaves the school unable to answer "who read my child's file in Grade 10" when the child is 19.

**Erasure** (§7.3) tombstones the person and scrubs the PII columns of every registered table; `audit.entry` is not in the scrub list because it holds no PII. After erasure the log still says "actor X opened the file of subject `4f1c…` on 12 November for `case_review`", and `4f1c…` resolves to a tombstone: the evidential fact survives, the identity does not. The event stream (`events.event`) follows the same rule.

**At offboarding** the school receives its full log with chain and anchors in the exit pack (§7.5). CAROS retains its own copy, with all persons tombstoned, for the same seven years as the processor's record of what it did (a legitimate-interest position for counsel: question C9).

## 5. Safeguarding escalation, end to end

### 5.1 What the escalation is, and what it is not

Under ADEK's Student Protection Policy every member of staff is a mandated reporter: concerns "should be notified to the CPC" (2.2), staff report to the CPC "immediately" (2.5), the institution must report to the ADEK Child Protection Unit "within 24 hours upon suspicion" (2.1), and if the CPC cannot be reached, or it is not in the student's best interest to inform them, staff report directly to the ADEK CPU or the Family Care Authority through the Safety Concern Portal (2.6). Under Wadeema's Law the duty to notify is personal to the educator (Article 42(2)) and its breach is criminal (Article 60).

**CAROS's escalation is the 2.2/2.5 step**: a counselor notifying the school's Child Protection Coordinator (ACS's Lead Child Protection Officer) through a channel that records that it happened, when, to whom, and whether it was picked up. It is **not** the report to ADEK (that is the CPC's act, and CAROS records its outcome), and it does **not** discharge the counselor's own legal duty (if nobody acknowledges, the counselor still reports). The screen says both in one sentence, and it shows the school's own contacts from `core.school.safeguarding_contacts` (jsonb): the ADEK policy lists Abu Dhabi Police 999, the Family Care Authority 800444, the MoE Child Protection Unit hotline 80085 and the Safety Concern Portal ([ADEK Student Protection Policy](https://www.gemsaa-abudhabi.com/en/-/media/project/gems/gaa_gems_american_academy_abu_dhabi/_files-and-documents/adek_s_student-protection-policy.pdf), Figure 1). **CAROS never contacts an authority.** The `urgent` tier's SLA text, "Route now under school policy", is the engine's description of a tier; the escalation is the counselor's act, and the engine never performs it (pass 3 C7).

### 5.2 Configuration: the route

`core.school.safeguarding_route` (jsonb, validated by a schema in `packages/contracts`):

```json
{
  "recipients": [
    {"membership_id": "…", "order": 1, "ack_sla_minutes": 120},
    {"membership_id": "…", "order": 2, "ack_sla_minutes": 120}
  ],
  "oversight": [{"membership_id": "…"}],
  "clock": "school_hours" | "continuous",
  "unacknowledged_alert_minutes": 1440
}
```

Rules: every recipient and oversight member must hold an active membership with the `safeguarding_lead` capability (ACS's CPO may be a principal or a designated teacher, so the capability sits on a `counselor` membership that carries nothing else; §3.6); the route must always name at least two recipients, because ADEK 2.4.2 requires "a delegate ... always be nominated in case the CPC is unavailable"; oversight members (for example the HS Principal) receive only the fact that a referral is unacknowledged, never its content, unless they are also recipients. Changing the route requires step-up by a `school_admin` and co-signature by a current `safeguarding_lead`, is audited `ROUTE_CHANGED`, and the previous route is kept (`core.school_route_history`). The default clock is `continuous`: safeguarding does not keep office hours, and the recipient can see the referral from a phone; a school may choose `school_hours` if its CPO is not reachable at night, in which case the screen tells the counselor so and shows the direct-report contacts more prominently.

### 5.3 The act, the email, and the acknowledgement

States on `signal.escalation.status`: `raised → notified → acknowledged → closed`, with side states `reminded`, `routed_to_deputy`, `routed_to_oversight`, `unacknowledged_24h`, each an event and an audit entry.

1. **Raise.** From an open case (C15), or from a student's file with no open case (a manual case is opened first, C2, so the escalation always hangs off a case). Step-up is required. The form replaces the prototype's single twelve-character box (pass 1 open decision 9, resolved: structured, forty characters) with fields stored on `signal.escalation`: `observed` (≥ 40 characters: what was seen or heard), `why_now` (≥ 20), `who_else_knows` (staff picked from the directory, plus a free field), `immediate_risk` (`yes | no | unsure`), `student_aware` (`yes | no`), `parents_informed` (`yes | no | not_appropriate`). The minimums are ACS's to adjust (question 60). Every field is `safeguarding` class.
2. **Preview.** The screen shows two boxes: *what the recipient's email will say* (the template rendered with real values) and *what the recipient will see after signing in* (the case, its evidence, its notes, the student's file). Nothing is "attached"; the case is shared by the `ESC` scope for the life of the escalation plus 90 days.
3. **Commit.** One transaction: the escalation row (`UNIQUE (school_id, case_id)` is the no-double-fire rule); the case moves to tier `urgent` and stage `act` (C15); an outbox row per recipient of order 1; `ESCALATED_SAFEGUARDING` with `detail = {escalation_id, case_id, recipient_membership_ids}`; the event `case.escalated`. The reason is on the escalation row, not in the audit entry (§0.2).
4. **The email.** Template `safeguarding_escalation`, versioned in `notify.template`, whose `variables` allowlist is exactly `{school_short_name, reference, raised_by_name, raised_at_local, url, counselor_internal_contact}` and whose rendering refuses any other variable. Rendered:

   > Subject: [ACS Abu Dhabi] CAROS: a safeguarding referral needs your acknowledgement
   >
   > A counselor has raised a safeguarding referral in CAROS.
   > Reference: ESC-7F3K-2Q
   > Raised by: Reem Haddad, at 10:42 today (Abu Dhabi time)
   > Please sign in to read and acknowledge it: https://app.caros.example/s/ESC-7F3K-2Q
   > If you cannot reach CAROS, contact Reem Haddad on the school's internal line (ext. 214).
   > This message deliberately contains no information about the student.

   **Never in the email:** the student's name, initials, year group or id; the tier; the case headline; any reason field; any evidence; the case id (the reference is a random, non-sequential token that resolves only after sign-in, so nothing about volume or identity leaks); any deep link that renders content without authentication. Plain text, no images, no tracking. Sent from CAROS's own domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC `p=reject`, §9.5) with the school's short name in the display name only. The prototype's "Student: name · year; Case: headline; Attached: evidence and the full case history" is not ported (§0.2).
5. **In-app** notification to the same recipients, and to the counselor's own screen a status line ("sent 10:42 · not yet acknowledged").
6. **Acknowledge.** The recipient signs in with Google, passes step-up, opens the referral, sees the case under `ESC` scope, and acknowledges. `acknowledged_at`, `acknowledged_by_person_id`, `ESCALATION_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `escalation.acknowledged`; the counselor is told immediately. Acknowledgement means "I have it", not "I agree"; the recipient may add a note (`safeguarding` class) the counselor does not see.
7. **Reminders and routing.** With the default SLA of 120 minutes: at 60 minutes, a second email and in-app notice to the same recipient; at 120 minutes, the order-2 recipient is notified the same way (`routed_to_deputy`) and the counselor is told; at 240 minutes, the oversight members receive "a safeguarding referral raised at 10:42 has not been acknowledged" (`routed_to_oversight`). At 24 hours (`unacknowledged_alert_minutes`), `unacknowledged_24h`: the counselor's screen shows a red, non-dismissible banner: "Not acknowledged within 24 hours. Under the school's policy (ADEK Student Protection 2.6) you may report directly to the ADEK Child Protection Unit (80085) or the Family Care Authority (800444). Record what you did." A "I reported directly" action writes `direct_report_recorded` with the authority and the time (`DIRECT_REPORT_RECORDED`; no detail). **CAROS never reports on anyone's behalf.**
8. **Outcome.** The acknowledging recipient closes the referral with `outcome_kind` from a vocabulary aligned to ADEK's flows: `reported_to_adek_cpu`, `reported_to_fca`, `reported_to_police`, `handed_to_external_agency`, `managed_in_school`, `no_further_action`, `duplicate_of_existing_referral`; plus `reported_at`, `authority_reference` and `outcome_note` (all `safeguarding` class). From `raised_at` the screen shows the ADEK 24-hour expectation as a countdown labelled "the school's reporting window under ADEK policy", because the CPC's clock runs from suspicion and this is the earliest CAROS knows of it. An escalation with no outcome at 24 hours reminds the recipient and the oversight members.
9. **After closing.** `ESC` scope persists for 90 days; the case follows its normal lifecycle; the counselor's later notes on the case stay `case_note` class but remain visible to the recipient during that window. A second escalation on the same case is impossible; the screen says so and offers a note or a direct call (the prototype's copy, kept). A new case for the same student (relapse, C19) may be escalated afresh.

### 5.4 The content rule as a test

`packages/notify/test/templates.spec.ts` renders every template with a fixture whose variables are all present, and asserts, for `safeguarding_escalation`: the output contains the reference and the URL; it contains none of the fixture student's given name, family name, initials, year group, tier word, headline, `observed` or `why_now` text, evidence text, or case id. A template that declares a variable outside its allowlist fails `notify.template`'s CHECK. The same discipline applies to every other template: `magic_link` and `parent_activation` carry no child's name; `meeting_confirmation` carries the date, time, the counselor's name and the child's first name only; `student_deadline_nudge` (to the student's own school address) may name the institution and the date and nothing about standing; `escalation_reminder` is the escalation template again. AI parent-email drafts are in-app messages (pass 1 5.3) and never enter the outbox.

### 5.5 Channels

Email goes through Azure Communication Services Email with data location United Arab Emirates (pass 1 DR-1); its payload is identifiers-only by the rule above, so Microsoft's note that content "may transit or be processed in other geographies" costs nothing. **SMS to the recipient is not available in v1:** Azure Communication Services lists the United Arab Emirates for toll-free inbound calling only, with no SMS send or receive capability ([Microsoft, phone number management for the UAE](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/communication-services/concepts/numbers/phone-number-management-for-united-arab-emirates)). Because the reminder payload is a reference and a sentence with no personal data, a third-party SMS provider with UAE coverage would be acceptable under the identifiers-only rule; that is open decision 8. Azure Monitor action groups do support SMS and voice to +971 ([Microsoft, action groups](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/alerts/action-groups)) and are the right channel for pass 3's operational on-call alerts (§9.6), not for referrals.

### 5.6 Who sees what about an escalation

Governed by ADEK 3.2 ("shared only with authorized individuals") and Wadeema Article 44 (the reporter's identity). The raising counselor sees the status, acknowledgement and outcome vocabulary of their own referral, never the recipient's notes. Recipients in the route see everything while active and for 90 days after outcome. The `caseload_lead` sees that a referral exists, its status and its owner, not its content. Support never sees it without the special flag. Students and parents never see it, and a subject-access extract excludes `safeguarding`-class rows unless the school, as controller, decides to disclose (question C8). A teacher whose flag fed the case is identified to the recipient inside CAROS and never in any family-facing output; SAR packs redact flag authorship by default. Nothing in an escalation is ever exportable except by a recipient, under step-up, for a statutory referral, watermarked.

### 5.7 Student disclosures and the student's own reporting channel

Pass 5 designs detection; this pass sets the route. A disclosure flagged by the safety screen on a discovery message, a reflection, a Personal Statement Lab draft or a mentor message writes `safety_flag` (pass 1) and creates an in-app alert to the student's owning counselor (and an identifiers-only email: "a message from a student needs your attention"), carrying the `urgent` tier's clock; if the alert is not opened within two school hours, it routes to the safeguarding route's first recipient exactly as an unacknowledged referral would. The student sees a fixed, school-approved message with the counselor's contact and the helplines the school lists, written by the school, never by a model. Separately, every student screen carries a "I need to talk to someone" action that creates a `signal.student_reflection` marked `urgent_request` with the same routing; that is the Child Digital Safety Law's "reporting channel" made concrete. Mentor messages with grooming indicators route to the `mentor_coordinator` and the first recipient of the safeguarding route (§2.3).

## 6. AI and residency: the rules

Pass 5 designs the mechanism. This section owns the rule: what may leave the UAE, in what form, to whom, under what retention, and what may never leave. The facts it stands on are in §1.6.

### 6.1 The position

**A model call is a cross-border transfer.** Nothing is stored abroad under the arrangement in §1.6, but the prompt is processed in the United States, and processing is a transfer under the PDPL's Articles 22 and 23. The only paths those articles offer without an adequacy list are contractual safeguards, express consent, or necessity. The plan takes **contractual safeguards** as the primary basis (Anthropic's DPA with its EU standard contractual clauses, the closest thing available to the PDPL's undefined "standard contract clauses", on top of the school's documented instruction and the notices in §1.5), and treats express consent as the school's alternative if its DPO prefers it (open decision 4). Counsel is asked whether this reading survives the Executive Regulations when they arrive (question C3).

**Two consequences follow, and both are structural.** First, every AI feature sits behind a per-school module flag (`core.school_module`) and a per-feature policy row (`ai.feature_policy`, §10) that the school enables in writing, so that a school, a regulator or a change in the law can turn the model off without touching anything else. Second, because the trust spine already says rules decide and the model only writes, **CAROS with the AI switched off is still CAROS**: the engine, the queue, the evidence chain, the file, the teacher loop, the IB module and the reporting all run without a model. The pilot's shadow phase can and should run with the AI features off (open decision 5).

### 6.5 The fairness audit's legal basis

Pass 3 §12.4 needs nationality, gender, language background and SEN status held apart from everything else, for one purpose. All four are sensitive under the PDPL's Article 1 (racial origin and health, with nationality treated as sensitive by policy). No PDPL article names bias testing as a purpose, and the Executive Regulations that might are not issued. The recommendation for counsel (question C4): the school processes the labels as controller under its own obligation to protect students from discriminatory treatment (the Student Protection Policy's definition of maltreatment reaches "other types of risks that impact their overall health and development, wellbeing, and safety", and an unfair alerting tool is such a risk), with explicit custodian consent as the fallback basis per label; CAROS processes them on instruction under the EU AI Act Article 10(5) pattern adopted contractually: purpose limited to bias detection and correction, access limited to the audit job's role (no application tier and not the engine, pass 3 D26), pseudonymised join with small-cell suppression, aggregate output only, deletion of the working table after each run and of the labels within a year or when the school withdraws them, and documentation of every run. The school may supply none, in which case the plan states that fairness by nationality and SEN is untested (pass 3 §12.4).

### 6.6 Measuring a minor's platform activity: the engagement rule

Pass 3 designed the engagement domain and shipped it off; pass 2 made Google Classroom a connector this pass must approve. **Rule:** the engagement domain may be enabled for a school only when all of the following hold: (1) the school enables it in writing (`ai.feature_policy` is the wrong table; it is `core.school_module 'engagement'` with `enabled_by`, `enabled_at`, `notice_version`) and the student and parent notices describe it in plain words ("CAROS notices when a student who usually uses the portal or turns in Classroom work stops, and tells the counselor"); (2) sources are limited to CAROS session events and Google Classroom submission states and timestamps, read through a school-created reader account with read-only scopes under domain-wide delegation (pass 2 §7.6), with no content, comments, attachments or announcement text; (3) the engine sees dates, not times: `engagement.activity_event` gains `occurred_on date` and the engine reads only that column (pass 3 already removed the night measure; this makes its return impossible without a schema change); (4) retention is 180 days rolling (pass 1); (5) a student can see their own activity summary in the portal, so the measurement is visible to the measured; (6) the DPA states that engagement signals are never used for discipline; (7) the domain's banner (pass 3 §10.3) shows on the caseload sheet whenever it is on, naming the sources. If ACS declines (question 14), the domain stays off with the visible banner and nothing else changes.

## 7. Retention and erasure

Every number in this section is a **proposal for counsel** and is written into `privacy.retention_class.proposed_days` (or `proposed_rule` where the trigger is a birthday) so that counsel's decision is a configuration change with an audit entry, not a migration. The governing idea: **CAROS is not the school's system of record for anything ADEK requires the school to keep.** Enrolment, grades, attendance, behaviour and graduation live in Veracross and are the school's five-year (or indefinite) records (ADEK Records Policy 2.1.3). Retention for copies follows the product's need; retention for CAROS-native welfare records follows the record's value to the child and the school's obligations.

### 7.2 What the law may require keeping, as far as this pass can find

- **ADEK Records Policy 2.1.3:** the school keeps enrolment, academic, attendance and disciplinary records at least five years after leaving and graduation records indefinitely. These are the school's records; CAROS's copies may be shorter as long as the school's system of record holds them (an export before deletion is always available).
- **Child protection cases** are a required record category in the same policy; no ADEK minimum beyond the general five years was found. The Student Protection Policy requires confidentiality of "open and closed cases" but sets no retention.
- **The PDPL** requires retention no longer than necessary and sets no minimums (Article 7 principles per DLA Piper).
- **Limitation periods** for civil claims under the new Civil Transactions Law, and any specific rule for claims by a person who was a minor, are counsel's question (C10); they may lengthen `welfare` and `safeguarding`.
- **CAROS's own commercial records** (invoices, contracts) follow UAE commercial and tax law and hold no student data.

### 7.3 Erasure, with an append-only audit log, immutable raw files and backups

**Who decides.** The school, as controller. Requests arrive at the school directly or through a portal form ("ask the school to correct or delete my data") that notifies the school's data protection lead; CAROS never decides to erase. `privacy.erasure_request` (pass 1) records the request, the identity verification (a portal session, or the school's own check), the school's decision and its legal basis or refusal reason. Grounds for refusal the screen offers: an open case or escalation; a retention the school is obliged to keep; a legal claim; the student is still enrolled (erasure of a current student's record would defeat the school's duty of care; rectification and restriction remain available).

**Grace.** An accepted request is scheduled 30 days out (`scheduled_for`), so an objection or a change of mind can stop it, and so that the point-in-time backup window begins to roll past the request.

**Execution** is one job, one transaction per table, all inside `withTenant()` as `system`, logged per table in `privacy.erasure_log`:

1. `core.person`: `given_name`, `family_name`, `preferred_name` set to the literal `[erased]`; `email`, `phone`, `google_subject`, `language_pref` set to NULL; `status = 'erased'`, `erased_at`. `core.person_name_history` (pass 2) deleted. The uuid stays.
2. For every table in `privacy.table_registry` whose subject or person column matches: scrub `pii_columns`, delete rows whose class says `delete`, anonymise rows whose class says `anonymise` (free-text columns set to NULL; structural columns kept). Cases, evidence snapshots (`snapshot` jsonb text fields), notes, teacher flag bodies, student voice, messages and generations lose their text; tiers, dates, rule keys and identifiers remain for aggregate reporting.
3. `doc.file` rows for the subject: blob soft-deleted, versions deleted, hard purge after the 30-day soft-delete window; the row keeps `sha256` and `byte_size` for the destruction statement and loses `original_filename`.
4. `ai.pseudonym_map` rows for the subject's generations deleted; `ai.generation.output_text` set to NULL.
5. `sis.external_identity` rows deleted, and a salted hash of each `(system, external_id)` written to `privacy.erased_external_identity` (§10) so that a later historical import cannot re-create the person: pass 2's identity ladder checks this table at rung 1 and refuses with `erased_subject`.
6. `audit.entry` and `events.event`: **untouched.** They hold uuids only; after step 1 the uuid resolves to a tombstone.
7. `ERASURE_EXECUTED`, and a destruction statement (tables, row counts, blob count, executed at) delivered to the school in-app for its own records (ADEK Records 1.4.2.b asks schools for "proof of evidence of destruction").

**Backups.** The point-in-time store holds the data for up to 35 days after execution; erasure is therefore complete in live systems immediately and in the operational backups within 35 days, which the DPA states. Long-term vaulted copies hold it for their retention (§7.1). Two rules make that acceptable: vaulted backups are encrypted, offline, and restorable only to a scratch server in a drill or an incident (pass 1 DR-9); and **every restore replays `privacy.erasure_log`**: the restore runbook's last step re-executes every erasure request whose `executed_at` is later than the backup's timestamp before the restored server is used for anything. That step is scripted, tested in the quarterly drill (pass 1 drill 3 gains an erased fixture person), and its omission blocks the drill's sign-off.

**Immutable raw imports.** A raw export in the WORM container cannot be edited or deleted before its 90-day retention expires (pass 1 DR-9; [Azure immutable storage](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/immutable-storage-overview)). Erasure of one student's rows inside such a file is impossible before expiry. Position: the retention is short (90 days) precisely so that this is tolerable; the DPA states that raw export files are retained unaltered for up to 90 days; and the raw-import container of each tenant uses a **per-tenant encryption scope with a customer-managed key** (§9.2) so that at offboarding the whole container becomes unreadable at once by destroying the key, which is the only way to "erase" WORM content early (Azure: if the key protecting an encryption scope is revoked, "the data will become inaccessible"; [encryption scopes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/encryption-scope-overview)). Pass 2's staged rows follow their 30-day class and are ordinary rows, scrubbed normally.

**Logs.** Application and security logs hold identifiers only (§9.4), so erasure does not reach them and does not need to. **Email.** The outbox holds `recipient_address` snapshots; those are `pii_columns` and are scrubbed. **The AI provider** holds nothing at rest under ZDR; content retained by its trust-and-safety process is pseudonymous and outside CAROS's reach, which the notices say.

### 7.4 Subject access requests, and the other rights

**Route.** The school is the controller and receives the request (from a parent for a child, or from a student, who at 18 requests for themselves). The portal offers a form that files a `privacy.sar_request` (§10) and notifies the school's data protection lead; requests made to the school directly are entered by the `caseload_lead` or `school_admin`. CAROS's service level to the school: the pack is available within **five working days**, leaving the school three weeks of the PDPL's one calendar month for review (DLA Piper's reading of the response period).

**The pack.** For one student: a machine-readable JSON (the PDPL's portability right, Article 14) and a readable PDF, generated by the worker from `privacy.table_registry` so no table can be forgotten, organised by data class with the class explained in plain words, and including: every record where the student is subject; the sources (which import, which system, when); the AI generations about the student (feature, date, output text) and, for each, the list of record types used (the prompts themselves are not stored); the notices the family acknowledged; and the **access history**: date and role of every reader of the student's record (§4.3), without staff names.

**The redaction step.** The pack is not released by CAROS. The school's `caseload_lead` reviews it in a screen that highlights third-party data (a teacher's authorship of a flag, another student's name in free text, the other parent's contact details, a mentor's identity) and professional records (counselor notes, engine evaluations), and decides per item to include or redact, choosing a reason from `config.vocabulary 'sar_redaction_reason'` (`third_party_data`, `safeguarding_confidentiality`, `legal_privilege`, `professional_opinion_policy`, `other_child`). Defaults: teacher authorship redacted (Wadeema Article 44); `safeguarding` rows excluded (ADEK 3.2; question C8); counselor notes **included**, because in CAROS they are shared records about the child (the FERPA "sole possession" exclusion does not apply, §1.2) and because the PDPL's access right has no professional-opinion exemption that this pass could find (question C7 asks counsel); engine evaluations included with the pass 3 templates' plain-language summaries, not raw features.

**Delivery.** Inside the portal to the requesting guardian's or student's session, never by email; watermarked with the requester and time; downloadable for 30 days; `SAR_DELIVERED`; the generated pack is deleted after 30 days.

**Rectification.** Facts imported from the SIS are corrected in the SIS and re-imported (pass 2's supersession); CAROS-native records are corrected by a superseding row (pass 1's `supersedes_note_id`), never edited in place. **Restriction and objection.** `privacy.processing_restriction` (§10): a row per person and class; while a `signal` restriction is active the engine skips the student (pass 3 gains the hook: `sis.student.processing_restricted_domains text[]`), the case is frozen with a visible banner "processing restricted at the family's request, under review", the co-pilot excludes the student, and exports exclude them; the school lifts it or turns it into an erasure. **Automated decisions** (PDPL Article 18): the engine proposes a tier and a person decides (invariant 3, pass 3); the notice says so, and the file's "why" panel is the human-readable explanation the right asks for.

### 7.5 Offboarding a school

Thirty days before the end date the school receives its exit pack: every student's SAR-format pack, every table as JSON Lines, every document, the audit log with its chain and anchors, the configuration and rule-set history, and the erasure log. During those thirty days the tenant is read-only. On the end date: rows deleted, blobs purged, the tenant's **encryption scope key destroyed** in Key Vault (which renders the raw-import and document containers unreadable even inside the soft-delete windows), `TENANT_OFFBOARDED`, and a destruction certificate with counts and the key-destruction record delivered to the school. Backups age out (35 days operational; the vaulted copies per §7.1), and the restore runbook replays offboarding for that tenant on any restore. CAROS retains the tombstoned audit copy under §4.4.

## 8. Threat model

Method: STRIDE over the trust boundaries (Microsoft's six categories: spoofing, tampering, repudiation, information disclosure, denial of service, elevation of privilege; [Microsoft threat modeling](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/security/develop/threat-modeling-tool-threats)), with the OWASP Top 10:2025 and the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 as checklists. Boundaries: browser to Application Gateway; web and worker to PostgreSQL (the tenant context); worker to external systems (Veracross, ManageBac, Classroom, the AI provider, email); school to CAROS (file uploads, SFTP); CAROS operators to production (support console, Azure); the build pipeline to the registry. The asset is a minor's welfare record under a real school's name; the adversaries are an external attacker, a compromised or curious member of school staff, a malicious parent, student or mentor, a CAROS insider, a compromised dependency, and an AI provider that is honest but retains. ## 9. The platform baseline

### 9.1 Transport

TLS 1.2 minimum, TLS 1.3 preferred, at Application Gateway WAF v2 in UAE North, with end-to-end TLS to Container Apps (pass 1 chose the regional gateway over Front Door precisely so that no request is decrypted outside the country). HSTS with a one-year max-age and preload; CAA records naming the certificate authority; certificates issued and renewed through Key Vault. PostgreSQL connections require TLS (`require_secure_transport = on`, certificate verification in the driver); Blob and Key Vault are HTTPS only; every outbound connector call verifies certificates and pins the host allowlist (SSRF control: a connector may reach only the hosts in `ingest.source_system.config.allowed_hosts`).

### 9.2 Encryption at rest and key management

Azure encrypts everything at rest by default with service-managed keys. **Decision: customer-managed keys (CMK) in Azure Key Vault (Premium, HSM-backed) for production**, chosen at creation because "You can select the mode only at server creation time. You can't change the mode from one to another for the lifetime of the server" ([Azure Database for PostgreSQL, data encryption](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/postgresql/security/security-data-encryption)); pass 6 must place this in week one. What CMK buys: the school can be told that CAROS holds the key to its own data and can destroy it; separation of duties between the people who run the database and the people who hold keys; and a crypto-shredding lever ("You can remove a key to make a database inaccessible", same page). What it costs: operational care. The requirements from the same page are adopted as configuration: Key Vault soft delete with 90-day retention and purge protection; the RBAC permission model with the server's user-assigned managed identity holding *Key Vault Crypto Service Encryption User*; public access disabled with the trusted-services bypass; automatic key-version updates through a versionless key URI; a resource lock on the vault; a key backup escrowed; and, because geo-redundant backup is on, a second key in a Key Vault in UAE Central with its own identity ("create the geo-redundant backup encryption key in a Key Vault instance that must exist in the region where the geo-redundant backup is stored"). A server that loses its key becomes `Inaccessible` within about an hour, so Resource Health and Activity Log alerts on key access failures page the on-call.

Storage: account-level CMK for every storage account, and **per-tenant encryption scopes** on the raw-import and document containers, each scope protected by a customer-managed key with automatic version updates (up to 10,000 auto-updating scopes per account; a scope cannot be deleted but can be disabled, and revoking its key makes the data inaccessible; [Azure encryption scopes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/encryption-scope-overview)). This is what makes offboarding a key destruction (§7.5) and what stops a misconfigured SAS from being readable across tenants (T1).

Application-level encryption, AES-256-GCM with a per-tenant data key wrapped by the tenant's Key Vault key: `ai.pseudonym_map.mapping_enc`, passkey credential metadata and TOTP seeds, activation-code and magic-link hashes are already hashes and need no encryption. Rotation: Key Vault rotation policy rotates the CMK yearly, with automatic version pickup; per-tenant data keys rotate yearly by re-wrapping; TLS certificates rotate automatically. Every rotation is an entry in the ops log and in the school's monthly report.

### 9.3 Secrets and credentials

No secret in code, in an environment variable, or in the repository: managed identities for every Azure-to-Azure call, Key Vault references for everything else, and GitHub Actions authenticating to Azure through OIDC federated credentials so that no long-lived deployment secret exists. Third-party credentials live in Key Vault, per tenant where they are per tenant, referenced from `ingest.source_system.config.secret_ref` (pass 1: "never secrets; those live in the key vault by reference"), and rotate on a schedule that is also a checklist item at offboarding: Veracross OAuth client credentials (tokens live one hour; the client secret is rotated yearly and when the school's `OAuth_App_Admin` changes); ManageBac read-only tokens (rotated each school year, revoked in ManageBac at offboarding); the Google service-account key for domain-wide delegation (rotated every 90 days, restricted to the delegated scopes); the Anthropic API key (per environment, rotated quarterly, with `allowed_inference_geos` enforced at the workspace); SFTP local-user SSH keys (per tenant, rotated yearly). Secret scanning runs in CI (gitleaks) and GitHub push protection is on. A leaked credential is a P2 incident (§9.6) and the rotation runbook is the first response.

### 9.4 Logs free of personal data

Structured logging (pino) through one serializer that emits only allowlisted fields: request id, session id hash, person uuid, school uuid, acting role, route, status, latency, error code, and Zod error *paths* (never values). Bodies, query strings, headers, email addresses and names never reach a log line; a lint rule forbids passing an object to the logger outside the serializer, and a CI job runs the application against both synthetic tenants and greps the captured logs for every name and email in the seed. Log Analytics retention: 90 days for application logs, one year for security logs (sign-in, grant, key access, DDL). `pgaudit` logs `DDL` and `ROLE` only (§4.2). Application Gateway WAF logs contain client IP addresses, which are personal data in some readings; they are kept 30 days. The AI gateway logs `generation_id`, model, token counts and `inference_geo`, never prompt or output text.

### 9.5 Email authentication

The sending domain is CAROS's own, with SPF, 2048-bit DKIM and DMARC at `p=reject` with aggregate reporting, configured on the Azure Communication Services custom domain (pass 1). The display name carries the school's short name; the address never impersonates the school's domain. Every message is plain text and identifiers-only (§5.4), which is also what lets staff be told: "an email that names a student is not from CAROS".

### 9.6 Incident response and breach notification

**Severities.** P1: confirmed exposure of student data, an audit-chain mismatch, or a safeguarding referral that could not be delivered. P2: suspected exposure, a leaked credential, a vulnerability with a plausible path to data. P3: a vulnerability without exposure. P4: availability.

**Process.** Detect (alerts in §9.4 and pass 3 §13.8; a monitored security mailbox and vulnerability disclosure policy) → contain (revoke sessions, rotate the affected credential, disable the feature or the connector, isolate the tenant if needed) → assess (which schools, which students, which classes; a scratch restore for forensics if the live database cannot be trusted) → **notify the school within 24 hours of becoming aware** (the DPA clock; Anthropic gives CAROS 48 hours, so CAROS's own clock to the school is tighter than its supplier's), with what is known, what is not, and what has been done, then every 24 hours → the school decides and acts as controller: notification to the Data Office "immediately" under PDPL Article 9 (the procedure the Executive Regulations will define), notification to ADEK under the Digital Policy (6.6 requires a protocol for reporting incidents to ADEK and bars communication to anyone but the provider and ADEK), and notification to families where the school judges the risk warrants it; CAROS provides the evidence pack (affected records by class, the audit entries, the timeline) and drafts nothing to families itself → recover → a written post-incident review within ten working days, shared with the affected schools → fixes tracked to closure. **Exercises:** a tabletop with the pilot school's IT and safeguarding lead before the first real record, and yearly after.

**On-call.** During the pilot, Davide; pass 6 sets the rota. Azure Monitor action groups deliver email, SMS and voice to +971 numbers ([Microsoft, action groups](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/alerts/action-groups)) with identifiers-only payloads, for the sweep watchdog (pass 3), key-access failures, chain mismatches, WAF anomalies and the security mailbox.

### 9.7 Backups, durability and the out-of-country question

Pass 1 DR-9 stands: 35-day point-in-time restore, geo-redundant backup to UAE Central, vaulted long-term backups, quarterly drills. This pass adds: backups are encrypted under the customer-managed keys of both regions; every restored server runs the RLS suite and the erasure replay before use (§7.3); and the vaulted yearly copies are proposed at three years (§7.1).

**Open decision 1 (pass 1): an encrypted out-of-country cold copy.** Position: **recommend it, conditionally.** The AWS UAE region's destruction in March 2026 and the loss of data that existed only there (pass 1 DR-1) is the demonstrated scenario an in-country-only design cannot survive. A nightly encrypted dump to an EU region (Sweden Central or West Europe) under a key that lives only in the UAE Managed HSM, restorable only under a declared disaster with the school's notice, would survive it. Legally, ciphertext whose key is elsewhere is still personal data under most readings, so it is an Article 23 transfer that needs the contractual basis and the school's written agreement (question 62 already asks). If a school declines, the risk is stated in its DPA and the design is unchanged. The infrastructure for the copy is small; the decision is the school's and counsel's.

### 9.8 Dependency and supply-chain hygiene

As in T14: committed pnpm lockfile; `minimumReleaseAge = 10080` for production dependencies with a documented exception path for security patches; `npm audit signatures` in CI; lifecycle scripts disabled except an allowlist; Renovate weekly; Dependabot alerts; base images and Actions pinned by digest or SHA; image scanning; GitHub artifact attestations for CAROS's own images; a CycloneDX SBOM per release stored with the image and given to schools on request; a quarterly review of the twenty most privileged transitive dependencies (anything touching crypto, auth, parsing or the database driver). OWASP Top 10:2025's A03, "Software Supply Chain Failures", is the reason this list is longer than it would have been two years ago.

### 9.9 The WAF, and when

Pass 1 open decision 3: (a) from the first deploy, (b) from the first real data, (c) after the review asks. **Decision: (b).** The synthetic-only months run without the WAF's largest-line cost; the WAF goes in front of production two weeks before the first real import, runs the OWASP Core Rule Set in detection mode for those two weeks to tune false positives against the real screens, then switches to prevention. Rate limiting and bot protection are on from day one of real data. No geographic blocking (families travel). The support console and administrative routes are additionally restricted by IP allowlist and by the Entra PIM requirement.

### 9.10 SFTP and connector hardening

If the weekly pack arrives by SFTP (pass 2 open decisions 2 and 17), it is Azure Blob's native SFTP: it requires a hierarchical-namespace account, authenticates **local users** by SSH key (passwords are Azure-generated and are not used here), scopes permissions per container, and supports home directories and ACLs; note that "SFTP is a platform level service, so port 22 will be open even if the account option is disabled" and that the account firewall applies to all endpoints ([Azure, SFTP support for Blob Storage](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/secure-file-transfer-protocol-support)). Configuration per tenant: one local user, one container, permissions `Create` and `Write` only (no `Read`, `List` or `Delete`), so a compromised school credential can neither read past files nor anyone else's; the school's egress IP allow-listed; host keys published to the school for pinning; keys rotated yearly. Whether the feature is offered in UAE North could not be confirmed from the documentation (the region list is not on the page); pass 6 verifies in the portal in week one, and the fallback is pass 2's small SFTP container. Connectors egress through a NAT gateway with a static IP so that iSAMS and Veracross allow-lists can name it (pass 2 §13), hold their credentials per tenant in Key Vault (§9.3), and may reach only allow-listed hosts (§9.1).

### 9.11 Controls a reviewer can be shown running

Every control above that can be a test is a test, and the pass 6 build makes them visible in CI: the RLS suite and the two-tenant canary; the matrix suite generated from `auth.role_permission`; the AI leak scanner and the model refusal tests; the log scanner; the template content test; pass 3's no-`fairness`-import test on `packages/engine`; the classification trigger tests; a **no-real-data test** that greps every file under `packages/seed` and every fixture for the names in ACS's published staff directory and for any string tagged `real:` in the repository, so that the synthetic tenants can never accidentally carry a real person; and pass 2's `forbidden_columns` behaving as a minimisation control the school can inspect in its mapping profile.

## Challenges

Each names the decision or the prototype behaviour it touches, states the alternative, what it costs and buys, and what this pass planned on.

**Challenge 1 · "Model choice is configuration" is kept, and Fable 5.1 is out.** *Decision touched: models.* Covered Models keep a minor's welfare text for thirty days under automated review with no zero-data-retention path except a temporary exemption (§1.6). The alternative is to accept the thirty days for the co-pilot's benefit; it costs a data class the school cannot defend to a regulator or a parent and it buys capability the other models nearly match. Planned on: exclusion by a schema flag and a refusal test (R2), with a re-evaluation when Enterprise Frontier Safeguards are documented (open decision 3).

**Challenge 2 · The escalation the prototype shows is the one thing this pass will not port.** *Invariant 5, and the frozen demo.* The modal's "what will be sent" puts the student's name, year, the headline and the file in an email. The alternative, an email that says only that a referral exists, costs the CPO one sign-in; it buys an inbox that can be forwarded, synced to a phone, or read over a shoulder without exposing a child. Planned on the reference-only email (§5.3). The demo stays frozen; the demo script must not read the old modal as a promise.

**Challenge 3 · Consent is the wrong basis for the core processing, and the school must say so.** *An assumption behind the prototype ("Contact consent · On file").* Treating a school's statutory guidance and safeguarding work as consent-based would let a withdrawal blind a counselor to a child who needs them. The alternative is what this pass recommends (§1.5): the school's contractual and legal-obligation bases, with ADEK Records 1.5.1's parental permission satisfied through the school's enrolment terms, and consent reserved for optional features. It costs the school a documented attestation before any real import (a gate this pass adds); it buys a basis that survives an angry parent. Counsel decides (question C1).

**Challenge 4 · Student sign-in depends on a school-side configuration nobody at CAROS controls.** *Decision touched: identity.* Google blocks under-18 users from unconfigured apps and puts parental consent on the school. The alternative, CAROS-issued student credentials, would cost a password system for 380 minors and the loss of the school's own MFA and deprovisioning. Planned on Google, with the configuration as an onboarding gate and a written checklist (§2.1).

**Challenge 5 · Mentor sessions are not recorded, whatever the copy says.** *Prototype behaviour changed.* A recording of a minor is a data class with retention, access and consent questions this pass cannot settle, and a feature CAROS does not have. Planned on schedule-and-messages only, with the school's own platform for calls (§2.3; open decision 9).

**Challenge 6 · The pilot should start with the AI off.** *Decision touched: AI features in v1.* Everything that makes CAROS CAROS runs without a model (§6.1). Turning the AI on before the DPA's AI annex, the notices and the school's per-feature acceptances exist would be processing minors' data abroad on an undocumented basis during the very weeks the school is deciding whether to trust the product. It costs the co-pilot in shadow mode, when there are no live cases anyway; it buys a clean first review. Recommendation, not a departure (open decision 5).

**Challenge 7 · Residency now has a price, and the school should choose.** *Decision touched: data residency* (pass 1 5.1). Position in §9.7: recommend the encrypted EU cold copy if the school agrees in writing; otherwise state the risk in the DPA.

**Challenge 8 · Cover is explicit, and break-glass is loud.** *CONTEXT §11.11.* The alternative, school-wide visibility for all counselors, is simpler and is what some teams want (question 65). Planned on caseload scope plus time-boxed cover and a self-service 24-hour break-glass that notifies the owner and the lead (§3.3), with a school override that may widen `signal` and `case_note` but never `safeguarding`.

**Challenge 9 · "Staff-only" does not mean "never shown to the family".** *CONTEXT §3 signal visibility.* Tiers, signals and notes stay off every student and parent screen, as decided. But a subject-access request is a right, not a screen, and this pass could find no PDPL exemption for professional opinion; counselor notes default to *included* in a SAR pack after the school's redaction review (§7.4). Counselors should write notes knowing that. Counsel confirms (question C7).

**Challenge 10 · The twelve-character reason becomes a form.** *Pass 1 5.5.* Planned on the structured referral with forty characters of observation (§5.3); the numbers are ACS's (question 60).

**Challenge 11 · The engagement domain has conditions, not a ban.** *CONTEXT §11.8.* Seven conditions (§6.6), the strictest of which removes time of day from the data the engine can see at the schema level. If ACS declines, the domain stays off and the banner says so.

**Challenge 12 · No ADEK residency rule was found.** *Decision touched: data residency.* The decision stands on the PDPL's transfer articles, the ICT Health Law and the school's expectation; this pass says plainly that it could not find an ADEK policy requiring in-country hosting, so that nobody later claims one exists (§1.1; question 29 stays open).
