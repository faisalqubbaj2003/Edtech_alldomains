## 0. Read this first

### 0.1 Departures from section 3 of CONTEXT.md

The first version declared none. Davide's decisions of 2026-09-24 on the pass 7 review make several, and each is declared here so that no later pass mistakes it for a silent change. Items 1 and 2 are the first version's narrowings, revised; items 3 to 9 are new.

1. **"Model choice must be configuration, never code" holds, but the configuration is bounded by a rule.** Claude Fable 5.1, Fable 5, Mythos 5 and Mythos 5.1 are Anthropic "Covered Models": prompts and completions "are retained for at least 30 days and then automatically deleted" and "assessed by automated safety systems", and zero data retention "is not available in workspaces, Claude Enterprise organizations, or third-party platforms" for them, except under a time-limited exemption while Enterprise Frontier Safeguards roll out ([Covered Models article](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15425695); [API and data retention](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)). A welfare record about a minor cannot be sent to a model that keeps it for a month on servers outside the UAE. §6 therefore forbids Covered Models for any feature that receives student-derived data, and the domain layer enforces two flags on pass 5's model registry, `ai.model` (D62; §12.9 of the pass 7 review moved them there from `ai.model_config`). Model choice stays configuration inside the set of models that satisfy the rule. Pass 5 plans on Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5 class models under a zero-data-retention organisation with inference pinned to the United States.
2. **"Parents receive no email in v1" becomes content-free transactional email** (decision C2). Parents receive magic links and role-only notices ("a counselor at the school has sent you an update in CAROS"), which name the sender's role from a fixed list and never a person, the child or the topic (F60). The parent activation code is **not** emailed: the school hands it over off-email (§2.2; the first version's §0.1 said otherwise, F77).
3. **Outbound email to staff goes beyond the two decided messages** (decision C3, F28). Kept and declared, all reference-only: the counselor's safety-alert email, escalation reminders and deputy routing to the Child Protection Officer's route, the "alert unopened" notice to the CPO, and grooming alerts to the mentor coordinator. Everything else is in-app. §5.8 lists every outbound message with its recipient, channel and content allowlist; CONTEXT §3's "Outbound notifications" row still needs updating to match (handoff in `08-changelog.md`).
4. **A second route to the Child Protection Officer exists, and it is a notice, never a referral** (F12, option b). When a student safety alert goes unopened, or falls in a category the school routes to the CPO at once (F09), the CPO receives a reference-only notice; a person then decides whether to escalate. No automatic referral is ever created (§5.7).
5. **Teachers can raise a safeguarding referral** (F10). A teacher's safeguarding flag goes straight to the route as a referral, with the escalation's content rules and invariant 5's guarantees (§5.3, §3.4). The first version gave teachers no route at all.
6. **A subject-access pack may, by the school's explicit act, include staff-only material** (decision C4, F14). CONTEXT §3 makes tiers, signals, evidence and notes staff-only. By default the pack now excludes every professional record and anything linked to an escalation or a safety alert; the school may include an item one at a time, which is the one path by which such material can reach a family (§7.4).
7. **Two staff roles join the five** (F16, F10): `school_admin` as its own role with no caseload path, and a `staff` role with no data access that carries capabilities such as `safeguarding_lead` and the new `safeguarding_oversight` for a Principal or a CPO who is neither counselor nor teacher (§2.5, §3.6). Section 3 allows "more roles later if needed"; these are rows, not a departure from the five product surfaces.
8. **The student safety screen has no model in v1** (decision C1, which settles F03). It is a lexicon; every lexicon hit reaches a person; no model reads a student's text for the screen. An add-only model layer is revisited after one term, once the CPO has seen the false-positive rate (§5.7, §6.2 R8).
9. **Two AI features leave v1** (F52, C1): the headline rephrase (the rule headline stays) and the model safety screen. The five AI features of section 3 are unchanged.

Three behaviours the prototype *shows* must not be ported to the Next.js build. They are listed in 0.2 rather than here because they are corrections to the code's promises, not departures from decisions.

### 0.4 The security model on one page

1. **The school is the controller; CAROS is its processor.** Everything CAROS does with a student's data is on the school's documented instruction, inside the UAE, under a data processing agreement whose annexes are this document's §1.4, §7.1 and §9 (§1.3).
2. **Identity is federated for people the school already knows, and bound to verified relationships for people it does not.** Staff and students sign in with the school's Google Workspace and get *no access* until an imported roster or an administrative act gives them a membership; access ends when the membership ends, which an hourly Directory read makes prompt, because Google sends no security events for Workspace accounts (F18). Parents sign in by magic link but *see* only the children a verified, activated, unrestricted SIS link connects them to, one link per SIS contact. Mentors are invited through the coordinator, vetted under the school's volunteer policy, and hold a passkey (§2).
3. **Permissions are rows, enforced by the database.** The tenant policy is restrictive, the role policies are permissive, both are generated from the matrix in §3.6 through pass 1 §2.4a, and a query with no context sees nothing. Each deployable connects with its own database identity, so the web app cannot become the worker (F01). The database now enforces the verbs a table carries too (escalate, approve, configure); the application checks them first and alone enforces `export` (§3, F02).
4. **Sensitive reads are acts.** Opening a file, a note, an escalation, a document, the audit log itself, or building a prompt writes an audit entry in the same transaction. The log is append-only, hash-chained per school, and anchored weekly to immutable storage (§4).
5. **Escalation is a workflow with a clock, and the email is a doorbell.** The email says that a referral exists and which role raised it, never a name. Everything else is behind sign-in and step-up. Unacknowledged referrals climb a configured route. The duty to report to the authority within 24 hours of suspicion belongs to **each member of staff** who suspects, not to the institution (ADEK Student Protection Policy v1.1, 3.1; F09), so the reporter's own countdown and the direct-report route are shown from the moment of escalation, never assumed to be done (§5).
6. **What leaves the country is pseudonymised, minimised, not retained, and never from the excluded columns.** Only zero-data-retention models with US-pinned inference; no Covered Models; no safeguarding, health, nationality, fairness or audit **column** ever. Free text is different, and the notices say so (F20): a student's reflection or a parent's message can carry any class, a disclosure or a diagnosis included, and pseudonymisation removes names, not facts. No model reads a student's words for the safety screen (C1). ADEK's explicit consent comes before any AI transfer abroad (Digital Policy 7.1.3.a, F04), and a per-school switch turns the AI features off with nothing else lost (§6).
7. **Retention is a schedule, erasure is a procedure, and both survive backups** because the erasure log is replayed on every restore and raw files sit under a per-school key (§7).
8. **The baseline is boring on purpose:** TLS everywhere, customer-managed keys, managed identities, no secrets in code, logs without names, dependency cooldowns, signed provenance, quarterly restore drills, a pen test before the first real record, and an incident process the school's IT can read (§9).

## 1. The regulatory map

### 1.1 What applies by law

**UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the Protection of Personal Data (the PDPL).** In force since 2 January 2022 ([u.ae, data protection laws](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/data/data-protection-laws), page updated 4 December 2025). The facts that matter on 2026-09-23:

- **The Executive Regulations have still not been issued.** The Chambers *Data Protection & Privacy 2026* UAE chapter (BSA Law and Bizilance, 10 March 2026) states that "The Implementing Regulations, intended to clarify key aspects of the law, have yet to be issued" and that "the Data Office has not yet become fully operational", with the TDRA giving administrative support during a transitional period ([Chambers](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/uae/trends-and-developments)). DLA Piper's handbook (updated 27 January 2025) adds that organisations will have six months after issuance to comply, and that no adequacy decisions and no penalty schedule exist yet ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)). **Consequence:** the obligations below exist in the law, but the breach-notification timeline, the penalties, the adequacy list and the detail of consent for minors are undefined. The plan designs to the strictest plausible reading (the GDPR shape the law follows) and re-checks the regulator quarterly; that check is a runbook item, not a hope. **Update (pass 8, F37):** on 14 June 2026 the UAE merged the Emirates Data Office into a new Federal Authority for AI and Data (Morgan Lewis, June 2026), and the Executive Regulations were still unissued in late August 2026 (GLA, August 2026), both as verified by the pass 7 review. The DPA and the breach flow name the new authority.
- **Scope (Article 2).** Applies to controllers and processors in the UAE regardless of where the data subject is, and to those outside the UAE who process data of subjects inside it. Excluded: government data, personal data for personal use, health and credit data "governed by their own respective legislation", and free zones with their own data protection laws ([Securiti](https://securiti.ai/uae-personal-data-protection-law/); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)). ACS sits on Saadiyat Island in mainland Abu Dhabi, not in ADGM or DIFC, so the federal law applies to it and to CAROS.
- **Definitions (Article 1).** Personal data is any data relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. Sensitive personal data is data revealing "family, racial origin, political or philosophical opinions, religious beliefs, criminal records, biometric data, or ... health" (DLA Piper's rendering). Safeguarding records reveal family circumstances; SEN records are health data; nationality is treated as sensitive by policy in this plan because it sits next to racial origin and because pass 3 needs it only for fairness testing.
- **Lawful basis and consent (Articles 4 and 6).** Processing requires consent except in listed cases: performance of a contract with the data subject, a legal obligation on the controller, protection of the data subject's interests, public interest, legal claims, medical and public-health purposes, archival and statistical purposes, and other cases the Executive Regulations may add. Consent must be "clear, simple, unambiguous, and accessible", in writing or electronic, and withdrawable ([Securiti](https://securiti.ai/uae-personal-data-protection-law/)). §1.5 applies this to minors.
- **Controller and processor (Articles 7 and 8).** The controller must ensure the processor provides "sufficient guarantees to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures"; the processor processes on the controller's instructions ([Securiti](https://securiti.ai/uae-personal-data-protection-law/)).
- **Breach notification (Article 9).** The controller notifies the regulator and the data subjects "immediately, after having become aware" of a breach that may risk their privacy, confidentiality and security; the processor notifies the controller immediately on becoming aware; the procedures and time limits are left to the Executive Regulations, which do not exist ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE); [Securiti](https://securiti.ai/uae-personal-data-protection-law/)). §9.6 sets a contractual clock that is tighter than any plausible regulation. **A conflict the first version did not flag** (F37): Article 9(2) requires the controller to notify data subjects of a breach that prejudices them, while ADEK's Digital Policy 6.6.1 bars a school from communicating a cybersecurity incident to anyone but the provider and ADEK. Family notice is therefore not simply optional; which rule governs, and in what order, is a counsel question (C27).
- **Data protection officer (Articles 10 and 11).** Mandatory where processing creates high risk through new technologies, involves "systematic and comprehensive assessment of sensitive personal data, including profiling and automated processing", or involves large volumes of sensitive data ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)). The signal engine is a systematic assessment of minors' data that includes profiling and automated processing. **CAROS appoints a DPO** before the first real record (it may be an external appointee; UAE residency is not required per DLA Piper), and the DPA asks the school to name its own.
- **Rights (Articles 13 to 18).** Access, portability in machine-readable form, rectification and erasure, restriction, cessation, and objection to automated decisions with legal or serious effects. Corrected by pass 8 (F14, F37): Article 13 is a right to **information** about the processing (including recipients and automated decisions), with grounds for refusal where a request conflicts with investigations by competent authorities (13(3)(b)) or affects others' data (13(3)(d)); it does not expressly require copies. The one-month response period is DLA Piper's reading, not the law's text ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)). §7.4 designs the subject access procedure on that narrower reading, pending counsel (C7).
- **Security (Article 20) and the impact assessment (Article 21).** Measures "according to the best international standards and practices, including encryption, pseudonymization" ([Securiti](https://securiti.ai/uae-personal-data-protection-law/)). **Corrected by pass 8 (F37):** Article 21 is the data protection impact assessment, mandatory for processing that involves systematic automated assessment of personal aspects, profiling included, with serious effects; the first version cited it as the record of processing. The signal engine profiling minors is the textbook trigger. ACS as controller owns the assessment; **CAROS drafts it for ACS**, and it is on the G-REAL list (§9.13). A record of processing activities (categories, purposes, authorised personnel, retention, transfers, security measures) is still kept, as the controller's and processor's documentation duty; counsel confirms the article (C24). `privacy.table_registry` (pass 1) is the machine-readable half of that record; §1.4 lists the human-readable half.
- **Cross-border transfer (Articles 22 and 23).** Transfers to countries with an adequate level of protection (a list the regulator has not published) or, otherwise, under the cases Article 23 lists: express consent, contractual necessity, judicial cooperation, public interest, and a contract route ([Securiti](https://securiti.ai/uae-personal-data-protection-law/); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)). **Corrected by pass 8 (F37):** Article 23's contract route is limited to countries without a data protection law and binds the recipient to the PDPL's own provisions and a supervisory authority there. EU standard contractual clauses do not do that; "standard contract clauses" was a law firm's gloss, not the article's text. **Every AI call that sends a student's data to a model outside the UAE is a transfer under Article 23**, even when nothing is stored abroad, and which Article 23 case covers it is now an open counsel question (C3, revised). §6 is built on that reading.
- **Retention and processors (Articles 5(7) and 8(4)).** Data may not be kept after its purpose is served unless it is anonymised (5(7)), and a processor must erase or return data at the end of processing (8(4)) (F37). Tombstones that keep a uuid and a salted hash, and CAROS's seven-year tombstoned audit copy after termination, are pseudonymised, not anonymised; §4.4 and §7.3 say so and put them to counsel (C9, C29).
- **Penalties.** None in the law itself; a Cabinet decision is pending. Criminal exposure exists independently under the Cybercrimes law and the Penal Code (below).

**Federal Law No. 3 of 2016 on Child Rights (Wadeema's Law).** Read from the KHDA-hosted English text ([PDF](https://www.khda.gov.ae/CMS/WebParts/TextEditor/Documents/Children_Law_English.pdf)). Article 1: "Child: Every human being born alive and who is under eighteen years old." Article 42(1): "Every person shall notify the child protection specialist or child protection units in case of anything that threatens the child's physical, psychological, moral or mental integrity or health"; 42(2): "The notification shall be mandatory for the educators, physicians, social specialists or others entrusted with the protection, care or education of the child." Article 44: "The identity of the reporter shall only be disclosed with his consent and it shall be forbidden to disclose the identity of all the parties of the incident and the witnesses in the cases of child assault or abuse" (the quotation is shortened: as the pass 7 review read it, the second limb concerns analyses, media reports and publication). Article 60: failure to report under Article 42 is punished "by imprisonment or a fine not less than AED 5,000". **Consequences:** the counselor's and the teacher's reporting duty is personal and criminal; CAROS supports it and never claims to discharge it (§5.1), and since pass 8 a teacher can make the internal referral in CAROS as well (F10). Article 44 shapes who may see a reporter's identity on an escalation and forbids any export that would reveal it (§5.6).

**ADEK school policies.** These are conditions of a private school's licence in Abu Dhabi; the ones that bind what a vendor must do were read from the published PDFs (all "Effective From AY 2024/25 (Fall term), Compliance From AY 2025/26 (Fall term)").

- *Student Protection Policy* v1.1, September 2024 ([ADEK](https://www.adek.gov.ae/-/media/Project/TAMM/ADEK/Policies/School-Policies/Health-safety-and-wellbeing/ADEK_S_Student-Protection-Policy_EN.pdf)). **Federal Decree-Law No. 26 of 2025 on Child Digital Safety.** In force 1 January 2026, with full compliance expected by January 2027 "unless the deadline is extended by a Cabinet decision" ([Clyde & Co, January 2026](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/01/uae-issues-landmark-child-digital-safety-law); [Baker McKenzie, January 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/uae-issues-new-child-digital-safety-law)). It applies to "digital platforms and internet service providers operating in the UAE or directed to users in the UAE", with digital platforms described broadly (websites, applications, search, gaming, streaming, e-commerce). A child is "an individual under 18 years of age". Obligations include privacy-by-default settings, "effective and reasonable age-verification mechanisms, with the strength of the mechanism proportionate to the platform's risk classification", for children under 13 no processing of personal data without verifiable custodian consent and clear disclosures, no targeted advertising or commercial profiling of children, filtering and parental controls, and reporting channels; the TDRA supervises ISPs; penalties are "blocking, suspension, or fines". Clyde & Co notes that "Certain education and health platforms may be exempted by Cabinet decision, subject to safeguards". **Consequence:** CAROS is plausibly a digital platform with under-18 users. Whether an education platform used under a school contract is in scope, or exempted, is unknown and is the first question for counsel (C2, extended by C32). The plan complies regardless: privacy by default (§3), age from the SIS date of birth (a stronger check than any self-declaration), no advertising and no commercial profiling (the engine profiles for welfare, which must be disclosed as such), and a student-facing reporting channel (§5.7). **Revised by pass 8 (F56):** the published summaries say platforms may not collect, process, publish or share the personal data of a child under 13 at all without explicit, documented, withdrawable parental consent and a privacy-policy disclosure, and no Cabinet decision exempting education has been found. So the under-13 gate reaches **the record itself**, not only AI features, until counsel answers: an under-13 student's record is imported only when a per-student custodian consent is recorded (`sis.student.under13_custodian_consent_at`, D58), and the import refuses and reports the row otherwise. Rare at Grades 9 to 12; universal the day CAROS reaches a middle school. Cabinet Resolution No. 106 of 2026 (effective 30 June 2026), on social-media accounts of children under 15, probably does not apply to CAROS; counsel confirms (C32).

**Federal Decree-Law No. 34 of 2021 on Combatting Rumours and Cybercrimes.** Unauthorised access to systems and data is criminal (Articles 2 to 4; the insider offence, access beyond authorisation by someone with access, is Article 6, as the pass 7 review read it, F37), interception and its disclosure carry imprisonment and fines up to AED 1,000,000 (Article 12), and DLA Piper reads Article 13 as imposing detention and/or AED 50,000 to 500,000 for collecting or processing personal data in violation of UAE law ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE); [Kayrouz & Associates guide](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/guide-to-cybercrime-law-in-the-uae)). **Consequence:** an insider at CAROS who reads a school's data without a grant is not only breaching a contract (§3.7, §8.7). ADEK Digital 6.6.2 also requires schools to adhere to this law in incident handling.

**Federal Decree-Law No. 31 of 2021 (Penal Code), Articles 431 and 432.** Article 432 is the disclosure of a professional secret by a person with professional access, and Article 431 is intrusion into privacy (corrected by pass 8 from the pass 7 review's reading, F37): a fine of at least AED 20,000 and/or at least one year's imprisonment (DLA Piper's summary). Applies to counselors and to CAROS staff alike.

**Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 on the Use of ICT in Health Fields, Article 13.** Health data "may not be stored, processed, generated or transferred outside the UAE" without authorisation; fines AED 500,000 to 700,000; exemptions under Ministerial Decision No. 51 of 2021 ([Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/news/health-data-transfers-outside-the-uae/); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2019/06/new-uae-law-regulates-healthcare-data); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)). Whether a school's SEN or medical fields are "health fields" processing is untested. **Rule:** CAROS imports no medical records (ADEK lists them as a school record category; CAROS has no use for them), and during the pilot no SEN label is collected at all (decision C11, §6.5). **Added by pass 8 (F37):** counselors attached to a Department of Health licensed centre may themselves produce health data, which the law localises (Article 13) and which Article 20 requires to be kept for 25 years (Latham & Watkins, as read by the pass 7 review). If ACS's counselors are so attached, counselor notes may be health data, which would bar them from any model and lengthen their retention; counsel is asked (C30, extending C5).

**Federal Decree-Law No. 25 of 2025 (Civil Transactions Law).** The age of majority is 18 Gregorian years from 1 June 2026, replacing 21 lunar years ([The National, 1 January 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/01/01/uae-lowers-age-of-adulthood-to-18-in-modernisation-drive/); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/legal/uae-civil-transaction-law)). **Consequence:** a Grade 12 student who turns 18 is an adult data subject. §3.5 sets what that does to parental access; counsel confirms.

### 1.2 What is good practice here, and not law

These shape the design and are what a school's lawyers and a US-trained counseling team will recognise. None of them binds ACS or CAROS in Abu Dhabi, and the plan says so wherever it borrows from them.

- **GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679).** Article 3 applies the Regulation to processing "in the context of the activities of an establishment of a controller or a processor in the Union" or to the offering of goods or services to, or monitoring of, data subjects "who are in the Union" ([Article 3](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-3-gdpr/)). ACS and CAROS have no EU establishment and offer nothing to people in the Union; families holding EU passports do not bring the GDPR with them. It is the design floor anyway, because the PDPL follows its shape, because the Executive Regulations are expected to as well, and because a second school may be in a jurisdiction where it does apply. Articles used as templates: 5 (principles), 6 and 9 (bases and special categories), 8 (a child's consent for information society services is lawful from 16, Member States may lower to 13, and the controller "shall make reasonable efforts to verify" parental consent; [Article 8](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-8-gdpr/)), 12 to 22 (rights), 25 (data protection by design), 28(3)(a) to (h) (the processor contract; quoted in §1.4), 30 (records), 32 (security), 33 (notification to the authority "not later than 72 hours" and by the processor to the controller "without undue delay"; [Article 33](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-33-gdpr/)), 35 (impact assessment). The Commission's Digital Omnibus would change the breach threshold and single-entry-point mechanics, but as of mid-2026 "the Data Omnibus, covering GDPR ... is still under negotiation" ([European Parliament legislative train](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-a-new-plan-for-europe-s-sustainable-prosperity-and-competitiveness/file-digital-package)); nothing to plan on.
- **FERPA (20 U.S.C. §1232g; 34 CFR Part 99).** Part 99 "applies to an educational agency or institution to which funds have been made available under any program administered by the Secretary" of Education ([34 CFR 99.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/99.1)). ACS is assisted by the State Department's Office of Overseas Schools, which "provides guidance and best practices but is not involved in day-to-day policies" and does not operate the schools ([State Department](https://www.state.gov/bureaus-offices/under-secretary-for-management/bureau-of-administration/office-of-overseas-schools)); those are not Department of Education funds. **Assumption:** FERPA does not bind ACS. The expectations it sets are real at an American-curriculum school, and the plan adopts three of its shapes. (1) A contractor is a "school official" only if it "performs an institutional service or function for which the agency or institution would otherwise use employees", "is under the direct control of the agency or institution with respect to the use and maintenance of education records" and is bound by the redisclosure rules ([34 CFR 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/99.31)); the DPA says exactly this. (2) The institution must use "reasonable methods to ensure that school officials obtain access to only those education records in which they have legitimate educational interests" (99.31(a)(1)(ii)); §3 is that method. (3) Records "kept in the sole possession of the maker, are used only as a personal memory aid, and are not accessible or revealed to any other person" are not education records ([34 CFR 99.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/99.3)); a counselor note in CAROS is *not* sole possession, because cover and the caseload lead can read it, so in a FERPA frame it becomes part of the record a parent may request. §7.4's subject-access procedure is built for that. An "eligible student" is one who "has reached 18 years of age" (99.3), the same threshold the UAE now uses.
- **The SDPC National Data Privacy Agreement, v2.2 (19 November 2025).** The Student Data Privacy Consortium's template, used in more than 222,000 signed agreements between US districts and vendors (corrected by pass 8 from the pass 7 review's reading; the first version's 130,000 was stale) ([SDPC](https://privacy.a4l.org/national-dpa/); [A4L announcement](https://a4l.org/student-data-privacy-consortium-sdpc-announce-the-release-of-the-national-data-privacy-agreement-version-2/)). It is the DPA shape an American-curriculum school's leadership may already know; §1.4 borrows its exhibit structure. The Student Privacy Pledge was retired by the Future of Privacy Forum on 25 April 2025 and should not be cited ([FPF](https://fpf.org/student-privacy-pledge/)).
- **CISA's K-12 Education Technology Secure by Design pledge** (September 2023): publish a vulnerability disclosure policy and a secure-by-design roadmap, provide single sign-on at no extra cost, and provide security audit logs at no extra cost ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/cisa-announces-secure-design-pledge-k-12-education-technology-providers)). All four are commitments in §9.13.
- **The UK ICO's Age Appropriate Design Code ("Children's code").** A statutory code of fifteen standards for online services "likely to be accessed by children" ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/)). Not UAE law; used as the template for the child-facing surface: best interests as the first test, high-privacy defaults, minimisation, profiling off unless justified with protections, no nudging, and notices a Grade 9 student can read (§1.5).
- **Standards.** OWASP ASVS 5.0 (released May 2025; about 350 requirements in 17 chapters; [OWASP ASVS on GitHub](https://github.com/OWASP/ASVS)) at Level 2 is the verification target; OWASP Top 10:2025 (A01 Broken Access Control through A10 Mishandling of Exceptional Conditions; [OWASP](https://top10.owasp.org/2025)) and the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 (LLM01 Prompt Injection through LLM10 Unbounded Consumption; [OWASP GenAI](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/)) organise §8; NIST SP 800-63B-4 (final, 26 August 2025; [NIST](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html)) sets the session and authenticator rules in §2.4; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and SOC 2 Type II are the certifications a school's IT will eventually ask for (§9.13). Microsoft's DESC CSP Security Standard certificate "applies to two Azure UAE regions: UAE Central (Abu Dhabi) and UAE North (Dubai)" ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/compliance/offerings/offering-uae-desc)); DESC is a Dubai standard and does not bind an Abu Dhabi school, but it is in-country evidence the school can read.

### 1.4 The data processing agreement, in outline

One agreement per school, signed before any real record is imported; the same text for every school with per-school annexes. Structure follows GDPR Article 28(3)(a) to (h) ([Article 28](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-28-gdpr/)), because the PDPL's Articles 7 and 8 ask for the same guarantees in fewer words, and because the SDPC NDPA's exhibit layout is what a US-trained school reads fluently.

| Clause | Content | Source of the obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Parties and roles | School as controller; CAROS as processor; the school's DPO or data protection lead and CAROS's DPO named | PDPL Articles 1, 7, 8, 10 |
| Instructions | Processing only on documented instructions: this DPA, the enabled modules (`core.school_module`), the mapping profiles the school activates, and requests by the school's authorised users. CAROS tells the school if an instruction appears unlawful | GDPR 28(3)(a) shape; ADEK Digital 5.5.2 |
| School-official language | CAROS performs a function the school would otherwise perform with employees, is under the school's direct control as to use and maintenance of records, and will not redisclose | 34 CFR 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B), adopted voluntarily |

### 1.5 Lawful basis, consent and notices for minors

The school chooses the basis; this plan recommends one and builds the mechanics for the alternatives.

**Recommended basis for the core processing** (directory, academic, attendance, behaviour, signals, cases, notes, university journey): not consent. Three of the PDPL Article 4 cases apply together: performance of the enrolment contract between the school and the family; the school's legal obligations under the ADEK Career and University Guidance Policy (guidance for every Cycle 3 student from Grade 9) and the Student Protection Policy (identifying and supporting students at risk); and protection of the data subject's interests (safeguarding). Consent is the wrong instrument for a function the school must perform and a child cannot opt out of; a withdrawn consent would leave a counselor unable to see a student who needs them. This reading is an **assumption for counsel** (question C1) and the school's DPO decides. **Pass 8 flags three weaknesses in it** (F37, from the pass 7 review's reading of the law and ADEK's policies): the contract basis needs the data subject to be a party, and the enrolment contract is with the parents, not the child; the legal-obligation basis covers federal laws, not ADEK policies, so the Guidance and Protection policies may not carry it; and ADEK's Digital Policy 7.1.2 and Student Mental Health Policy 3.6 require consent procedures, including parental consent for structured counselling, which this plan does not model. Counsel is asked (C26); until the answer, the protection-of-interests basis carries safeguarding, and the school's attestation must name its basis for the rest.

**Where consent is right:** optional processing that the family or student can decline without losing the school's service. Mentor programme participation (student and custodian, recorded on `mentor.pairing`); Google Classroom activity as an engagement source (§6.6); AI features where the school elects consent as its Article 23 transfer basis rather than contractual safeguards (open decision 4); and, until counsel answers C32, **any processing at all** of a student under 13 (Child Digital Safety Law, F56): the record is imported only with a per-student custodian consent recorded, checked at import against `sis.student.date_of_birth`.

**ADEK Records 1.5.1 ("parent's permission before transferring student information to any third party").** This is the one rule that is not satisfied by a lawful basis. The school must be able to show that parents permitted the transfer to CAROS. Two mechanisms, both supported: the school's enrolment terms name educational service providers and CAROS is added to the school's published list (the usual practice; question 109 asks whether ACS's terms do this), or a per-family notice with acknowledgement collected through the parent portal's activation (§2.2). CAROS records the school's attestation of its mechanism in `core.school.legal_basis_attestation` (jsonb: mechanism, date, attesting person) and **refuses to import real data until it is present**: a real tenant cannot move from `onboarding` to `shadow` without it (pass 1 §2.3's status model, which every pass now shares, F36; D45's trigger). Per-guardian `consent_status` (pass 1) then means: `given` when the family acknowledged the notice or a consent column was mapped; `withdrawn` when a guardian objected through the portal or the school recorded it; `unknown` otherwise. **What `unknown` permits:** everything the school's attested basis covers. **What `withdrawn` does:** it is a signal to the school, shown on the file, not an automatic switch, because the school as controller decides whether the objection can be honoured (an objection to safeguarding processing cannot be). Optional features check `consent_status = 'given'` on the relevant link.

**Transparency.** Three notices, versioned in `privacy.notice_version` (new table, §10) and acknowledged in `privacy.notice_ack` on first sign-in and on each new version: one for staff, one for parents, one for students written at a reading level a Grade 9 student can follow (the Children's code's transparency standard). Each states: what CAROS holds, why, that an engine evaluates each student against their own history and what that means (pass 3's disclosure question 107), that AI features send pseudonymised text for **inference in the United States** to a named provider (F76), that pseudonymisation removes names but a student's or parent's own words can still carry anything they wrote (F20), that the provider may keep content its safety systems flag for up to two years and classification scores for up to seven years, and what "flagged" means (F63, §1.6), that no model reads a student's words for the safety screen (C1), that shadow mode produces inferences nobody acts on during the pilot, that mentors are alumni who may live abroad and see a student's first name by default (F76), who can see what (the matrix rendered from `auth.role_permission`), how long data is kept, and how to ask for a copy, a correction or erasure. The school reviews the text; CAROS does not publish a notice a school has not approved.

**Students who turn 18.** From the eighteenth birthday, rights belong to the student (Civil Transactions Law; FERPA's "eligible student" as the American frame). **Decided 2026-09-24 (C5), for now:** existing guardian links continue by default, because the school's relationship with the family continues and tuition is a family matter; the student is told, on their next sign-in, that their parents can see the parent portal's view and may ask the school to end it. If the student objects, the school decides and records it on the link (`ended_reason = 'student_objection_18'`). ACS is asked for its view before the pilot (question 148), and the default is revisited if ACS or counsel prefers suspension until the student opts in (question C6). After 18, only the student may file a subject-access request about themselves (§7.4, F14).

**Age verification.** The SIS date of birth is the age source; CAROS asks nobody their age. Under-13 students (rare in Grade 9; possible in an international intake) are identified at import and the under-13 gate applies automatically.

## 2. Identity

Three populations, three paths, one rule: **signing in proves who you are; it never decides what you may see.** Access comes from memberships, roster rows, guardian links and pairings, all of which are written by imports or by an administrator's audited act, never by the act of signing in.

### 2.1 Staff and students: Google Workspace

**Protocol.** OpenID Connect authorization-code flow with PKCE, handled server-side in `apps/web`, scopes `openid email profile` only. No Drive, Gmail or Classroom scope is ever requested by the sign-in client; the Classroom reader (pass 2 §7.6) is a separate service account the school delegates to, so the sign-in app stays eligible for the Workspace admin's "Limited" setting ("App can only access unrestricted services"; [Google Workspace Admin Help](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/apps/control-which-apps-access-google-workspace-data)). The request carries `hd=<school domain>` for the account-picker, and the server **validates the `hd` claim in the ID token**, not the parameter: Google's own guidance is "Don't rely on this UI optimization to control who can access your app, as client-side requests can be modified. Be sure to validate that the returned ID token has an `hd` claim value that matches what you expect", because "the ID token `hd` claim is contained within a security token from Google, so the value can be trusted" ([Google OpenID Connect](https://developers.google.com/identity/openid-connect/openid-connect)). The server also validates `iss` (`https://accounts.google.com` or `accounts.google.com`), `aud` (the client id), `exp`, `nonce`, `state`, and requires `email_verified = true`. The identity key is `sub`, which is "unique among all Google Accounts and never reused"; the email is display text (same page). A token whose `hd` matches no row in `auth.school_domain` (new, §10: `school_id`, `domain`, `population staff|student|both`, `verified_at`) is rejected before any tenant query runs; the domain-to-school lookup is the one deliberate cross-tenant read in the system, and it reads a table that holds domains and nothing about people.

**Mapping a Google identity to a person and a role.** In the tenant the `hd` claim selects: (1) look up `core.person.google_subject = sub`; (2) if absent, look up `core.person.email = token.email` among persons of kind `staff` or `student` **that have no `google_subject` stored** (F75: otherwise a recycled address would bind a new hire to a departed counselor's person), with pass 2's corroboration rules (for students the SIS is the source of the address, so a unique match binds; for staff a unique match binds only with a staff-import source, and the binding is audited `IDENTITY_BOUND` with both values); a match on a person who already has a different `google_subject` is refused and queued for the `school_admin`; (3) if nothing matches, the person sees a "no access" page that names the school's contact, the attempt is audited `SIGN_IN_DENIED` with reason `no_person`, and a queue item appears for the `school_admin` ("a Google account in your domain signed in and has no CAROS record"). **Nothing is created at sign-in.** Pass 1's `auth.membership.source = 'sso_jit'` is therefore never written; §10 removes the value so that no later session can reintroduce just-in-time provisioning by accident. A person with no active membership sees the same "no access" page. A person with one membership enters as that role; with several, they choose an acting role (§2.4).

**Roles come from two sources only.** `teacher` memberships are written by the staff import (pass 2 §4.8) and end when the staff snapshot no longer lists the person. `counselor`, `school_admin` and `staff` memberships and every capability (`ib_coordinator`, `caseload_lead`, `safeguarding_lead`, `safeguarding_oversight`, `mentor_coordinator`, and for schools that use them `year_lead`, `tutor` and `ucas_adviser`) are granted by a `school_admin` in the administration screens (§2.5), under step-up, confirmed by a second `school_admin` (the tenant's second `school_admin` itself excepted, §2.5 rule 1), never to oneself, and audited (F16). A job title in an export is never an authorisation (pass 2's rule, kept).

**Google's verification of the CAROS client** (§12.14 of the pass 7 review). CAROS owns the sign-in OAuth client in its own Google Cloud project and completes Google's brand verification for it; it requests no sensitive or restricted scope, so sensitive-scope verification does not apply to it. The delegated reader account (Classroom and the Directory read below) is authorised by the school's Workspace administrator through domain-wide delegation, which is the school's act, not a Google verification.

**What the school must configure, and why it is a gate.** Google Workspace for Education requires the school to configure third-party apps for users designated under 18: such users "are blocked from using unconfigured third-party apps" and may only request access; the admin marks the app Trusted, Limited, Specific Google data or Blocked per organisational unit ([Manage access to unconfigured third-party apps for users designated as under 18](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/getting-started/editions/manage-access-to-unconfigured-third-party-apps-for-users-designated-as-under-18)). The same page states: "For users under the age of 18, your organization is responsible for obtaining parental consent, if required by applicable law, before allowing these users to access third-party apps." That sentence is Google saying what §1.5 says: the school's basis and its ADEK Records 1.5.1 permission come before a student can sign in. Onboarding step (pass 6): the school's Workspace admin sets CAROS to Limited for the staff and student organisational units, confirms which units are designated under 18 in the age-based access setting, and CAROS records the confirmation in the tenant's onboarding checklist.

**Deprovisioning.** Four signals, each of which revokes every session of the person and is audited `SESSION_REVOKED` with its reason:

1. **Membership end.** When the last active membership ends (import absence after pass 2's 14-day grace, or an administrator's "offboard now", which needs no grace), sessions are revoked in the same transaction. Pass 2 said "sessions are revoked at `left`"; the precise rule is *at the end of the last active membership*, which is normally the same moment, and immediately when an administrator ends it by hand.
2. **Directory reconciliation, mandatory** (revised by pass 8, F18). Google's Cross-Account Protection page states that it does not currently send security events for Google Workspace users ([Google, Cross-Account Protection](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/risc), as checked by the pass 7 review), and ACS's staff and students sign in with Workspace accounts; a RISC receiver would receive nothing for them, and a test with a signed test event would pass while the control did nothing. So the first version's RISC receiver is dropped from the thin slice and from the G-REAL list, and kept only for consumer Google accounts, if mentors ever sign in with one. Instead, a `users.list` on the Admin SDK Directory API, through the same domain-wide-delegated reader account pass 2 designs for Classroom, with the read-only scope `admin.directory.user.readonly`, ends memberships whose Google account is `suspended` or deleted ([Directory API users resource](https://developers.google.com/workspace/admin/directory/reference/rest/v1/users)). It runs **hourly for staff** and nightly for students, and it is mandatory: a school that will not grant the scope must accept, in its onboarding checklist, that a suspended account keeps access until the next staff import or a manual end (question 110, revised). It runs for every tenant pass 1's `core.schools_with_accounts()` returns, which is every status but `offboarded`, so a tenant still in `onboarding`, whose `school_admin` holders already sign in, is covered (F36, F18). **The reader account's Workspace role** (F74, F18). What is known: for Classroom, only a course's teachers and domain administrators see all of its submissions, so the reader must be a domain administrator or be added as a teacher of every course CAROS reads (pass 2 §7.6). For the Directory read, the account needs an administrator privilege that can read users; the minimum role is not settled by this plan and is to be confirmed with ACS IT under question 110. A custom administrator role limited to reading users would be the narrowest choice, but that one is available in ACS's Workspace edition, and that it suffices for `users.list`, are assumptions checked at onboarding, as pass 2 §7.6 also says.
3. **The school's leaver runbook gains a step.** Alongside "suspend the Google account" (question 20), the school's `school_admin` ends the person in CAROS ("offboard now"), which revokes every session at once. The onboarding checklist gives the school this line for its own runbook.
4. **CAROS administrator action.** "Sign out everywhere" on a person, or ending the tenant.

**Students whose accounts persist after leaving.** A student who has graduated may keep a Google account for a while; their `student` membership ends with the enrolment (pass 2 §4.4), so the account signs in to "no access" until the alumni or mentor path gives it something (§2.3).

### 2.2 Parents: magic links bound to verified relationships

Three ideas, kept apart: the **link** signs in a person; the **relationship** decides what that person sees; the **activation** proves that the inbox reaches the right human before either matters.

1. **Guardians exist only from the SIS.** A `core.person` of kind `guardian` and its `family.guardian_link` rows come from the contacts import (`verified_source = 'sis_import'`) or from a `school_admin` who creates a link by hand with a reason (`verified_source = 'school_admin'`). No self-registration exists. `parentOf()`'s fabricated default is not ported.
2. **Activation, once per guardian.** Before the first magic link is ever sent, the guardian must present an **activation code** that the school issued through a channel that is not the email: printed in the enrolment pack, handed over at a parent evening, sent from the school's own SMS or messaging system, or read out by the front office after the school's identity check. The code is eight characters of Crockford base32 (40 bits), single-use, expires after 30 days, stored hashed, issued and reissued only by a `school_admin` (audited `ACTIVATION_ISSUED`). The guardian enters the code and their email; the email **must equal the SIS address** on the guardian record; a mismatch is refused with a message to contact the school, which corrects the SIS and re-imports. CAROS never lets a guardian change the address they are about to prove. Then, and only then, the first magic link goes to the SIS address. The activation binds `verified_at` and sets `activation_status = 'active'` on every link of **that SIS contact**, and `auth.is_guardian_of()` itself requires an active activation, so a link is never readable because the magic-link code forgot to check (F57). A link added later for an already-activated contact (a sibling enrols) becomes active only when the import that creates it has been approved by a second person, with the new link listed individually in the preview's security section (pass 2, handoff); until then it is `pending`. Schools that cannot distribute codes may choose knowledge-based activation (student number plus the student's date of birth plus the last four digits of the guardian's SIS phone number); it is weaker, because the student's number and birthday are widely known, so it is allowed only when the school records that choice in `core.school.parent_activation_mode = 'knowledge'` and accepts the residual risk in the DPA. Recommendation: codes.
3. **The link.** 256 bits from a CSPRNG; only its SHA-256 is stored (`auth.magic_link.token_hash`); 15-minute expiry (a compromise between NIST's 10-minute out-of-band rule and email delivery delays; [NIST SP 800-63B-4 §3.1.3.2](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html)); single use; **bound to the requesting browser**: the request sets a `__Host-ml_nonce` cookie, the link's GET renders a confirmation page, and consumption is a POST that succeeds only when the cookie matches the row. A link opened on another device shows "open this on the device where you asked for it", with a six-digit code displayed on the requesting device that can be typed on the second one. This defeats the two ordinary failures of magic links: a forwarded or harvested link, and mail-security proxies that pre-fetch every URL (the GET does nothing). Rate limits: three links per address per hour, ten per day, uniform responses so addresses cannot be enumerated. Audited `MAGIC_LINK_SENT` and `MAGIC_LINK_CONSUMED`.
4. **After activation.** Plain magic links to the SIS address, or a passkey the guardian may enrol to skip the inbox (WebAuthn Level 3 became a W3C Recommendation on 25 August 2026; [W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/)).
5. **What a signed-in guardian sees.** Exactly the students with an active, verified `guardian_link` (`linked_children` scope, pass 1). Several children: a child switcher; each child under that child's grade-conditional navigation; no merged view (pass 2 §4.7). The switcher lists linked children only, and every route that takes a student id returns the same not-found response for a non-linked id as for a non-existent one, so a guardian cannot learn that another child exists.
6. **Two guardians sharing one email** (pass 2 open decision 4), **revised by pass 8 (F57).** The first version merged them into one person, which meant a court order against one parent could not be applied without cutting off the other. Now: **one `core.person` and one set of links per SIS contact, never merged**, and one activation code per contact. A magic link requested for a shared address asks, after the browser-bound step, which of the named contacts is signing in, and that contact's first sign-in on a device requires their own activation code or their own passkey, so each session belongs to one contact. Every message and meeting request shows the contact who wrote it. **Restrictions:** a restriction imported from the SIS (a court order, a "no contact" flag; `family.guardian_link.restriction`, D42) suspends that contact's links at once and nobody else's; if the restricted contact shares an address with another, the shared-address sign-in is suspended for both until the school records separate addresses, because a shared inbox cannot keep one of them out. Which Veracross fields carry such restrictions is question 153.
7. **The SIS address changes.** When an import changes a guardian's email, the activation is invalidated (`activation_status = 'reactivation_required'`), sessions are revoked, and the new address must activate again with a new code; `auth.is_guardian_of()` refuses the link in the meantime (F57). The school confirmed the change by importing it; CAROS still confirms that the new inbox reaches the same relationship, because this is where a typo becomes a stranger reading a child's file (§8.3).
8. **Ended links.** A child leaves, or the school ends a link (custody change, court order): access ends at the moment the link ends; the guardian sees "no children linked" and the school's contact.
9. **A guardian who is also staff.** Two persons unless a `school_admin` links them through pass 2's identity candidate. Even when linked, sessions are per acting role (§2.4): a staff session never gains `linked_children` and a parent session never gains `caseload` or `roster`.

### 2.3 Mentors: invited, vetted, in-platform only

Mentors are alumni volunteers, adults, outside the school's identity system, and in contact with minors. ADEK's Student Protection Policy defines a volunteer as "an individual engaged by a educational institution on a non-remunerated basis to interact with students" and obliges principals to "ensure the vetting, hiring, and monitoring of all staff, volunteers, and invited visitors" ([ADEK Student Protection Policy v1.1](https://www.adek.gov.ae/-/media/Project/TAMM/ADEK/Policies/School-Policies/Health-safety-and-wellbeing/ADEK_S_Student-Protection-Policy_EN.pdf), the Roles section). ADEK's Digital Policy 6.4 adds that live virtual interactions with invited visitors need parents' consent and ADEK's approval (F04). The path follows both.

1. **Sponsorship and vetting.** A counselor holding the new `mentor_coordinator` capability (D35: allowed on the counselor role only) creates the mentor as a `core.person` of kind `mentor` (name, personal email) and records the school's vetting on `mentor.profile.vetting` (jsonb, §10): identity document seen (type, expiry, by whom, when), criminal-record check (jurisdiction, date, reference), safeguarding training date, signed volunteer agreement date, and a `vetting_expires_at` for annual renewal. The documents themselves stay in the school's volunteer file; CAROS holds the fact that they were seen, not the images (minimisation). `verification_status = 'verified'` can be set only when every field is present, and `active` requires `verified` (pass 1's CHECK). Only the school can do any of this; CAROS staff cannot verify a mentor.
2. **Invitation and credential.** The coordinator gives the vetted mentor a single-use, seven-day invitation code in person or through the school's volunteer office, **not by email** (revised by pass 8: decision C3 keeps email to the declared safety messages, and no credential is ever persisted in the outbox, F16). The mentor enters it at the CAROS sign-up page and creates a **passkey** (WebAuthn: platform authenticator or security key). For a device without passkey support, the fallback is a password of at least 12 characters checked against the Have I Been Pwned Pwned Passwords range API (k-anonymity on the first five characters of the SHA-1; no key or licence required; [HIBP API v3](https://haveibeenpwned.com/API/v3)), which is how NIST's requirement that "verifiers SHALL compare the prospective secret against a blocklist that contains known commonly used, expected, or compromised passwords" is met ([NIST SP 800-63B-4 §3.1.1.2](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html)), stored with Argon2id, **plus mandatory TOTP**. Recovery is by the coordinator re-inviting, never by email alone. `auth.session.auth_method` gains `passkey`, `password_totp` and drops the placeholder `mentor_password` (§10).
3. **Assurance and lifetime.** AAL2-equivalent; sessions of 12 hours absolute and 30 minutes idle (§2.4). Mentors are the population with the least school oversight of their devices, so they get the shortest staff-like sessions.
4. **What a mentor can see.** Students with an `active` pairing only, and within that only the rows of **their own** pairing (`own_pairing` scope, F59), never another mentor's thread with the same student. Columns, through `mentor.v_mentee`: the student's **first name** by default (the full name only if the student chooses it in `share_scope`; mentors may live abroad, and the consent text says so, F76), year group, declared goal, and whatever else the student put in `share_scope` (goal by default; network optional). Pass 1's column revocations hide email, phone, date of birth, nationality and fee status from the mentor tier; this pass adds `sis.student.student_number` and all of `academic`, `attendance`, `behaviour`, `signal`, `family` to the never-visible list for mentors (the matrix in §3.6 has no mentor entry in those rows). A mentor cannot search for students, cannot see other mentors' pairings, and cannot initiate a pairing: the school arranges introductions (`mentor.pairing.arranged_by_person_id` is required before `active`), which is invariant 11 as a constraint.
5. **In-platform contact, visible to the school.** The `mentor` schema has no email or phone column (pass 1). Messages are `mentor.message` rows, append-only, whose student subject is derived through the pairing (F59). Every thread is readable by the student's counselor (caseload scope), by the `mentor_coordinator` and by the `safeguarding_lead` (school scope), and both participants are told so in the thread header; parents see only that a pairing exists and the mentor's headline, never the messages (F59). That visibility is the safeguarding control; the deterrent is knowing it exists. Two mechanical controls sit under it. **Holding:** a message containing a phone number, an email address, a social-media handle, or a phrase from the lexicon's "move the conversation elsewhere" list is held for the coordinator's review before delivery, and the sender is told it is held; the match is by pattern only, with no model (decision C1). **Rate:** a mentor may send at most twenty messages a day per student. The lexicon safety screen runs on every message: a disclosure routes to the counselor exactly as student free text does (§5.7); grooming indicators route to the coordinator by the reference-only grooming-alert email (§5.8) and to the safeguarding lead in-app.
6. **Sessions.** Scheduled through CAROS and, in v1, **held in person on school premises only**, under the school's supervision policy (decision C6). Video on the school's own platform is deferred until ACS has parental consent and ADEK approval in place under Digital Policy 6.4 (question 155); a `mentor.session.mode` CHECK allows only the in-person value until then (D57). CAROS stores the schedule, whether it was held, and the mentor's note (`school_visible` is always true in v1; the column stays for a later decision but no screen can set it false). **CAROS records nothing.** The prototype's "sessions ... are recorded" is not ported (§0.2); `recording_file_id` stays NULL. Whether the school records its own calls is the school's decision under its own policy (open decision 9).
7. **Ending.** The student, the counselor or the coordinator ends a pairing with one action; the mentor's read access to that student ends at that instant (RLS), and mentors have no export, so nothing leaves with them. A mentor's `active` flag is cleared at the end of the programme year and on any safeguarding concern; the person record follows the mentor retention class (§7.1).
8. **Pass 1's alumni fixtures** (`PATH_ALUMNI`, `ALUMNI_OUTCOMES`) are reporting content, not identities, and never a route into the mentor path.

### 2.4 Sessions, the acting role, and step-up

- **Sessions are server-side rows** (`auth.session`) referenced by an opaque identifier of 128 random bits, well above the 64 bits OWASP requires ([OWASP Session Management Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html)), in a `__Host-caros_s` cookie with `Secure`, `HttpOnly` and `SameSite=Lax` (`Lax` because the return from Google is a top-level navigation that must carry the cookie; CSRF on mutations is covered by tRPC's origin check and a per-session token). No JWT-only trust: every request loads the row, checks `revoked_at`, `expires_at` and idle time, and updates `last_seen_at`. Every check runs before `withTenant()` opens the transaction that will carry the person id (pass 1 DR-4).
- **One acting role per session** (pass 1 open decision 7, resolved as option (a)). A person with several memberships picks one at sign-in; the row records `acting_role`; the database tier role and the column privileges follow it. Switching role creates a **new** session after re-authentication (the OWASP rule that the identifier "must be renewed or regenerated ... after any privilege level change"), and the old session is revoked. Capabilities are read live from the membership on every request, so ending a capability takes effect on the next request.
- **Lifetimes.** NIST SP 800-63B-4 sets, at AAL2, a reauthentication timeout that "SHOULD be no more than 24 hours" and an inactivity timeout that "SHOULD be no more than 1 hour"; at AAL1 an overall timeout of no more than 30 days ([NIST](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html), §§2.1.3, 2.2.3). Proposal, per population:

| Population | Absolute | Idle | Concurrent sessions | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Counselor, teacher, any capability holder | 12 hours | 60 minutes | 5 | A school day; welfare data; shared staffroom machines |
| Student | 7 days | 24 hours | unlimited | Own record only; phones; NIST AAL1 allows 30 days |
| Parent | 14 days | 48 hours | unlimited | Own children's record; phones; the 14 days *is* the "remember me" |
| Mentor | 12 hours | 30 minutes | 2 | Outsider with access to a minor's messages |
| CAROS support (under grant) | 1 hour | 15 minutes | 1 | AAL3-shaped; every read audited |

- **Revocation** on: membership end (including one ended by the hourly Directory read, §2.1), credential change, "sign out everywhere", tenant suspension, and the daily job that expires stale rows. Sessions belong to the `session` retention class (30 days after expiry, pass 1).
- **Step-up** is re-authentication within the last ten minutes, recorded in `auth.session.step_up_at` and audited `STEP_UP` with the action it unlocked. **Revised by pass 8 (F19):** Google's OpenID Connect reference documents only `none`, `consent` and `select_account` for `prompt`, documents no `max_age`, and returns `auth_time` only when it is requested through the `claims` parameter; the first version's `prompt=login` could come straight back from a live Google session and prove nothing. So for Google users, step-up requests `auth_time` through `claims` and rejects any token whose `auth_time` is older than ten minutes; a B1 spike tests this against a live session before the step-up screens are built (pass 6 handoff), and if Google will not force a fresh authentication, staff step up with a **CAROS-held passkey** enrolled at first sign-in (WebAuthn is already in the plan for mentors and parents). For mentors it is a passkey assertion with user verification; for parents a fresh magic link. Required for: raising an escalation; acknowledging or closing one; any `export` action (§3.6); opening the audit-log screen; granting, accepting or revoking a support grant; changing memberships, capabilities, role permissions or the safeguarding route; activating a mapping profile or rolling back an import; self-granting break-glass cover (§3.3); scheduling or executing an erasure; downloading a document of class `safeguarding` or `case_note`.
- **Anomalies.** A staff sign-in from a new country or autonomous system emits an in-app notice to the person and the `school_admin` (identifiers and coarse location only); more than five failed magic-link requests for one address in an hour throttles the address; more than twenty `SIGN_IN_DENIED` events from one domain in a day pages the on-call, because it may be a misconfigured import rather than an attack.

### 2.5 Identity administration

The `school_admin` **role** (revised by pass 8, F16: no longer a capability on a counselor membership) owns five screens, each action audited (`MEMBERSHIP_CHANGED`, `CAPABILITY_REQUESTED`, `CAPABILITY_CONFIRMED`, `SECOND_ADMIN_CONFIRMED`, `CASELOAD_ASSIGNED`, `COVER_GRANTED`, `ACTIVATION_ISSUED`, `MENTOR_VETTED`, `ROUTE_CHANGED`, `DOMAIN_MAPPED`) and each grant behind step-up: **People** (identities that signed in with no person, and pass 2's identity candidates); **Memberships** (grant or end memberships and capabilities); **Caseload and cover** (primary assignments and planned cover, run with the `caseload_lead`); **Parents** (activation codes, activation status, link ends); **Mentors** (vetting, invitations, pairings, held messages, with the `mentor_coordinator`). Four rules keep the administrator from becoming a single point of failure, or a back door:

1. **Bootstrap under a school-approved grant** (F35). The first `school_admin` membership in a real tenant, the tenant row, its first domain and its safeguarding route are created by CAROS at onboarding through audited domain functions, under a one-time bootstrap write grant whose approval is the signed DPA and the first `school_admin`'s written confirmation. The DPA names **two different people**: the signatory, from the school's leadership, and the first `school_admin`; if it would name only one person, onboarding step 3 cannot start (pass 2 §9) (`auth.support_grant` with `mode = 'write'`, `kind = 'bootstrap'`, D44), valid only while the tenant has fewer than two `school_admin` memberships. CAROS creates no other membership and writes nothing else in a real tenant except through a write grant the school approves (§3.7). **The second `school_admin`** (F16; pass 2 §9 step 5). Four eyes cannot be satisfied while the tenant has one `school_admin`, so the first nominates the second, under step-up, and that one grant is confirmed by the DPA signatory, who is neither the nominator nor, by the rule above, the first `school_admin`, and who must not be the nominee. The confirmation is written, recorded on the grant with its evidence under the bootstrap grant, and audited with its own action, `SECOND_ADMIN_CONFIRMED`. The path exists once per tenant, only while it has exactly one `school_admin`.
2. **Domains need proof** (F35). A Google domain maps to a tenant only on DNS proof (a TXT record the school's Workspace administrator publishes), a `school_admin`'s approval under step-up (the signed DPA for the first domain), and an audit row (`DOMAIN_MAPPED`); `auth.school_domain` has no write grant for any tier and is changed only by that definer function (D37).
3. **Four eyes for every capability, never for oneself** (F16). Granting any capability, and every `school_admin` membership after the second, is a request by one `school_admin` and a confirmation by an existing `school_admin` who is not the grantee (`auth.capability_grant`, pass 1 §2.4); nobody grants anything to themselves, nobody confirms a grant to themselves, and nobody assigns cover to themselves (D43). Only the second `school_admin` is confirmed differently, under rule 1.
4. **No welfare access by role.** `school_admin` has no caseload path and no row in any welfare class: the matrix gives it `config`, `membership`, `ingest`, `directory` and `documents` of kinds `sis_export` and `sample_export` only (§3.6).

## 3. Authorization

### 3.1 The permission model as data

Pass 1's `auth.role_permission` stands, with the pass 8 changes that make this section's matrix storable (F02): scope and a `row_kind` are part of the unique key, pass 1's scope list gains `esc`, `addressee`, `lead_cover`, `own_pairing`, `year_group` and `tutor_group`, a table's class can be a per-row expression, and pass 1 §2.4a maps every qualifier printed in §3.6 to its mechanism and its negative test. This pass changes four further things and fills the rows.

1. **`auth.data_class` gains `sensitivity`** (`0` to `5`, §10), which drives three mechanical consequences: whether a read writes an access entry (sensitivity 3 and above always, 2 at file-open granularity, 0 and 1 never), whether a download is watermarked and step-up gated (4 and 5 always), and whether a class may ever be sent to a model (5 never, 4 never, 3 only pseudonymised; §6.2).

| Sensitivity | Classes | Access logging |
|---|---|---|
| 0 reference | `reference`, `config` (non-personal rows) | none |
| 1 directory | `directory`, `xp` (F77) | none for lists; `DIRECTORY_SEARCH` for whole-school search |
| 2 educational record | `academic`, `attendance`, `behaviour`, `university`, `ib`, `discovery` (proposals, roadmaps), `documents`, `family`, `guardian_link`, `membership`, `session`, `notification`, `support_grant`, `privacy_request`, `ingest`, `reporting` | `FILE_ACCESS` when a student's file is opened; `PREVIEW_ACCESS` for import previews; `EXPORT` |
| 3 welfare-sensitive | `signal`, `signal_shadow`, `counselor_log`, `case_note`, `contact_record` (F15), `teacher_flag`, `student_voice`, `engagement`, `mentor` (messages, notes), `ai` (generations about a student), `discovery` (chat text) | every read of a record, by the reading transaction |
| 4 safeguarding | `safeguarding`, `safety_alert` (F13) | every read, with purpose required |
| 5 special category | `fairness_label`; columns `sis.student.nationality_iso`, `fee_status_home_country`, `date_of_birth`; any SEN or health field if one is ever imported | no application role reads `fairness_label`; column reads are inside a file open and are logged with it |

2. **Three data classes from pass 3 are added** (`signal_shadow`, `counselor_log`, `fairness_label`; pass 3 D30) and placed in the matrix.
3. **A sixth action, `export`,** is added to `auth.perm_action`: producing a file, a PDF, a printable list or a clipboard copy of more than one student's record, or any document of sensitivity 3 or above. Export rows are sparse on purpose.
4. **A fifth capability, `mentor_coordinator`,** on the counselor role: vets mentors, arranges pairings, reviews held messages (§2.3). Pass 8 adds `safeguarding_oversight` (no data grant, F16) and, for schools with shared pastoral responsibility, `year_lead`, `tutor` and `ucas_adviser` (F33); and `safeguarding_lead` may now sit on any staff membership (F10).

A new role, or a school that wants a stricter matrix, is rows. The school-specific override rule (a school row with the same role, class, action, capability and row kind replaces the default rows for that key) is pass 1's. **The widening rule, stated once (decided on F47, 2026-09-24):** by default a school may only **narrow** the defaults. The D36 trigger refuses a school row whose scope ranks higher than the default's, or ranks the same and differs, using pass 1's `auth.scope_rank()` (individual < relationship < group < school), and refuses any school row for a key that has no default and any school row for `safeguarding`. One exception is kept ready and switched off: if ACS's counselors answer question 65 with "share cases", CAROS sets `core.school.allow_counselor_widening` for that school (a CAROS-approved configuration, audited), and the trigger then accepts school rows widening counselor `signal` and `case_note` read to `school` scope, and nothing else. The trigger, §3.3, §3.8 and pass 6's B0.7 and §5.4 tests all state this same rule.

### 3.2 Enforcement, in four layers that each hold alone

1. **Database, tenant.** The `RESTRICTIVE` `tenant_isolation` policy on every tenant table, `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`, composite foreign keys, a `NOBYPASSRLS` login role, and a transaction context that fails closed (pass 1 DR-4). Nothing in this pass weakens it; §8.1 lists the tests.
2. **Database, role.** The `PERMISSIVE` role policies generated by `auth.protect()` from `auth.role_permission`, plus column privileges per tier. The tier the transaction assumed is evaluated in the policy expression and passed to `auth.allowed()`, and each deployable has its own login role granted only its own tiers (pass 1 DR-4, F01). These enforce `read`, `write` and, since pass 8, the verbs a table carries: an insert or update on `signal.escalation` passes with `escalate`, on `ib.selection` with `approve`, on configuration tables with `configure` (F02). Only `export`, which produces a file rather than a row, is not visible to row-level security.
3. **Domain layer.** Every domain function begins with `assertAllowed(class, action, subjects)` for its verb, which checks `approve`, `escalate` and `configure` before the database does and is the only place `export` is enforced. State transitions run through `SECURITY DEFINER` transition functions, and user tiers hold column-level update grants only (pass 1 §2.1 rules 11 and 12, F15, F17). The assertion calls the same `auth.allowed()` function the policies call, so there is one definition of "allowed". A function that touches a sensitivity-3-or-above record then writes its access entry in the same transaction (§4.1). A lint rule (`dependency-cruiser` plus a custom rule) fails the build if a domain function issues a query before its assertion.
4. **API and UI.** tRPC middleware refuses any procedure without an authenticated session and an acting role, sets the tenant context from the session (never from the request body), and rate-limits per session. The UI hides what a role cannot do, and is never the reason it cannot.

The worker connects as `caros_worker`, runs as `actor_kind = 'system'` under `caros_t_system`, and is bound by the tenant policy like everyone else. Its only cross-tenant reads are pass 1's ID-only definer functions (`core.schools_due_for_sweep()`, `core.schools_holding_data()`, `core.schools_with_accounts()`, `notify.schools_with_due_outbox()`), which return school ids and nothing else. The sweep, connectors and data alerts key on a school holding data (`status IN ('shadow','live')`), never on "active" (F36), so nothing runs for a `suspended` or `offboarding` tenant, and the nightly sweep also waits for `core.school.sweep_enabled_at` (pass 1 §2.3); account housekeeping that can only remove access (the Directory read of §2.1, session expiry) keys on a school having accounts, every status but `offboarded`, because the `school_admin` holders sign in during `onboarding` (F18). Its per-school jobs open one `withTenant()` per school. The job queue runs under its own login with no tenant privilege, and a job for school A that carries school B's ids writes nothing (pass 1 DR-2).

### 3.3 Counselor cover and handover: caseload plus explicit, logged cover

Pass 1's open decision 8 offered caseload-scoped (a), school-scoped (b) or caseload plus explicit cover (c). **Decision: (c).** The default is caseload scope. A counselor reaches another counselor's student through exactly four paths, each of which is a row and an audit entry:

| Path | Who | How | Duration | Audit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Planned cover** | `caseload_lead` grants; or the owner grants for their own absence | `auth.caseload_assignment` with `kind = 'cover'`, `reason`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, in timestamps (F16) | ≤ 30 days; renewable | `COVER_GRANTED`; every read under cover carries `purpose = 'cover:<assignment_id>'`. **Never self-assigned:** the person granting cover cannot be its recipient (D43), so neither a lead nor a school administrator can give themselves a caseload |
| **Break-glass cover** | a **practising counselor** only: a `counselor` membership with an active primary caseload, never a school administrator, a `staff` member or a capability holder acting as such (F16) | `cover` row with `reason ≥ 20 chars`, `valid_to = now() + interval '24 hours'` (a timestamp, so 24 hours means 24 hours), `self_granted = true`; step-up required; **at most two per counselor in any seven days** | until the lead confirms it, which must happen **the same school day**, or it lapses at the end of that day; once confirmed, 24 hours; not renewable by the same person; the lead can convert it to planned cover | `COVER_GRANTED` with `self_granted`; in-app notice to the owner and the lead at once; the lead's confirmation or its lapse is audited (`COVER_CONFIRMED`, `COVER_LAPSED`); a weekly digest of break-glass use to the lead; the counselor sees a persistent banner "you are reading under break-glass cover" |
| **Pastoral assignment** (schools that use it, F33) | `school_admin`, four eyes, or the staff import | `auth.pastoral_assignment` of kind `year_lead`, `tutor` or `ucas_adviser` | while the assignment runs | `CASELOAD_ASSIGNED` with the kind; reads carry `purpose = 'pastoral'`; case ownership stays with the one primary counselor |
| **Reassignment** | owner, or `caseload_lead` | pass 1 C14; the primary assignment moves | permanent | `CASE_REASSIGNED`, `CASELOAD_ASSIGNED` |
| **Coordinator** | `ib_coordinator` | `ib_cohort` scope for `ib` data only | standing | reads of IB records are sensitivity 2; no case, note or signal is reachable through this path |

The `caseload_lead` sees case existence, tier and owner for the whole school (a team lead has to run a team) and may open any file, with every open written as `FILE_ACCESS` with `purpose` required and visible to the owning counselor on the file's access panel. What the lead does *not* get by default is school-wide read of `case_note`: notes are readable by the owner, active cover, and the lead **only when a cover or reassignment is in progress for that student** (the `lead_cover` scope: an active cover row held by anyone, or a reassignment in the last 14 days; pass 1 §2.4). Question 65 asked ACS whether counselors should see each other's cases by default. **Until ACS answers, the rule is narrow-only** (decided on F47): no school row may widen a default. The option is kept ready: if the team says yes, CAROS sets `allow_counselor_widening` for the school, and school rows may then widen counselor `signal` and `case_note` read to school scope, and never `safeguarding` (§3.1, D36).

The **safeguarding lead** reaches a case only through an escalation (`ESC` scope: the case, its evidence, its notes and the student's file, read-only, while the escalation is open and for 90 days after outcome). They do not see the caseload.

### 3.4 Teachers, scoped by roster

`roster` and `own_section` scopes come from `sis.section_teacher × sis.section_membership` on current rows only (pass 1, pass 2 §5.4). Teachers have their own database tier, `caros_t_teacher`, so column rules that differ from a counselor's are enforced by the database (F75). A teacher sees: the directory of their sections (name, year group, section); their own sections' assessments and grades; attendance events for their own sections, codes only (the teacher tier has no `SELECT` on `reason_code`, `reason_label` or `minutes_late`, and attendance is protected with its section as a subject so `own_section` can match; `sis.v_attendance_teacher` remains as a convenience); the flags they wrote, through `signal.v_teacher_flag_mine`, which shows the routed label and never `case_id` or `status`; the IB level question for their subjects, through the sign-off card (`ib.v_signoff_card`: the student's name, the prerequisite mark and the student's reason, never the prototype's "Personal band", which is an engine output; F58); Extended Essays they are party to; documents attached to those essays; reference requests addressed to them and letters they write. A teacher never sees a tier, a case, a note, another teacher's flag, a signal, family data, a student's other grades, or discovery data. **The routed label never states a tier or that a case exists** (F58): the first version's "the counselor has taken this up" told the teacher a case existed. The vocabulary is now `received` ("received by the counselor") and `reviewed` ("reviewed by the counselor"), and attaching a flag to a case, reviewing it with no action, and merging it with other concerns all render as one of those two. Sign-off is only through `ib.sign_off()`: HL picks in an open round, signed as the acting teacher, the pick itself untouched (F58). Pass 3's per-teacher concern-rate report is readable by the `caseload_lead` only.

**Concerns and referrals** (F10). A teacher may log a flag about a student on their roster, in their IB subject, or whose Extended Essay they supervise, so subject teachers and supervisors are no longer shut out. The flag form opens with the school's rule, in the school's words and with its CPO's title: "If a student told you something that suggests harm, report it to the Child Protection Officer now. A concern logged here is not a safeguarding report." A flag of kind **safeguarding** is a referral: it asks for the referral fields of §5.3, goes straight to the safeguarding route through `signal.raise_teacher_referral()`, with the same reference-only email, reason and no-double-fire rule as a counselor's escalation (pass 1 C23), and the teacher then sees only its status (sent, acknowledged) through `signal.v_escalation_status`. The student's counselor is told in-app. Flag text is read by the lexicon safety screen (§5.7).

### 3.5 Students and parents

Students read their own record (`self`) across `directory`, `academic`, `attendance` (summary counts and their own events without reason text), `university`, `ib`, `discovery`, `xp`, `documents` (of the kinds and classes a student may read, never a reference letter), `mentor` (their own pairings' threads), and write where the product lets them, through column-level grants and transition functions only (F15, F17). They never read `signal`, `case_note`, `contact_record` (meetings and the contact log, F15), `safeguarding`, `safety_alert` (whether an alert was raised about their words, F13), `teacher_flag`, `engagement`, `family` messages, `ai` generations other than their own discovery and Extended Essay feedback, or `audit`. Parents read `linked_children` across `directory`, `academic` (the school-issued transcript summaries and current grades the parent portal already shows for Grade 11 and above), `attendance` (summary; no reason text), `university` (targets, requirements, costs, offers; not statement drafts, which are the student's own writing), `ib` (selection status and CAS totals; not sign-off notes), `discovery` (approved pathways only), `family` (the messages they sent or received and their own meeting requests, never the other guardian's thread, F15), `guardian_link` (their own link rows, which they never write, F15), `mentor` (that a pairing exists and the mentor's headline, through `mentor.v_family_pairing`; not the messages, mentee notes or session requests, F59), and `documents` of kind `transcript`. A parent never reads `xp` (the student's gamification is the student's, F77), `contact_record` (meeting notes and the counselor's contact log, F15), `safety_alert`, `student_voice` (a child's words to their counselor are not the parent's by default; question C7 asks counsel whether the school must disclose them on request), `signal`, `case_note`, `safeguarding`, `teacher_flag`, `behaviour` (the school communicates behaviour through its own channels in v1; question 111), or `engagement`.

The grade-conditional navigation is a product rule, not a boundary: RLS bounds what the person can read regardless of which grade the UI thinks they are.

**At 18** (Civil Transactions Law), the student's own access is unchanged and parental access continues under the default in §1.5 unless the student objects and the school agrees (decision C5, for now; question 148 asks ACS).

### 3.6 The permission matrix

### 3.7 The CAROS support path: not a role, unavoidable, and designed to be embarrassing to use

**Principle.** No CAROS person has standing read access to any real tenant, and **no CAROS person writes into a real tenant except under a school-approved, time-boxed write grant that names its tables and the domain functions it may run**, each run audited (F35). Production database credentials for humans do not exist; each deployable connects with its own managed identity (pass 1 DR-4); developers work against the synthetic tenants (pass 1 DR-9). The onboarding engineer builds a school's mapping profiles against a pseudonymised sample the school produces (pass 2 §9), and the first real preview is the school's own. The tenant's creation, its first `school_admin`, its first domain and its safeguarding route are made under the one-time bootstrap grant of §2.5.

**When support must look.** A defect the synthetic tenants cannot reproduce, a school's request to diagnose a wrong import, a restore verification, an incident. The path is pass 1's `auth.support_grant`, completed:

1. **Request.** A CAROS operator opens a grant request naming the school, a ticket reference (the ticket system is named in the DPA annex and holds no student data, F61), a reason of at least 20 characters, the requested duration (≤ 8 hours; pass 1's CHECK), the mode (`read`, or `write` with the domain functions it will run), the tables needed, the operator's location, and whether **unmasking** is required (default no: support reads see person names, emails and free text replaced by stable per-grant pseudonyms, through the masked views in pass 1's `support` schema, which are the support tier's only grant; it holds no base table, F35). Raw SIS exports, staged rows, rejected rows and the outbox are never on a grant.
2. **Approval.** By the school, always: a `school_admin` or `caseload_lead` approves in-app under step-up, and the approval is what activates the grant. **The first version's emergency path, in which a second CAROS operator could approve without the school, is removed** (F35: two CAROS operators could otherwise activate access to a school's data with no one at the school involved). If the school cannot be reached during a live incident, CAROS works on infrastructure and aggregate metadata only until one of the school's approvers responds; the DPA names at least two approvers and how to reach them out of hours (question 114). A `safeguarding`-class read needs a separate flag on the grant that only the school's `safeguarding_lead` can approve; the expectation is that it is never used, and any use is reported to the school's leadership.
3. **Session.** The operator authenticates to the support console, a separate small app with its own login role `caros_support` (pass 1 DR-4, F01), with a hardware-key passkey (Entra ID with phishing-resistant MFA and Privileged Identity Management for the Azure side), **from a CAROS-managed device** (F61). The session is `auth_method = 'support'`, 1 hour absolute, 15 minutes idle, `actor_kind = 'support'`, `actor_label = <operator id>`, `SET LOCAL ROLE caros_t_support`. `auth.allowed()` honours the support claim only while an approved, unrevoked, unexpired grant exists for that school **that names this operator and this table** (`auth.support_grant_covers()`, pass 1 §2.4, F35); a second operator cannot ride another's grant. Real data is shown to CAROS only in person or on the school's own video platform during onboarding and reveal sessions, never on a CAROS conferencing tenant and never in a ticket (F61).
4. **Every read is a row.** `SUPPORT_ACCESS` with the grant id, ticket, tables and, for a student-subject row, the student id; the school sees these entries in its own audit screen as "CAROS support read this record", and the counselor sees them on the file's access panel like any other read.
5. **Writes only under a write grant** (F35). A read grant never writes. A fix is a migration or a job reviewed in a pull request (pass 1 DR-2), an action the school's own `school_admin` performs with the operator on a call, or a **write grant** the school approves that names its tables and the domain functions the operator may run (authoring a mapping profile, loading a caseload through the Studio with its security section, rolling back an import), each run through the audited domain function and recorded as `WRITE_GRANT_EXECUTED`. Pass 2's rollback authority: the `school_admin` rolls back; support may roll back only under such a grant.
6. **Review.** Monthly: every grant, its approver, its duration, its row count, and any four-eyes approvals, reviewed by CAROS's DPO and sent to each school as part of its monthly report. A grant with no ticket, or more than two grants for one school in a month, is an item on that report.
7. **The onboarding engineer's path** (pass 2) is the same grant, requested by the engineer, approved by the school's `school_admin` as part of onboarding, limited to `ingest` screens and previews, and masked by default; authoring the school's mapping profiles is a write grant on the profile tables only. Threshold activation during shadow mode (pass 3) is the caseload lead's act with the engineer's acknowledgement recorded on the version note; it needs no grant, because the engineer does not perform it.

**Azure-side access** is separate and also grant-shaped: no standing `Owner` or `Contributor` on the production subscription; Entra Privileged Identity Management activates a role for a bounded time with justification; the activation appears in Azure Activity Log, which is exported to the security Log Analytics workspace and reviewed monthly. Database-level emergencies (a stuck migration) use a break-glass `caros_migrator` credential held in Key Vault behind a second person's approval, rotated after each use, and its use is an incident record. **Microsoft's own access** is controlled by Customer Lockbox, which needs a paid support plan; CAROS buys it (F61, pass 1 DR-1 cost table). **Access from abroad**: operators may work from outside the UAE on managed devices, with their location recorded on each grant; a UAE-hosted Bastion or virtual desktop is deferred until support access is frequent, and whether viewing tenant data from abroad is a transfer is a counsel question (C31).

### 3.8 Tests that make the matrix true

In `packages/db/test/authz/`, run on every pull request against both synthetic tenants (pass 1 DR-8):

- For every (role or capability, data class, action) cell in §3.6: a positive fixture (a student in scope) and a negative fixture (a student out of scope: other caseload, other roster, other child, other pairing, other school). The suite is generated from `auth.role_permission` and a fixture map, so a new row without fixtures fails the build.
- The eight "should see nothing" cases: no context; expired session; ended membership; ended cover; ended guardian link; ended pairing; teacher after a roster move; counselor after reassignment.
- Column cases: mentor cannot select the revoked columns; teacher's attendance view has no reason text; guardian tier cannot select nationality.
- Verb cases: `approve`, `escalate`, `configure` succeed **end to end through RLS** for every role that holds them (F02: the first version tested verbs only negatively, and the counselor's escalation insert would have failed), and are refused in both the domain layer and the database for every role that lacks the row, including when the row exists for `read`; `export` refused in the domain layer likewise.
- Qualifiers: every row of pass 1 §2.4a has its negative fixture, among them a parent reading the other guardian's thread or ending their link, a student reading a meeting note, a reference letter file or a co-pilot answer, a teacher selecting `case_id` on their own flag or a student's other grades, mentor A reading mentor B's thread, and a school admin reading any welfare class (F15, F58, F59).
- A flagged fixture per role (F13): a reflection, a chat message and a mentor message with a raised safety alert; the student, parent and mentor tiers can select neither the alert nor `safety_flag`.
- Narrowing trigger, the rule of §3.1 exactly (F47): a school row that ranks higher, or ranks the same and differs, is refused; a school row for a key with no default is refused; a `safeguarding` row is refused; with `allow_counselor_widening` off, a counselor `signal` row at school scope is refused, and with it on, accepted.
- Roles and deployables (F01): the web identity cannot `SET ROLE` to system, support or owner; a user tier or the AI tier with `app.actor_kind = 'system'` sees nothing, because pass 1's `auth.allowed()` refuses any non-user actor under those tiers; a connection with no `SET ROLE` reads nothing.
- Support (F35): reads without a grant return zero rows; reads with a masked grant return pseudonyms; an expired grant returns zero rows in the same session; a second operator reading under the first operator's grant returns zero rows; a table not on the grant returns zero rows; the support tier can select no base table.
- Jobs (F36): a job for school A carrying school B's ids writes nothing.

## 4. Audit

### 4.1 What is logged

Pass 1's `audit.action` taxonomy stands and grows. Two rules govern every entry: **`detail` holds identifiers and enumerations only**, validated on insert against a per-action JSON schema in `packages/contracts` (an entry with an unknown key, or a string longer than 64 characters in any value, is refused), and **the actor, the school, the session and the request come from the transaction context**, never from the caller (pass 1's `audit.record()`). Names, note bodies, reasons, message text, email addresses and phone numbers never enter the log; the entry points at the row that holds them, and that row can be erased without breaking the log.

### 4.2 Append-only, and whether tamper evidence is warranted

**What already holds** (pass 1): no tier holds `UPDATE` or `DELETE` on `audit.entry`; a trigger raises on both; the only insert path is `audit.record()`, `SECURITY DEFINER`, owned by `caros_policy`; partitions carry no direct privileges; the application role cannot bypass row security or own tables.

**What remains.** Three actors could still alter the log: the migration role `caros_owner`, which owns the table and could drop the trigger; an Azure administrator, who could restore an altered backup over the live server; and a holder of the break-glass database credential. Each is a CAROS insider or a compromised CAROS credential, and the log is precisely the evidence a school would need against that insider. So **the hash chain is on** (pass 1 open decision 13), and it is anchored outside the database:

1. **Chain.** Pass 1's `audit.chain()` trigger: per school, `entry_hash = sha256(prev_hash ‖ id ‖ occurred_at ‖ action ‖ actor ‖ object ‖ subject ‖ detail)`.
2. **Anchor.** A weekly job writes, per school, `{school_id, as_of, entry_count, head_hash}` to a blob in a container with a **locked time-based retention policy** of ten years in UAE North. Under such a policy "objects can be created and read, but not modified or deleted", a locked policy "can't be deleted" and its retention can be extended but not decreased, and immutability holds "not even [for] users with account administrative privileges" ([Azure immutable storage overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/immutable-storage-overview)). The same head hash is printed in the school's monthly report, so the school holds a copy CAROS cannot reach.
3. **Verification.** Weekly, the job recomputes each school's chain from the last anchor and compares. A mismatch is a P1 incident (§9.6) and the school is told the same day.
4. **DDL and role changes are logged outside the database.** `pgaudit` is supported on Azure Database for PostgreSQL flexible server and can log by statement class; `pgaudit.log` is set to `DDL, ROLE` so that dropping a trigger, altering a policy or granting a privilege lands in Azure Monitor Logs, in a workspace the application identity cannot write to ([Azure, audit logging with pgaudit](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/postgresql/security/security-audit)). `READ` and `WRITE` classes are deliberately **not** enabled: they would copy row contents, including personal data, into a log store with its own retention.
5. **Migrations** run under a dedicated deploy identity (`caros_migrator`), from reviewed SQL, with pass 1's nightly drift check. While Davide is the only builder (decision C8), a migration that touches `audit.*` needs the different-model review pass 6 reserves for it, and human review is asynchronous; the two-approval `CODEOWNERS` rule switches on when a second regular builder joins (F46).

Is it warranted? The log is the record a safeguarding review, an ADEK inspection, a family dispute or a court will ask for. The cost is one digest per row and one small blob per week. A school's IT review will ask whether the vendor's own administrator can edit the log; the answer becomes "not without breaking a chain whose head you already hold". Yes.

### 4.3 Who may read the audit log

| Reader | What | How |
|---|---|---|
| The owning counselor | Every entry about a student in their caseload: their own actions and everyone else's reads, including cover, the lead, the safeguarding lead and CAROS support | The file's "who has looked at this file" panel (the prototype's access audit, made real); step-up not required, because transparency to the owner is the point |
| `caseload_lead`, `school_admin` | The school's whole log, filtered by actor, student, action, period | The audit screen; step-up required; every query writes `AUDIT_ACCESS`, and the screen shows that entry too, so reading the log is visibly an act |
| `safeguarding_lead` | Entries on escalations and on the cases they were given through an escalation | Same screen, `ESC` scope |
| Student, parent | Not the raw log. The subject-access extract (§7.4) may include an access history, **date and role of each reader**, not staff names; it never includes reads under the escalation scope or with purpose `escalation`, because a list of readers can reveal that a referral exists (F14, C4) | Generated by the SAR job; excluded by default, included item by item by the school (§7.4) |
| CAROS support | Under a grant, `AUDIT_ACCESS` logged | The support console |
| CAROS security monitoring | Aggregates over metadata only (action counts by actor, off-hours reads, bulk reads, reads outside caseload under break-glass) run by the worker as `system`, emitting alerts with identifiers only | No human reads an entry without a grant |

### 4.4 The audit log's retention, and erasure

**Proposal for counsel:** audit entries are kept for **seven years after the subject student leaves the school** (entries with no student subject: seven years from `occurred_at`); entries about an escalation follow the `safeguarding` class if that is longer. Rationale: ADEK requires the school to keep student records at least five years after leaving (Records Policy 2.1.3); disputes about access to a child's welfare record can arise years later; seven years is the common commercial limitation horizon. A shorter number leaves the school unable to answer "who read my child's file in Grade 10" when the child is 19.

**Erasure** (§7.3) tombstones the person and scrubs the PII columns of every registered table; `audit.entry` is not in the scrub list because it holds no PII. After erasure the log still says "actor X opened the file of subject `4f1c…` on 12 November for `case_review`", and `4f1c…` resolves to a tombstone: the evidential fact survives, the identity does not. The event stream (`events.event`) follows the same rule.

**At offboarding** the school receives its full log with chain and anchors in the exit pack (§7.5). CAROS proposes to retain its own copy, with all persons tombstoned, for the same seven years as the processor's record of what it did. **Pass 8 states the weakness** (F37): tombstoned entries keep uuids that the school's own copy can resolve, so the copy is pseudonymised, not anonymised, and PDPL Articles 5(7) and 8(4) require data to be anonymised after its purpose and a processor to erase or return it. This is a counsel question (C9, C29), not a settled position.

## 5. Safeguarding escalation, end to end

### 5.1 What the escalation is, and what it is not

Under ADEK's Student Protection Policy v1.1 every member of staff is a mandated reporter: concerns "should be notified to the CPC" (3.2), staff report to the CPC "immediately" (3.5), **each member of staff** must report to the ADEK Child Protection Unit "within 24 hours upon suspicion" (3.1), if the CPC cannot be reached, or it is not in the student's best interest to inform them, staff report directly to the ADEK CPU or the Family Care Authority through the Safety Concern Portal (3.6), and emergencies go to the Police on 999 and the Principal (3.7). Under Wadeema's Law the duty to notify is personal to the educator (Article 42(2)) and its breach is criminal (Article 60). Revised by pass 8 (F09, F55): the first version cited v1.0's numbering and attributed the 24-hour clock to the institution or the CPC; it is each reporter's own.

**CAROS's escalation is the 3.2 and 3.5 step**: a counselor, or since pass 8 a teacher (F10), notifying the school's Child Protection Coordinator (ACS's Lead Child Protection Officer) through a channel that records that it happened, when, to whom, and whether it was picked up. It is **not** the report to the authority, and it does **not** discharge the reporter's own legal duty: the reporter's 24-hour window runs from their own suspicion, whether or not anyone acknowledges. So the screen shows, **from the moment of escalation**, the reporter's own countdown, labelled as the reporter's duty, and the direct-report route, in one sentence that also says the CPC has been notified. The contacts come from two places: the regulator's profile (pass 1 `ref.regulator_profile`, F31), which for an ADEK school lists the Police (999), the Family Care Authority (800444), the Ministry of Education's Child Protection Unit hotline (80085) and the Safety Concern Portal, each with its hours and a last-verified date; and the school's own `core.school.safeguarding_contacts` (the Principal's and the CPO's internal numbers). A tenant whose regulator profile is empty (Wellesmere under KHDA today) shows only the school's own contacts and no regulator-specific text. **No policy clause number appears in any interface text**; the policy is named, and its numbers live in the profile (F55). **CAROS never contacts an authority.** The `urgent` tier's SLA text, "Route now under school policy", is the engine's description of a tier; the escalation is a person's act, and the engine never performs it (pass 3 C7).

### 5.2 Configuration: the route

`core.school.safeguarding_route` (jsonb, validated by a schema in `packages/contracts`):

```json
{
  "recipients": [
    {"membership_id": "…", "order": 1, "ack_sla_minutes": 120},
    {"membership_id": "…", "order": 2, "ack_sla_minutes": 120}
  ],
  "oversight": [{"membership_id": "…"}],
  "clock": "school_hours" | "continuous",
  "unacknowledged_alert_minutes": 1440,
  "leadership_contacts": [{"membership_id": "…", "label": "HS Principal"}],
  "emergency_contacts": {"principal_phone": "…", "cpo_phone": "…"}
}
```

and, beside it, `core.school.safety_alert_routes` (jsonb, pass 8, F09): for each lexicon category, who is told and when, for example `{"suicidal_ideation": {"notify": ["owning_counselor", "leadership_contacts"], "at": "immediately"}, "severe_substance_use": {"notify": ["owning_counselor", "leadership_contacts"], "at": "immediately"}, "maltreatment": {"notify": ["owning_counselor", "route_order_1"], "at": "immediately"}, "self_harm": {"notify": ["owning_counselor", "route_order_1"], "at": "immediately"}, "other": {"notify": ["owning_counselor"], "at": "immediately", "unopened_notice_to": "route_order_1", "unopened_after_school_minutes": 120}}`. The keys are pass 5's route keys: `suicidal_ideation`, `self_harm`, `maltreatment`, `severe_substance_use` and `other`. **Exploitation and grooming map to `maltreatment`**, as pass 5 §6.2's lexicon maps them, and every category the school has not routed separately (eating disorders, other substance use, running away, wanting a person) falls to `other`. The defaults come from the regulator's profile (for ADEK, the Student Mental Health Policy's 3.5.1(a) and (b)), and **the routing targets are the school's configuration, confirmed with ACS's CPO, not hard-coded** (decision on F09; question 151).

Rules: every recipient must hold an active membership with the `safeguarding_lead` capability, on **any staff role** (F10; ACS's CPO may be a principal or a designated teacher, and since pass 8 their capability sits on a `staff` or `teacher` membership, not a counselor's, so it carries no caseload or break-glass path, F16); the route must always name at least two recipients, because ADEK 3.4.2 requires that "a delegate ... always be nominated in case the CPC is unavailable"; oversight members (for example the HS Principal) hold the `safeguarding_oversight` capability, which carries **no data grant**, and receive only an in-app notice that a referral is unacknowledged, never its content (F16; by decision C3 that notice is in-app, not email); leadership contacts are told of the alert categories the school routes to them. Changing the route requires step-up by a `school_admin` and co-signature by a current `safeguarding_lead`, is audited `ROUTE_CHANGED`, and the previous route is kept (`core.school_route_history`). **The route is validated nightly, not only when it changes** (F77): a recipient whose membership has ended or lost the capability raises `ROUTE_VALIDATION_FAILED`, an in-app alert to the `school_admin` and the remaining recipients, and a red line on the counselor's escalation screen until it is fixed. The default clock is `continuous`: safeguarding does not keep office hours, and the recipient can see the referral from a phone; a school may choose `school_hours` if its CPO is not reachable at night, in which case the screen tells the counselor so and shows the direct-report contacts more prominently.

### 5.3 The act, the email, and the acknowledgement

States on `signal.escalation.status`: `raised → notified → acknowledged → closed`, with side states `reminded`, `routed_to_deputy`, `routed_to_oversight`, `unacknowledged_24h`, each an event and an audit entry.

1. **Raise.** From an open case (C15), from a student's file with no open case (a manual case is opened first, C2, so the escalation always hangs off a case), or from a teacher's safeguarding flag (pass 1 C23, F10: the definer function opens the case if none is open, with the student's primary counselor as owner, and the teacher never sees it). Step-up is required. The form replaces the prototype's single twelve-character box (pass 1 open decision 9, resolved: structured, forty characters) with fields stored on `signal.escalation`: `observed` (≥ 40 characters: what was seen or heard), `why_now` (≥ 20), `who_else_knows` (staff picked from the directory, plus a free field), `immediate_risk` (`yes | no | unsure`), `student_aware` (`yes | no`), `parents_informed` (`yes | no | not_appropriate`). The minimums are ACS's to adjust (question 60). Every field is `safeguarding` class. **`immediate_risk = yes` now acts** (F09): the screen shows at once the Police (999) and the Principal's contact from the route's `emergency_contacts`, the whole route and the leadership contacts are notified immediately rather than by the reminder ladder, and `IMMEDIATE_RISK_SHOWN` is written.
2. **Preview.** The screen shows two boxes: *what the recipient's email will say* (the template rendered with real values) and *what the recipient will see after signing in* (the case, its evidence, its notes, the student's file). Nothing is "attached"; the case is shared by the `ESC` scope for the life of the escalation plus 90 days.
3. **Commit.** One transaction: the escalation row, under two rules that together are "cannot double-fire" (F07): **at most one open escalation per case** (a partial unique index, D46) and **one idempotency key per submission**, so one act is never recorded twice; a later escalation on the same case, once the earlier one is closed, is allowed and carries `previous_escalation_id`, because real safeguarding concerns recur. The case moves to tier `urgent` and stage `act` (C15) and is **pinned**: until a person sets the tier after the outcome, the engine may raise it and never lower or close it (pass 1 `signal.case_escalation_pin`, F07). An outbox row per order-1 recipient through `notify.enqueue()` (pass 1 §2.4, F02); `ESCALATED_SAFEGUARDING` with `detail = {escalation_id, case_id, recipient_membership_ids}` (or `TEACHER_REFERRAL` as well, for a teacher's); the event `case.escalated`. The reason is on the escalation row, not in the audit entry (§0.2).
4. **The email.** Template `safeguarding_escalation`, versioned in `notify.template`, whose `variables` allowlist is exactly `{school_short_name, reference, raised_by_role, raised_at_local, url, school_contact_line}` and whose rendering refuses any other variable. **Revised by pass 8 (F60):** the email names the sender's **role** from a fixed list, never a person, and the fallback line is the school's counseling office, not a named counselor, because an email is read in mailboxes and on phones CAROS does not control. Rendered:

   > Subject: [ACS Abu Dhabi] CAROS: a safeguarding referral needs your acknowledgement
   >
   > A counselor has raised a safeguarding referral in CAROS.
   > Reference: ESC-7F3K-2Q
   > Raised at 10:42 today (Abu Dhabi time)
   > Please sign in to read and acknowledge it: https://app.caros.example/s/ESC-7F3K-2Q
   > If you cannot reach CAROS, call the school's counseling office on the internal line.
   > This message deliberately contains no information about the student.

   **Never in the email:** the name of anyone, the raising counselor's or teacher's included; the student's name, initials, year group or id; the tier; the case headline; any reason field; any evidence; the case id (the reference is a random, non-sequential token that resolves only after sign-in, so nothing about volume or identity leaks); any deep link that renders content without authentication. For a teacher's referral the role is "A teacher". Plain text, no images, no tracking. Sent from CAROS's own domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC `p=reject`, §9.5) with the school's short name in the display name only. The prototype's "Student: name · year; Case: headline; Attached: evidence and the full case history" is not ported (§0.2).
5. **In-app** notification to the same recipients, and to the raiser's own screen a status line ("sent 10:42 · not yet acknowledged").
6. **Acknowledge.** The recipient signs in with Google, passes step-up, opens the referral, sees the case under `ESC` scope, and acknowledges. `acknowledged_at`, `acknowledged_by_person_id`, `ESCALATION_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `escalation.acknowledged`; the raiser is told immediately. Acknowledgement means "I have it", not "I agree"; the recipient may add a note (`safeguarding` class) the raiser does not see.
7. **Reminders and routing.** With the default SLA of 120 minutes: at 60 minutes, the `escalation_reminder` email (the same reference-only content) and an in-app notice to the same recipient; at 120 minutes, the order-2 recipient is sent the escalation email and notice (`routed_to_deputy`) and the raiser is told; at 240 minutes, the oversight members receive an **in-app** notice that "a safeguarding referral raised at 10:42 has not been acknowledged" (`routed_to_oversight`; in-app by decision C3). At 24 hours (`unacknowledged_alert_minutes`), `unacknowledged_24h`: a further reminder email to the route, and the raiser's banner turns red and non-dismissible, restating what it has said since the referral was raised: "Your own duty to report does not wait for acknowledgement. You may report directly to the authorities listed below. Record what you did." It names the policy, never a clause number, and lists the contacts from the regulator profile (F55, F31). A "I reported directly" action writes `direct_report_recorded` with the authority and the time (`DIRECT_REPORT_RECORDED`; no detail). **CAROS never reports on anyone's behalf.**
8. **New information while it is open** (pass 1 C24, F07). The raising or owning counselor, or the teacher who raised it, adds an update (`signal.escalation_update`, append-only, `safeguarding` class); the current route receives the `escalation_reminder` email and an in-app notice; `ESCALATION_UPDATED`.
9. **Outcome.** The acknowledging recipient closes the referral with `outcome_kind` from the regulator profile's outcome vocabulary (for ADEK: `reported_to_adek_cpu`, `reported_to_fca`, `reported_to_police`, `handed_to_external_agency`, `managed_in_school`, `no_further_action`, `duplicate_of_existing_referral`; a tenant with an empty profile gets the regulator-neutral subset), plus `reported_at`, `authority_reference` and `outcome_note` (all `safeguarding` class). An escalation with no outcome at 24 hours sends the recipients a reminder email and the oversight members an in-app notice.
10. **After closing.** `ESC` scope persists for 90 days. The case stays pinned until a person sets its tier (pass 1 C10), which releases the pin (`ESCALATION_PIN_RELEASED`); auto-review never closes it inside the `ESC` window (pass 1 C21). The counselor's later notes stay `case_note` class and remain visible to the recipient during that window. A new concern after the referral closes is a new escalation, linked to the first (F07); the first version's "a second escalation on the same case is impossible" is withdrawn.

### 5.4 The content rule as a test

`packages/notify/test/templates.spec.ts` renders every template in §5.8 with a fixture whose variables are all present, and asserts, for every email template: the output contains none of the fixture student's given name, family name, initials or year group, **no person's name at all** (the raiser's, the counselor's, the recipient's or a mentor's; F60), no tier word, headline, `observed` or `why_now` text, evidence text, case id, or topic of a message or meeting; a sender is named only by a role from the fixed list (`a counselor`, `a teacher`, `a mentor`, `the school`), and a safeguarding role (the CPO, the safeguarding lead) only ever as `the school`. For `safeguarding_escalation` and `escalation_reminder` it also asserts the reference and the URL are present. The kinds the first version's content test never reached (the counselor's safety-alert email, the chase to reference writers, the CPO route) are all either in §5.8 with this test or in-app (F12, F28). A template that declares a variable outside its allowlist fails `notify.template`'s CHECK. In-app notices, read after sign-in, may show full names and roles (decision on F60). AI parent-email drafts are in-app messages (pass 1 5.3) and never enter the outbox.

### 5.5 Channels

Email goes through Azure Communication Services Email with data location United Arab Emirates (pass 1 DR-1). **Revised by pass 8 (F60):** the first version called its emails "identifiers only", which was not true of the meeting confirmation (a child's first name and a meeting with the counselor) or the deadline nudge (an institution and a date); those land in mailboxes outside the UAE, and Microsoft says content "may transit or be processed in other geographies". Every email is now content-free by §5.8, so that note costs nothing. Delivery status is **polled** from the service rather than received through Event Grid, whose events carry the recipient address in a global topic. **SMS to the recipient is not available in v1:** Azure Communication Services lists the United Arab Emirates for toll-free inbound calling only, with no SMS send or receive capability ([Microsoft, phone number management for the UAE](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/communication-services/concepts/numbers/phone-number-management-for-united-arab-emirates)), and decision C3 keeps every notice beyond the declared emails in-app, so open decision 8 (a third-party SMS provider) is closed for v1. Azure Monitor action groups do support SMS and voice to +971 ([Microsoft, action groups](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/alerts/action-groups)) and are the channel for pass 3's operational on-call alerts (§9.6), not for referrals; their processing is global, so their payloads carry counts, never student ids (F60).

### 5.6 Who sees what about an escalation

Governed by ADEK Student Protection Policy v1.1, 4.2 ("shared only with authorized individuals") and Wadeema Article 44 (the reporter's identity). The raising counselor or teacher sees the status, acknowledgement and outcome kind of their own referral, through `signal.v_escalation_status`, never the recipient's notes. Recipients in the route see everything while active and for 90 days after outcome. The `caseload_lead` sees that a referral exists, its status and its owner, through the same view, not its content. Support never sees it without the special flag. Students and parents never see it. **A subject-access request about a student with an escalation or a safety alert is routed to the safeguarding lead, not the caseload lead** (F14, decision C4), and the pack excludes by default every `safeguarding`-class row, anything linked to an escalation or a safety alert (including `ESCALATED_SAFEGUARDING` audit rows, `case.escalated` events and meetings whose outcome is `escalate`), and every read made under the escalation scope or with purpose `escalation`, because a routine request by a parent who may be the subject of the concern could otherwise tip them off; inclusion of any such item is the school's explicit act, item by item (§7.4; counsel C8). A teacher whose flag fed the case is identified to the recipient inside CAROS and never in any family-facing output; SAR packs redact flag authorship by default. Nothing in an escalation is ever exportable except by a recipient, under step-up, for a statutory referral, watermarked.

### 5.7 Student disclosures and the student's own reporting channel

Pass 5 designs the lexicon; this pass sets the route. Rewritten by pass 8 for decisions C1 and on F09, F11, F12 and F13.

**The screen is a lexicon, with no model** (decision C1, settling F03). It reads a discovery message, a reflection, a Personal Statement Lab draft, an Extended Essay rationale, a mentor message, and since pass 8 a teacher flag's text (F10); the list is the lexicon version's `screened_kinds` (pass 5 D68). **Every lexicon hit creates an alert that reaches a person**; nothing closes a hit or lowers its urgency except a person's recorded disposition, and there is no `cleared_by_model` state. Alert load is the lexicon's problem, owned by the CPO (pass 5 question 129), not a reason to add a model. An add-only model layer, which could only add an alert or raise urgency, is revisited after one term, once the CPO has seen the false-positive rate.

**The alert** is `signal.safety_alert` (pass 5 D68), in its own staff-only class `safety_alert` (sensitivity 4), not `student_voice` (F13): the student, a parent using the child's phone, or a mentor must never learn that an alert was raised about their words, so the student and mentor tiers cannot select the alert or any `safety_flag` or extraction column (pass 1 §2.22). Each alert keeps the lexicon version, the category, the matched span and the rule that fired, and is retained with the welfare class, so a past alert stays explainable after the lexicon changes (invariant 4, F48).

**Routing by category** (F09; ADEK Student Mental Health Policy 3.5.1). The school's `safety_alert_routes` (§5.2) decide who is told and when, from defaults in the regulator profile, and the school's CPO confirms them before any student sees the screen: for suicidal ideation and severe substance use, the owning counselor and the named leadership contact **immediately**; for maltreatment and potential self-harm, the owning counselor and the CPO **immediately**; otherwise the owning counselor, carrying the `urgent` tier's clock. Each person told receives an in-app alert and the reference-only **safety-alert email** ("a student safety alert needs your attention; sign in to CAROS", which fits an alert raised on a student's words and one raised on a teacher's flag alike, F10), kept by decision C3 because safety needs reach when a person is not signed in. The routing is written as `SAFETY_ALERT_ROUTED`, so pattern-only routing is no longer unaudited (F12).

**The CPO receives a notice, never an automatic referral** (F12, option b). If an alert that did not already go to the CPO is not opened within two school hours, the first recipient of the safeguarding route receives the reference-only **alert-unopened notice** (in-app and by email): an alert about a student has not been opened. It creates no `signal.escalation`, records no reason, and cannot double-fire: at most one notice per alert, and none for an alert the CPO already holds. A person, the counselor or the CPO, then decides whether to escalate (§5.3), and a counselor's escalation of the same concern links the alert (`escalation_id`). `ALERT_UNOPENED_NOTICE_SENT`.

**What the student sees** is a fixed, school-approved message, written by the school, never by a model, with the counselor's contact and the school's list of helplines. **The list is split, and the checked numbers are a draft** (decision on F11): "someone to talk to now" lists only lines documented as 24/7 (currently 800-SAKINA; for a Dubai school, DFWAC 800111), and "to report that a child is being harmed" lists the reporting lines (116111, the Family Care Authority's 800444, and for Dubai the Community Development Authority's 800988), each with its hours. The message never says "call any time" unless every line listed is 24/7, and Estijaba (8001717) is left out until a current source shows it offers support. **ACS's CPO confirms the list before any student sees it**, and each number stores its last-verified date (`verified_at` in the regulator profile's and the school's contacts; question 129, pass 5 handoff).

**The student's own channel.** Every student screen carries an "I need to talk to someone" action that creates a `signal.student_reflection` marked `urgent_request`, routed like an alert of the `other` category but immediately; it is never sent to a model or an extraction (pass 5 D69, F21). That is the Child Digital Safety Law's "reporting channel" made concrete. **Mentor messages** with grooming indicators route to the `mentor_coordinator` by the reference-only grooming-alert email and to the safeguarding lead in-app (§2.3, C3).

## 6. AI and residency: the rules

Pass 5 designs the mechanism. This section owns the rule: what may leave the UAE, in what form, to whom, under what retention, and what may never leave. The facts it stands on are in §1.6.

### 6.1 The position

**A model call is a cross-border transfer.** Nothing is stored abroad under the arrangement in §1.6, but the prompt is processed in the United States, and processing is a transfer under the PDPL's Articles 22 and 23. **Corrected by pass 8 (F37):** the first version took "contractual safeguards" as the primary basis and treated Anthropic's EU standard contractual clauses as the PDPL's "standard contract clauses". Article 23's contract route is narrower: it is limited to countries without a data protection law and binds the recipient to the PDPL's own provisions and a supervisory authority there, which EU clauses do not do. The plan therefore does not claim a settled basis. Express consent per family, necessity, and a contract drafted to Article 23's own terms are all put to counsel (C3, revised), and the school's DPO chooses once counsel answers (open decision 4).

**ADEK's consent comes first** (F04). ADEK Digital Policy 7.1.3.a requires the school's contract with CAROS to forbid any sharing of personal data, inside or outside the country, without ADEK's explicit consent. **ADEK consent is a named gate before G-AI** (and before any EU cold copy, §9.7): no AI feature runs on real student data until ADEK has consented to the transfer, whatever the PDPL basis. The sequence decided on 2026-09-24: ask ACS how it has handled 7.1.3.a with ManageBac and Veracross (question 149), then draft the ADEK request and its evidence pack (question 150), raised with ACS this term so that the April 2027 G-REAL target is not held by it (pass 6 handoff).

**Two consequences follow, and both are structural.** First, every AI feature sits behind a per-school module flag (`core.school_module`) and a per-feature policy row (`ai.feature_policy`, §10) that the school enables in writing, so that a school, a regulator or a change in the law can turn the model off without touching anything else. Second, because the trust spine already says rules decide and the model only writes, **CAROS with the AI switched off is still CAROS**: the engine, the queue, the evidence chain, the file, the teacher loop, the IB module and the reporting all run without a model. The pilot's shadow phase can and should run with the AI features off (open decision 5).

### 6.5 The fairness audit's legal basis

**Decided 2026-09-24 (C11): no special-category label is collected during the pilot.** The pass 7 review computed that at pilot scale the audit has almost no power (two groups of 175 at 20% against 16% give about 16% power), while a group of 30 crosses the 0.8 to 1.25 thresholds about half the time by chance (F68). So the audit runs only once pooled data across schools gives it power, and the pilot documents say plainly that fairness by nationality, gender, language background and SEN is untested during the pilot. What follows is the basis for when it does run.

Pass 3 §12.4 needs nationality, gender, language background and SEN status held apart from everything else, for one purpose. All four are sensitive under the PDPL's Article 1 (racial origin and health, with nationality treated as sensitive by policy). No PDPL article names bias testing as a purpose, and the Executive Regulations that might are not issued. The recommendation for counsel (question C4): the school processes the labels as controller under its own obligation to protect students from discriminatory treatment (the Student Protection Policy's definition of **safeguarding** reaches "other types of risks that impact their overall health and development, wellbeing, and safety", and an unfair alerting tool is such a risk; corrected by pass 8, F55, which found the first version had quoted this as the definition of maltreatment), with explicit custodian consent as the fallback basis per label; CAROS processes them on instruction under the EU AI Act Article 10(5) pattern adopted contractually: purpose limited to bias detection and correction, access limited to the audit job's role (no application tier and not the engine, pass 3 D26), pseudonymised join with small-cell suppression, aggregate output only with intervals, a few comparisons pre-registered and pooled across quarters with a multiple-comparisons rule (F68), deletion of the working table after each run and of the labels within a year or when the school withdraws them, and documentation of every run.

### 6.6 Measuring a minor's platform activity: the engagement rule

Pass 3 designed the engagement domain and shipped it off; pass 2 made Google Classroom a connector this pass must approve. **Rule:** the engagement domain may be enabled for a school only when all of the following hold: (1) the school enables it in writing (`ai.feature_policy` is the wrong table; it is `core.school_module 'engagement'` with `enabled_by`, `enabled_at`, `notice_version_id`, the D60 name, §12.7) and the student and parent notices describe it in plain words ("CAROS notices when a student who usually uses the portal or turns in Classroom work stops, and tells the counselor"); (2) sources are limited to CAROS session events and Google Classroom submission states and timestamps, read through a school-created reader account with read-only scopes under domain-wide delegation (pass 2 §7.6), with no content, comments, attachments or announcement text; (3) the engine sees dates, not times: `engagement.activity_event` gains `occurred_on date` and the engine reads only that column (pass 3 already removed the night measure; this makes its return impossible without a schema change); (4) retention is 180 days rolling (pass 1); (5) a student can see their own activity summary in the portal, so the measurement is visible to the measured; (6) the DPA states that engagement signals are never used for discipline; (7) the domain's banner (pass 3 §10.3) shows on the caseload sheet whenever it is on, naming the sources. If ACS declines (question 14), the domain stays off with the visible banner and nothing else changes.

## 7. Retention and erasure

Every number in this section is a **proposal for counsel** and is written into `privacy.retention_class.proposed_days` (or `proposed_rule` where the trigger is a birthday) so that counsel's decision is a configuration change with an audit entry, not a migration. A school may differ from the platform schedule through `privacy.retention_override` (pass 1 §2.21, F65), within the minimums of its regulator's profile; the schedule below is reasoned from ADEK's rules and is the ADEK profile's default, not every school's. The governing idea: **CAROS is not the school's system of record for anything ADEK requires the school to keep.** Enrolment, academic achievement, daily attendance and disciplinary records live in Veracross and are the school's five-year records, and graduation records its indefinite ones (ADEK Records Policy 2.1.3 and 2.1.3.a). CAROS holds copies of those for the engine, and it is the *only* record of the things it creates: cases, notes, interventions, escalations, teacher flags, student voice, mentor messages. Retention for copies follows the product's need; retention for CAROS-native welfare records follows the record's value to the child and the school's obligations.

### 7.2 What the law may require keeping, as far as this pass can find

- **ADEK Records Policy 2.1.3:** the school keeps enrolment, academic achievement, daily attendance and disciplinary records at least five years after leaving and graduation records indefinitely. These are the school's records; CAROS's copies may be shorter as long as the school's system of record holds them (an export before deletion is always available).
- **Child protection cases** are a required record category in the same policy, but the five-year clause does not name them (corrected by pass 8, F37); no ADEK minimum for them was found. The Student Protection Policy requires confidentiality of "open and closed cases" but sets no retention.
- **The PDPL** forbids keeping data after its purpose unless it is anonymised (Article 5(7)), requires a processor to erase or return data at the end of processing (Article 8(4)), and sets no minimums (corrected by pass 8 from the first version's "Article 7 principles", F37).
- **The ICT Health Law** requires health data to be kept 25 years (Article 20), which binds only if counselor records are health data (counsel C30).
- **Limitation periods** for civil claims under the new Civil Transactions Law, and any specific rule for claims by a person who was a minor, are counsel's question (C10); they may lengthen `welfare` and `safeguarding`.
- **CAROS's own commercial records** (invoices, contracts) follow UAE commercial and tax law and hold no student data.

### 7.3 Erasure, with an append-only audit log, immutable raw files and backups

**Who decides.** The school, as controller. Requests arrive at the school directly or through a portal form ("ask the school to correct or delete my data") that notifies the school's data protection lead; CAROS never decides to erase. `privacy.erasure_request` (pass 1) records the request, the identity verification (a portal session, or the school's own check), the school's decision and its legal basis or refusal reason. Grounds for refusal the screen offers: an open case or escalation; a retention the school is obliged to keep; a legal claim; the student is still enrolled (erasure of a current student's record would defeat the school's duty of care; rectification and restriction remain available).

**Grace.** An accepted request is scheduled 30 days out (`scheduled_for`), so an objection or a change of mind can stop it, and so that the point-in-time backup window begins to roll past the request.

**Execution** is one job, one transaction per table, all inside `withTenant()` as `system`, logged per table in `privacy.erasure_log`:

1. `core.person`: `given_name`, `family_name`, `preferred_name` set to the literal `[erased]`; `email`, `phone`, `google_subject`, `language_pref` set to NULL; `status = 'erased'`, `erased_at`. `core.person_name_history` (pass 2) deleted. The uuid stays.
2. For every table in `privacy.table_registry` whose subject or person column matches: scrub `pii_columns`, delete rows whose class says `delete`, anonymise rows whose class says `anonymise` (free-text columns set to NULL; structural columns kept). Cases, evidence snapshots (`snapshot` jsonb text fields), notes, teacher flag bodies, student voice, messages and generations lose their text; tiers, dates, rule keys and identifiers remain for aggregate reporting.
3. `doc.file` rows for the subject: blob soft-deleted, versions deleted, hard purge after the 30-day soft-delete window; the row keeps `sha256` and `byte_size` for the destruction statement and loses `original_filename`.
4. `ai.pseudonym_map` rows for the subject's generations deleted; `ai.generation.output_text` set to NULL.
5. `sis.external_identity` rows deleted, and a salted hash of each `(system, external_id)` written to `privacy.erased_external_identity` (§10) so that a later historical import cannot re-create the person: pass 2's identity ladder checks this table at rung 1 and refuses with `erased_subject`.
6. `audit.entry` and `events.event`: **untouched.** They hold uuids only; after step 1 the uuid resolves to a tombstone.
7. `ERASURE_EXECUTED`, and a destruction statement (tables, row counts, blob count, executed at) delivered to the school in-app for its own records (ADEK Records 1.4.2.b asks schools for "proof of evidence of destruction").
8. The non-tenant stores in pass 1's `privacy.non_tenant_store` (F36): queued and completed jobs naming the subject are deleted (their payloads hold ids only, and completed jobs expire in seven days anyway), and the email provider's delivery-status records follow the provider's own retention, stated in the DPA.

**What erasure leaves, stated plainly** (F37). A tombstone keeps the uuid and, in `privacy.erased_external_identity`, a salted hash of each external id. That is pseudonymisation, not anonymisation: the school's own copy of the uuid mapping could re-identify the row. PDPL Article 5(7) permits keeping data after its purpose only when it is anonymised, so whether tombstones and the salted-hash register satisfy it is a counsel question (C29); the design keeps them because without them a later historical import would re-create the erased person.

**Backups.** The point-in-time store holds the data for up to 35 days after execution; erasure is therefore complete in live systems immediately and in the operational backups within 35 days, which the DPA states. Long-term vaulted copies hold it for their retention (§7.1). Two rules make that acceptable: vaulted backups are encrypted, offline, and restorable only to a scratch server in a drill or an incident (pass 1 DR-9); and **every restore replays `privacy.erasure_log`**: the restore runbook's last step re-executes every erasure request whose `executed_at` is later than the backup's timestamp before the restored server is used for anything. That step is scripted, tested in the quarterly drill (pass 1 drill 3 gains an erased fixture person), and its omission blocks the drill's sign-off.

**Immutable raw imports.** A raw export in the WORM container cannot be edited or deleted before its 90-day retention expires (pass 1 DR-9; [Azure immutable storage](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/immutable-storage-overview)). Erasure of one student's rows inside such a file is impossible before expiry. Position: the retention is short (90 days) precisely so that this is tolerable; the DPA states that raw export files are retained unaltered for up to 90 days; and the raw-import container of each tenant uses a **per-tenant encryption scope with a customer-managed key** (§9.2) so that at offboarding the whole container becomes unreadable at once by destroying the key, which is the only way to "erase" WORM content early (Azure: if the key protecting an encryption scope is revoked, "the data will become inaccessible"; [encryption scopes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/encryption-scope-overview)). Pass 2's staged rows follow their 30-day class and are ordinary rows, scrubbed normally.

**Logs.** Application and security logs hold identifiers only (§9.4), so erasure does not reach them and does not need to. **Email.** The outbox holds `recipient_address` snapshots; those are `pii_columns` and are scrubbed. **The AI provider** holds nothing at rest under ZDR; content retained by its trust-and-safety process is pseudonymous and outside CAROS's reach, which the notices say.

### 7.4 Subject access requests, and the other rights

**Rewritten by pass 8 for decision C4 (which settles F14).** The first version included counselor notes, engine evaluations, AI generations and a list of readers by default, which meant a routine request by a parent who may be the subject of a concern could reveal that a referral exists. It also rested on a reading of the PDPL's access right broader than the text: Article 13 is a right to **information** about the processing, with refusal grounds where a request conflicts with investigations by competent authorities (13(3)(b)) or affects others' data (13(3)(d)), and it does not expressly require copies (F37). Until counsel answers C7 and C8, the pack below is the default.

**Route.** The school is the controller and receives the request: from a parent for a child under 18, or from the student, who **after 18 is the only person who may request** about themselves (F14). The portal offers a form that files a `privacy.sar_request` (§10) and notifies the school's data protection lead; requests made to the school directly are entered by the `caseload_lead` or `school_admin`. **A request about a student who has, or has had, an escalation or a safety alert is routed to the safeguarding lead, not the caseload lead**, because the caseload lead sees only an escalation's existence and status and cannot judge what the pack would reveal (§5.6). CAROS's service level to the school: the pack is available within **five working days**, leaving the school three weeks of a one-month response period for review (DLA Piper's reading; the period is not in the law's text).

**The pack, by default.** For one student: a machine-readable JSON (the PDPL's portability right, Article 14) and a readable PDF, generated by the worker from `privacy.table_registry` so no table can be forgotten, organised by data class with the class explained in plain words, and including the student's educational record (directory, academic, attendance, IB, university, discovery proposals and roadmaps), the sources (which import, which system, when), and the notices the family acknowledged. **Excluded by default** (decision C4): every professional record, meaning counselor notes, meeting notes and the contact log, engine evaluations, signals, tiers and evidence, and AI generations about the student; **anything linked to an escalation or a safety alert**, including `ESCALATED_SAFEGUARDING` audit rows, `case.escalated` events, safety alerts, and meetings whose outcome is `escalate`; and the **access history**, whose list of readers could itself show that a safeguarding lead read the file, together with every read made under the escalation scope or with purpose `escalation`, which is never included at all. `safeguarding`-class rows are excluded as before (ADEK Student Protection Policy v1.1, 4.1 to 4.3; counsel C8).

**The school's review.** The pack is not released by CAROS. The reviewer (the caseload lead, or the safeguarding lead where the route above sends it) sees the default pack and, beside it, the excluded items; **including any professional record is an explicit act, item by item**, recorded with a reason from `config.vocabulary 'sar_inclusion_reason'` and audited. The reviewer also redacts third-party data in what is included (a teacher's authorship of a flag, another student's name in free text, the other parent's contact details, a mentor's identity), choosing a reason from `config.vocabulary 'sar_redaction_reason'` (`third_party_data`, `safeguarding_confidentiality`, `legal_privilege`, `professional_opinion_policy`, `other_child`). Teacher authorship is always redacted (Wadeema Article 44). This per-item inclusion is the one path by which staff-only material can reach a family, and it is declared as a departure in §0.1.

**Delivery.** Inside the portal to the requesting guardian's or student's session, never by email; watermarked with the requester and time; downloadable for 30 days; `SAR_DELIVERED`; the generated pack is deleted after 30 days.

**Rectification.** Facts imported from the SIS are corrected in the SIS and re-imported (pass 2's supersession); CAROS-native records are corrected by a superseding row (pass 1's `supersedes_note_id`), never edited in place. **Restriction and objection.** `privacy.processing_restriction` (§10): a row per person and class; while a `signal` restriction is active the engine skips the student (pass 3 gains the hook: `sis.student.processing_restricted_domains text[]`), the case is frozen with a visible banner "processing restricted at the family's request, under review", the co-pilot excludes the student, and exports exclude them; the school lifts it or turns it into an erasure. **Automated decisions** (PDPL Article 18): the engine proposes a tier and a person decides (invariant 3, pass 3); the notice says so, and the file's "why" panel is the human-readable explanation the right asks for.

### 7.5 Offboarding a school

Thirty days before the end date the school receives its exit pack, **delivered in the school's portal to its `school_admin` under step-up, never by email or a shared link** (F76): every student's SAR-format pack, every table as JSON Lines, every document, the audit log with its chain and anchors, the configuration and rule-set history, and the erasure log. During those thirty days the tenant's status is `offboarding` and it is read-only (pass 1 §2.3's status model, F36). On the end date: rows deleted, blobs purged, the tenant's **encryption scope key destroyed** in Key Vault (which renders the raw-import and document containers unreadable even inside the soft-delete windows), the status set to `offboarded` (the `core.school` row itself is kept, holding no personal data, because audit and events keep its id), `TENANT_OFFBOARDED`, and a destruction certificate with counts and the key-destruction record delivered to the school. Backups age out (35 days operational; the vaulted copies per §7.1), and the restore runbook replays offboarding for that tenant on any restore. CAROS proposes to retain the tombstoned audit copy under §4.4, subject to counsel (C9, C29).

## 8. Threat model

Method: STRIDE over the trust boundaries (Microsoft's six categories: spoofing, tampering, repudiation, information disclosure, denial of service, elevation of privilege; [Microsoft threat modeling](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/security/develop/threat-modeling-tool-threats)), with the OWASP Top 10:2025 and the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025 as checklists. Boundaries: browser to Application Gateway; web and worker to PostgreSQL (the tenant context); worker to external systems (Veracross, ManageBac, Classroom, the AI provider, email); school to CAROS (file uploads, SFTP); CAROS operators to production (support console, Azure); the build pipeline to the registry. The asset is a minor's welfare record under a real school's name; the adversaries are an external attacker, a compromised or curious member of school staff, a malicious parent, student or mentor, a CAROS insider, a compromised dependency, and an AI provider that is honest but retains. Each threat below names the controls that exist (with the pass that introduced them), what detects a failure, the residual risk, and the test that proves the control.

## 9. The platform baseline

### 9.1 Transport

TLS 1.2 minimum, TLS 1.3 preferred, at Application Gateway WAF v2 in UAE North, with end-to-end TLS to Container Apps (pass 1 chose the regional gateway over Front Door precisely so that no request is decrypted outside the country). HSTS with a one-year max-age and preload; CAA records naming the certificate authority; certificates issued and renewed through Key Vault. PostgreSQL connections require TLS (`require_secure_transport = on`, certificate verification in the driver); Blob and Key Vault are HTTPS only; every outbound connector call verifies certificates and pins the host allowlist (SSRF control: a connector may reach only the hosts in `ingest.source_system.config.allowed_hosts`).

**Leaks from the browser itself** (pass 8, F76). Fonts are self-hosted and the Content Security Policy sets `font-src 'self'`, because a runtime font load from Google would send every student's IP address to Google. The escalation form, case notes, reflections and the Personal Statement Lab set `spellcheck="false"`, and the IT checklist gives the school a Chrome policy line that turns off enhanced spell check and blocks grammar extensions on staff machines, because both send typed text to their providers. The DPA's school-side annex lists this (§1.4).

### 9.2 Encryption at rest and key management

Azure encrypts everything at rest by default with service-managed keys. **Decision: customer-managed keys (CMK) in Azure Key Vault (Premium, HSM-backed) for production**, chosen at creation because "You can select the mode only at server creation time. You can't change the mode from one to another for the lifetime of the server" ([Azure Database for PostgreSQL, data encryption](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/postgresql/security/security-data-encryption)); pass 6 must place this in week one. What CMK buys: the school can be told that CAROS holds the key to its own data and can destroy it; separation of duties between the people who run the database and the people who hold keys; and a crypto-shredding lever ("You can remove a key to make a database inaccessible", same page). What it costs: operational care. The requirements from the same page are adopted as configuration: Key Vault soft delete with 90-day retention and purge protection; the RBAC permission model with the server's user-assigned managed identity holding *Key Vault Crypto Service Encryption User*; public access disabled with the trusted-services bypass; automatic key-version updates through a versionless key URI; a resource lock on the vault; a key backup escrowed (inside the UAE: Key Vault backups restore only within the same Azure geography, which is why the EU cold copy of §9.7 needs key material held outside the UAE if it is chosen, F22); and, because geo-redundant backup is on, a second key in a Key Vault in UAE Central with its own identity ("create the geo-redundant backup encryption key in a Key Vault instance that must exist in the region where the geo-redundant backup is stored"). A server that loses its key becomes `Inaccessible` within about an hour, so Resource Health and Activity Log alerts on key access failures page the on-call.

Storage: account-level CMK for every storage account, and **per-tenant encryption scopes** on the raw-import and document containers, each scope protected by a customer-managed key with automatic version updates (up to 10,000 auto-updating scopes per account; a scope cannot be deleted but can be disabled, and revoking its key makes the data inaccessible; [Azure encryption scopes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/encryption-scope-overview)). This is what makes offboarding a key destruction (§7.5) and what stops a misconfigured SAS from being readable across tenants (T1).

Application-level encryption, AES-256-GCM with a per-tenant data key wrapped by the tenant's Key Vault key: `ai.pseudonym_map.mapping_enc`, passkey credential metadata and TOTP seeds, activation-code and magic-link hashes are already hashes and need no encryption. Rotation: Key Vault rotation policy rotates the CMK yearly, with automatic version pickup; per-tenant data keys rotate yearly by re-wrapping; TLS certificates rotate automatically. Every rotation is an entry in the ops log and in the school's monthly report.

### 9.3 Secrets and credentials

No secret in code, in an environment variable, or in the repository: managed identities for every Azure-to-Azure call, Key Vault references for everything else, and GitHub Actions authenticating to Azure through OIDC federated credentials so that no long-lived deployment secret exists. Third-party credentials live in Key Vault, per tenant where they are per tenant, referenced from `ingest.source_system.config.secret_ref` (pass 1: "never secrets; those live in the key vault by reference"), and rotate on a schedule that is also a checklist item at offboarding: Veracross OAuth client credentials (tokens live one hour; the client secret is rotated yearly and when the school's `OAuth_App_Admin` changes; the connector's `verify()` refuses a token whose scopes reach student alerts, which carry family and medical text, or the Health resource, F54); Veracross's Data Export Package SFTP password, if the school delivers the weekly pack that way (rotated every 90 days, §9.10, F54); ManageBac read-only tokens (rotated each school year, revoked in ManageBac at offboarding); the Google service-account key for domain-wide delegation (rotated every 90 days, restricted to the delegated scopes; the reader account it acts as holds the least Workspace role that works, which for Classroom is a domain administrator or a teacher of every course read, and for the Directory read is to be confirmed with ACS IT under question 110, §2.1, F74); the Anthropic API key (per environment, rotated quarterly, with `allowed_inference_geos` enforced at the workspace); SFTP local-user SSH keys (per tenant, rotated yearly). Secret scanning runs in CI (gitleaks) and GitHub push protection is on. A leaked credential is a P2 incident (§9.6) and the rotation runbook is the first response.

### 9.4 Logs free of personal data

Structured logging (pino) through one serializer that emits only allowlisted fields: request id, session id hash, person uuid, school uuid, acting role, route, status, latency, error code, and Zod error *paths* (never values). Bodies, query strings, headers, email addresses and names never reach a log line; a lint rule forbids passing an object to the logger outside the serializer, and a CI job runs the application against both synthetic tenants and greps the captured logs for every name and email in the seed. Log Analytics retention: 90 days for application logs, one year for security logs (sign-in, grant, key access, DDL). Alerts to the ops channel carry counts and job identifiers, never student ids: the first version's `sweep.partial` alert with student ids is changed (F60), and the channel's provider is named in §1.3 (Azure Monitor action groups). `pgaudit` logs `DDL` and `ROLE` only (§4.2). Application Gateway WAF logs contain client IP addresses, which are personal data in some readings; they are kept 30 days. The AI gateway logs `generation_id`, model, token counts and `inference_geo`, never prompt or output text.

### 9.5 Email authentication

The sending domain is CAROS's own, with SPF, 2048-bit DKIM and DMARC at `p=reject` with aggregate reporting, configured on the Azure Communication Services custom domain (pass 1). The display name carries the school's short name; the address never impersonates the school's domain. Every message is plain text and identifiers-only (§5.4), which is also what lets staff be told: "an email that names a student is not from CAROS".

### 9.6 Incident response and breach notification

**Severities.** P1: confirmed exposure of student data, an audit-chain mismatch, or a safeguarding referral that could not be delivered. P2: suspected exposure, a leaked credential, a vulnerability with a plausible path to data. P3: a vulnerability without exposure. P4: availability.

**Process.** Detect (alerts in §9.4 and pass 3 §13.8; a monitored security mailbox and vulnerability disclosure policy) → contain (revoke sessions, rotate the affected credential, disable the feature or the connector, isolate the tenant if needed) → assess (which schools, which students, which classes; a scratch restore for forensics if the live database cannot be trusted) → **notify the school within 24 hours of becoming aware** (the DPA clock; Anthropic gives CAROS 48 hours, so CAROS's own clock to the school is tighter than its supplier's), with what is known, what is not, and what has been done, then every 24 hours → the school decides and acts as controller: notification to the Federal Authority for AI and Data (which absorbed the Data Office in June 2026) "immediately" under PDPL Article 9 (the procedure the Executive Regulations will define), notification to ADEK under the Digital Policy (6.6 requires a protocol for reporting incidents to ADEK and 6.6.1 bars communication to anyone but the provider and ADEK), and notification to families, which PDPL 9(2) requires for a prejudicial breach and ADEK 6.6.1 appears to bar; the conflict is flagged to the school in every incident and put to counsel (C27, F37); CAROS provides the evidence pack (affected records by class, the audit entries, the timeline) and drafts nothing to families itself → recover → a written post-incident review within ten working days, shared with the affected schools → fixes tracked to closure. **Exercises:** a tabletop with the pilot school's IT and safeguarding lead before the first real record, and yearly after.

**On-call.** During the pilot, Davide; pass 6 sets the rota. Azure Monitor action groups deliver email, SMS and voice to +971 numbers ([Microsoft, action groups](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/alerts/action-groups)) with count-only payloads, for the sweep watchdog (pass 3), key-access failures, chain mismatches, WAF anomalies and the security mailbox. Action-group SMS has no acknowledgement and no escalation policy (its replies are only DISABLE, ENABLE, STOP, START and HELP), so a small job pages the second person if an alert is still firing after 15 minutes (F45; pass 6 handoff).

### 9.7 Backups, durability and the out-of-country question

Pass 1 DR-9 stands: 35-day point-in-time restore, geo-redundant backup to UAE Central, vaulted long-term backups, quarterly drills. This pass adds: backups are encrypted under the customer-managed keys of both regions; every restored server runs the RLS suite and the erasure replay before use (§7.3); and the vaulted yearly copies are proposed at three years (§7.1).

**Open decision 1 (pass 1): an encrypted out-of-country cold copy.** Position: **recommend it, conditionally.** The AWS UAE region's destruction in March 2026 and the loss of data that existed only there (pass 1 DR-1) is the demonstrated scenario an in-country-only design cannot survive. **Corrected by pass 8 (F22).** The first version proposed a nightly encrypted dump to an EU region under a key that lives only in the UAE Managed HSM. That copy could not be restored in the disaster it exists for: if both UAE regions are lost, a key that exists only there is lost too, and Key Vault backups restore only within the same Azure geography. The real choice for ACS and counsel is:

- **(a) EU ciphertext with key material held outside the UAE**: a Managed HSM replica in an EU region, or security-domain shares held abroad by named people; a named restore region (Sweden Central or West Europe) and a named trigger (a declared loss of both UAE regions, with the school's notice); and an EU restore drill on synthetic data before the copy holds anything real. The copy and the means to read it would both sit outside the UAE; or
- **(b) in-country only**, with the country-level risk stated in the DPA.

Either way, **ADEK's explicit consent under Digital Policy 7.1.3.a comes first** (F04): (a) cannot start without it, and (b) does not need it for the copy. Legally, ciphertext is still personal data under most readings, and with the key abroad it is plainly a transfer under Article 23 (counsel C12). Question 62 asks ACS; the recommendation is (a) if ACS, ADEK and counsel all agree in writing, and (b) otherwise. The infrastructure is small; the decision is the school's, ADEK's and counsel's.

### 9.9 The WAF, and when

Pass 1 open decision 3: (a) from the first deploy, (b) from the first real data, (c) after the review asks. **Decision: (b).** The synthetic-only months run without the WAF's largest-line cost; the WAF goes in front of production two weeks before the first real import, runs the OWASP Core Rule Set in detection mode for those two weeks to tune false positives against the real screens, then switches to prevention. Rate limiting and bot protection are on from day one of real data. No geographic blocking (families travel). The support console and administrative routes are additionally restricted by IP allowlist and by the Entra PIM requirement.

### 9.10 SFTP and connector hardening

If the weekly pack arrives by SFTP (pass 2 open decisions 2 and 17), it is Azure Blob's native SFTP: it requires a hierarchical-namespace account, authenticates **local users** by SSH key by default, scopes permissions per container, and supports home directories and ACLs; note that "SFTP is a platform level service, so port 22 will be open even if the account option is disabled" and that the account firewall applies to all endpoints ([Azure, SFTP support for Blob Storage](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/secure-file-transfer-protocol-support)). Configuration per tenant: one local user, one container, permissions `Create` and `Write` only (no `Read`, `List` or `Delete`), so a compromised school credential can neither read past files nor anyone else's; the school's egress IP allow-listed; host keys published to the school for pinning; keys rotated yearly. **Revised by pass 8 (F54, §12.15):** Veracross's Data Export Package, a paid module, can deliver the weekly pack itself on a schedule, but its SFTP destination takes a URL, a username and a password, with no key authentication documented. For that path CAROS accepts password SFTP on that tenant's local user (an Azure-generated password), with the Veracross egress IP allow-listed, the password rotated every 90 days and held in Key Vault, and the same write-only permissions. Blob SFTP costs $0.30 an hour per SFTP-enabled account (pass 1 cost table, F71). Whether the feature is offered in UAE North could not be confirmed from the documentation (the region list is not on the page); pass 6 verifies in the portal, and the fallback is pass 2's small SFTP container. Connectors egress through a NAT gateway with a static IP so that iSAMS and Veracross allow-lists can name it (pass 2 §13), hold their credentials per tenant in Key Vault (§9.3), and may reach only allow-listed hosts (§9.1).

## Challenges

Each names the decision or the prototype behaviour it touches, states the alternative, what it costs and buys, and what this pass planned on.

**Challenge 1 · "Model choice is configuration" is kept, and Fable 5.1 is out.** *Decision touched: models.* Covered Models keep a minor's welfare text for thirty days under automated review with no zero-data-retention path except a temporary exemption (§1.6). The alternative is to accept the thirty days for the co-pilot's benefit; it costs a data class the school cannot defend to a regulator or a parent and it buys capability the other models nearly match. Planned on: exclusion by a schema flag and a refusal test (R2), with a re-evaluation when Enterprise Frontier Safeguards are documented (open decision 3).

**Challenge 2 · The escalation the prototype shows is the one thing this pass will not port.** *Invariant 5, and the frozen demo.* The modal's "what will be sent" puts the student's name, year, the headline and the file in an email. The alternative, an email that says only that a referral exists, costs the CPO one sign-in; it buys an inbox that can be forwarded, synced to a phone, or read over a shoulder without exposing a child. Planned on the reference-only email (§5.3). The demo stays frozen; the demo script must not read the old modal as a promise.

**Challenge 3 · Consent is the wrong basis for the core processing, and the school must say so.** *An assumption behind the prototype ("Contact consent · On file").* Treating a school's statutory guidance and safeguarding work as consent-based would let a withdrawal blind a counselor to a child who needs them. The alternative is what this pass recommends (§1.5): the school's contractual and legal-obligation bases, with ADEK Records 1.5.1's parental permission satisfied through the school's enrolment terms, and consent reserved for optional features. It costs the school a documented attestation before any real import (a gate this pass adds); it buys a basis that survives an angry parent. Counsel decides (question C1).

**Challenge 4 · Student sign-in depends on a school-side configuration nobody at CAROS controls.** *Decision touched: identity.* Google blocks under-18 users from unconfigured apps and puts parental consent on the school. The alternative, CAROS-issued student credentials, would cost a password system for 380 minors and the loss of the school's own MFA and deprovisioning. Planned on Google, with the configuration as an onboarding gate and a written checklist (§2.1).

**Challenge 5 · Mentor sessions are not recorded, whatever the copy says.** *Prototype behaviour changed.* A recording of a minor is a data class with retention, access and consent questions this pass cannot settle, and a feature CAROS does not have. Planned on schedule-and-messages only; since decision C6, v1 sessions are in person on school premises, and video on the school's platform waits for ADEK 6.4 consent (§2.3; open decision 9).

**Challenge 6 · The pilot should start with the AI off.** *Decision touched: AI features in v1.* Everything that makes CAROS CAROS runs without a model (§6.1). **Challenge 10 · The twelve-character reason becomes a form.** *Pass 1 5.5.* Planned on the structured referral with forty characters of observation (§5.3); the numbers are ACS's (question 60).

**Challenge 11 · The engagement domain has conditions, not a ban.** *CONTEXT §11.8.* Seven conditions (§6.6), the strictest of which removes time of day from the data the engine can see at the schema level. If ACS declines, the domain stays off and the banner says so.

**Challenge 12 · No ADEK residency rule was found, but an ADEK consent rule was missed.** *Decision touched: data residency.* The decision stands on the PDPL's transfer articles, the ICT Health Law and the school's expectation; no ADEK policy requires in-country hosting.
